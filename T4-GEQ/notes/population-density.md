## Density and distribution

- **Population density** is how many people there are per unit of area. It is a *number*.
- **Population distribution** is where the people are. It is a *pattern*: even or uneven, clustered, linear along coasts or rivers, or dispersed.
- A question asking you to *describe the distribution* wants locations and the pattern between them, not a single density figure.
- *Densely populated* (many people per km²) and *sparsely populated* (few) are the describing words.

$$\text{population density} = \frac{\text{total population}}{\text{area in km}^2}$$

**Worked example.** A region with 2400 people in 300 km²: $2400 \div 300 = 8$ people/km².

Density is an **average**: a region's figure hides empty mountains and crowded cities inside it. This is the weakness of choropleth maps.

## The global distribution

- The distribution is very uneven. About 90% of people live in the Northern Hemisphere, and most live within about 100–150 km of the sea and on lowlands.
- **Four great clusters**: East Asia (eastern China, Japan, Korea); South Asia (the Ganges valley, Bangladesh); Europe; and north-east USA.
- Smaller clusters: Java, the Nile valley and delta, West Africa's coast, south-east Brazil.
- **Empty areas** are deserts (the Sahara, central Australia), cold lands (northern Canada, Siberia, Antarctica), high mountains (the Himalayas, the Andes) and dense rainforest (the Amazon basin).

## Factors affecting density and distribution

| Factor | Encourages high density | Encourages low density |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical: relief** | flat lowland, gentle slopes, river valleys and deltas | steep mountains, high altitude |
| **Physical: climate** | moderate temperatures, reliable rainfall, a long growing season | arid; very cold; very wet and hot; extreme seasons |
| **Physical: soil** | deep, fertile alluvial or volcanic soil | thin, stony, leached, waterlogged or frozen soil |
| **Physical: water** | a reliable supply, a navigable river, a coastline | no surface water |
| **Physical: vegetation and hazards** | open land; few hazards | dense forest; frequent floods, drought or eruptions |
| **Physical: resources** | minerals, fuel, timber, fishing grounds | no exploitable resource |
| **Economic** | jobs in industry and services, a port, trade routes, investment, good transport | remote, few jobs, poor transport, high costs |
| **Social** | services (schools, hospitals, entertainment); family and community already there; historical settlement | isolation, few services, a harsh life |
| **Political** | stability, government investment, planned new towns, capital-city functions | conflict, insecurity, restricted settlement (national parks, military zones) |

Physical factors explain the *broad* pattern. Human factors increasingly explain the *detail*, because technology lets people live in difficult places (Las Vegas, Dubai) and modern jobs cluster in cities.

## High-density examples

| Area | Density | Reasons |
|---|---|---|
| **Bangladesh and the Ganges–Brahmaputra delta** (South Asia) | over 1100 people per km², one of the highest for any large country | flat, fertile alluvial soil renewed by floods; abundant water; monsoon rain lets rice be grown for several harvests a year; a long history of intensive farming; high natural increase |
| **Auckland** (New Zealand) | about a third of the country's population on a narrow isthmus | two sheltered harbours; a mild climate; flat to rolling volcanic land; New Zealand's main port and international airport; jobs, universities and services; the main destination for immigrants |
| *Other examples* | Java; the Nile valley (about 95% of Egyptians live on about 5% of the land); the Netherlands; Singapore | fertile river or volcanic land; trade and industry |

## Low-density examples

| Area | Density | Reasons |
|---|---|---|
| **The Sahara** (North Africa) | under 1 person per km² over most of it | under 250 mm of rain a year, extreme heat, no surface water, sandy and rocky soils, few jobs; people live only at oases and along the Nile |
| **New Zealand's West Coast and the Southern Alps** | only a few people per km² | steep mountains, very high rainfall, thin soils, isolation behind the Alps, few jobs since gold and coal mining declined |
| *Other examples* | the Amazon rainforest; northern Canada and Siberia; Antarctica; the Himalayas; central Australia | dense forest and disease; permafrost and a short summer; ice; altitude; aridity |

## New Zealand's distribution

| Pattern | Reason |
|---|---|
| About three-quarters of people live in the **North Island** | a warmer climate, more lowland, the largest cities, and the gold rushes long over in the south |
| About a third live in **Auckland** | port, airport, jobs, immigration, two harbours |
| Most people live on coastal lowlands and plains (Canterbury Plains, Waikato, Manawatū) | flat fertile land, ports, transport routes |
| The centre of the South Island and the West Coast are sparsely populated | mountains, rainfall, isolation, national parks |
| The population has drifted north for decades | jobs and growth concentrated in Auckland, Waikato and the Bay of Plenty |

## Describing a distribution from a map

1. Give the **general pattern** first: uneven, concentrated in the south-east, along the coast, along river valleys.
2. Give the **anomalies**, the exceptions to the pattern.
3. **Anchor** both with named places, compass directions, figures from the key and grid references.
4. If asked to explain, link each pattern to a factor: "dense along the valley floor *because* it is flat, has fertile alluvium and the main road."

Sample: "Population is densest in the north-west, over 500 per km² around the capital, and along the coastal plain, but sparse (under 10 per km²) in the mountainous interior and the arid south."

## Common exam errors

- Answering a *distribution* question with only a density figure.
- Explaining with a single factor; the mark scheme wants several, each developed.
- Saying "nice climate". Say *what* about the climate helps: reliable rainfall for crops, or no frost.
- Forgetting the human factors: jobs, services, government.
