# How the topics overlap — and where to keep them apart

Geography's themes share ideas, which is useful in essays and dangerous in short answers. This page shows which section each topic belongs to, the links that earn credit in an essay, the look-alike terms that lose marks, and which case study to use for which question. Every model named here has a row in the [models index](../notes/models-index.md).

## (b) Legitimate essay links

These links show understanding across themes and lift an essay. Use one or two; keep the essay on its own theme.

| Link | Chain | Use it in |
|---|---|---|
| Farm mechanisation → urbanisation | mechanisation (Clark–Fisher: primary jobs fall) → rural unemployment pushes people out → rural–urban migration → the steep middle of the urbanisation curve | B: history of urbanisation; C: economic transition |
| DTM stage ↔ development level | as income, education and urban living rise, birth rates fall: The Gambia late stage 2, India 3, NZ 4, Japan 5 | B: population change; C: why indicators move together |
| Remittances and the informal sector | migrants' remittances fund school fees and housing at home; slum dwellers work informally; both are invisible or partial in GDP | B: impacts of migration, slums; C: limits of GDP |
| Urbanisation → flashy hydrographs | roofs and roads → less infiltration, more runoff, drains → short lag, high peak | A: flooding causes (Auckland, 27 January 2023) |
| Slums on hazardous land → landslides | migrants can only build on steep or flood-prone land → heavy rain → landslides and floods | B: squatter-settlement problems (Rio, 2010); A: mass movement |
| Ageing population → migration policy | low fertility → shrinking workforce → governments admit younger migrants | B: population policy (Germany, 2015–16; Japan's 2019 work visa) |
| Sea-level rise → climate refugees | warming → thermal expansion and ice melt → flooding and salt water on atolls → planned migration | C: global warming (Kiribati, Pacific Access Category); B: forced migration |
| NZ regional inequality ↔ migration to Auckland | the periphery loses jobs and services → young people move to Auckland → the periphery ages and declines further | C: regional inequality; B: internal migration, Auckland's growth |
| Food shortage ↔ over-population | population outgrows food supply and technology → malnutrition, migration (Sahel) | C: food shortages; B: population–resource balance |
| Deforestation → floods and global warming | fewer trees → more runoff and erosion; carbon released | A: flooding, ecosystems; C: carbon cycle |
| Multiplier effect ↔ urban growth | factories and tourism create jobs → migrants arrive → the city grows | C: manufacturing and tourism; B: pull factors |

## (c) Keep distinct

These pairs look alike and are marked as different things.

| Terms | The difference |
|---|---|
| **Urbanisation** vs **urban growth** vs **urban sprawl** vs **over-urbanisation** | rising *proportion* urban vs more urban *people or area* vs uncontrolled low-density *spread onto rural land* vs urban population growing *faster than jobs, housing and services* |
| **Suburbanisation** vs **counter-urbanisation** vs **re-urbanisation** vs **gentrification** | moving to the *edge of the same city* vs leaving the city for *small towns and villages* vs moving *back into the inner city* vs *richer people renovating* run-down inner housing and displacing poorer residents (a process within re-urbanisation, not the same) |
| **Rural–urban migration** vs **international migration** | within a country, countryside to city (B, urbanisation) vs across a border (B, migration: Samoa, Syria) |
| **DTM** vs **Rostow** vs **Clark–Fisher** vs **urbanisation curve** vs **Butler** | different axes and different stage numbers: DTM = birth/death rates over time (5 stages); Rostow = economic growth (5 stages); Clark–Fisher = % employed per sector (3 stages); urbanisation curve = % urban (3 phases); Butler = tourist numbers at one resort (6 stages). Stage 2 of one is not stage 2 of another |
| **Burgess / Hoyt** vs **Griffin–Ford** | HIC: wealth *rises* outwards; LIC/Latin America: wealth *falls* outwards, with squatters on the edge |
| **Bid-rent** vs the zone models | bid-rent *explains* why land uses sort by distance; it does not draw zones |
| **Core–frame** vs the city models | core–frame describes *only the inside of the CBD* |
| **HIC** vs **LIC** urban problems and solutions | HIC: sprawl, congestion, inner-city decay → green belts, intensification, regeneration. LIC: squatter settlements, no services, informal work → site-and-service, in-situ upgrading, rural development |
| Birth rate, life expectancy, infant mortality in **B** vs **C** | B: *explain population change* (why it rises or falls); C: *indicators of development* (what they reveal or hide) |
| **Dependency ratio** vs **dependency theory** | dependants per 100 people of working age (B) vs Frank's model of the periphery depending on the core (C) |
| **Core–periphery** vs **core–frame** | rich and poor countries or regions (C) vs the inside of a CBD (B) |
| **Over-population** vs **high density** | too many people *for the resources and technology* vs many people per km². Singapore is dense, not over-populated |
| **River defences** vs **coastal defences** | stopbanks, dams, channelisation, floodplain zoning vs sea walls, groynes, rock armour, gabions, beach nourishment. A river method in a coastal answer is not credited |
| **Weathering** vs **erosion** | breakdown *in place* vs wearing away *and removal* by a moving agent |
| **Corrosion** vs **carbonation** | erosion by acidic *river or sea water* dissolving rock vs *weathering* of limestone by rainwater carbonic acid |
| **Magnitude** vs **intensity** | energy released (Richter, moment magnitude) vs effects felt (Mercalli) |
| **Weather** vs **climate** | conditions now vs the 30-year average |
| **Global warming** vs **the greenhouse effect** | the rise in temperature vs the natural trapping of heat (the *enhanced* effect causes warming) |
| **Mitigation** vs **adaptation** | cutting the cause (emissions) vs living with the effects |
| **Constructive** vs **destructive** (waves and plate boundaries) | waves: build vs erode beaches; boundaries: plates apart vs together |
| **Erosion** vs **transport** "solution" | the same word for dissolving rock (erosion) and carrying dissolved load (transport) — say which |
