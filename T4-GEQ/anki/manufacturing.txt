Pre-Q Basic	Manufacturing (secondary industry)?	Turning raw materials or components into finished products	
Pre-Q Basic	Heavy industry?	Bulky raw materials and energy make large products (steel)	
Pre-Q Basic	Light industry?	Small components make small, light products (electronics)	
Pre-Q Basic	Processing industry?	Changes a raw material (dairy factory, sawmill)	
Pre-Q Basic	Assembly industry?	Puts together components made elsewhere (car plant)	
Pre-Q Basic	High-technology industry?	Research-intensive products (computers, pharmaceuticals)	
Pre-Q Basic	Footloose industry?	Not tied to raw materials or power; free to locate widely	think electricity-powered high-tech
Pre-Q Basic	Agglomeration?	Related firms clustering to share suppliers, labour and ideas	think Silicon Valley
Pre-Q Basic	Industrial inertia?	Industry stays after its original location reasons have gone	not the same as agglomeration
Pre-Q Basic	Industrial estate?	Planned factory area with shared roads and services	
Pre-Q Basic	Science park?	Landscaped site of high-tech research firms, often near a university	
Pre-Q Basic	Special economic zone?	Tax breaks and fewer rules to attract foreign manufacturers (Shenzhen)	
Pre-Q Basic	Just-in-time?	Components delivered exactly when needed; little stock held	think stock levels
Pre-Q Basic	Thermal pollution?	Warm cooling water discharged into rivers	
Pre-Q Basic	Company town?	Town dependent on one main employer	
Pre-Q Basic	Weight-losing industry?	Inputs weigh more than the product, so it locates near raw materials	
Pre-Q Basic	Acid rain: which gases cause it?	Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides	
Pre-Q Basic	Why did early industry locate on coalfields?	Coal was bulky and costly to move	think transport cost
Pre-Q Basic	Why is modern industry more footloose?	Electricity, containers and imported materials; markets and labour matter more	
Pre-Q Basic	Aluminium smelter: key location factor?	Cheap electricity (e.g. hydro)	
Pre-Q Basic	When does a factory locate near raw materials?	When inputs are bulky, weight-losing or perishable	think weight loss in processing
Pre-Q Basic	When does a factory locate near its market?	When the product gains weight, or is fragile or perishable	opposite of weight-losing
