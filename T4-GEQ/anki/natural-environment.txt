#deck:Geography::2 The natural environment
What drives plate movement?	Convection currents in the mantle, powered by heat from radioactive decay
Constructive plate boundary	Plates pull apart and magma rises: mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys, shield volcanoes, shallow earthquakes
Destructive plate boundary	Denser oceanic plate subducts under continental: trench, fold mountains, composite volcanoes, deep earthquakes
Collision plate boundary	Two continental plates meet and neither subducts: high fold mountains, strong earthquakes, no volcanoes
Conservative plate boundary	Plates slide past each other: fault line, shallow earthquakes, no volcanoes
Difference between the focus and the epicentre	The focus is where the rock ruptures underground; the epicentre is the point on the surface directly above it
Volcano formed by runny low-silica basaltic magma	Shield volcano
Volcano formed by sticky andesitic magma	Composite cone
Which is more predictable, earthquakes or eruptions?	Eruptions, from gas emissions, ground deformation and tremors; earthquakes have no reliable short-term prediction
Four ways to reduce earthquake impact	Monitoring, aseismic building design, drills and preparation, land-use planning away from faults
Difference between weathering and erosion	Weathering breaks rock down in place; erosion removes the loosened material
Freeze-thaw weathering	Water in a crack freezes and expands about 9 per cent, prising the rock apart over repeated cycles
Carbonation	Rainwater with dissolved CO2 forms carbonic acid, which dissolves calcium carbonate in limestone
Hydrolysis	Acidic water reacts with feldspar in granite, rotting it to clay
Exfoliation	Daily heating and cooling makes the outer skin of rock expand and contract until it peels off
Plucking	Meltwater refreezes onto rock at the base of a glacier and the moving ice tears blocks away
Formation of a cirque	Snow collects in a hollow, freeze-thaw steepens the back wall, rotational slip and abrasion deepen the floor
Definition of an arête	A knife-edge ridge where two cirques erode back-to-back
Definition of a hanging valley	A tributary valley left above the main glacial trough because its smaller glacier eroded less deeply
Definition of a drainage basin	The area drained by one river and its tributaries
Definition of a watershed	The ridge of high ground separating one drainage basin from the next
Difference between infiltration and percolation	Infiltration is water soaking into the soil; percolation is water passing from soil into rock
Difference between throughflow and surface runoff	Throughflow moves laterally through the soil; surface runoff flows over the ground when rain exceeds infiltration
Definition of baseflow	Slow groundwater seepage that sustains a river between storms
Formula for river discharge	\(Q = \text{cross-sectional area} \times \text{mean velocity}\), in m³/s
Definition of lag time	The gap between peak rainfall and peak discharge
Five factors giving a short lag time and high peak	Intense rain, steep slopes, impermeable rock, saturated ground, urban land use
Why urbanisation worsens floods in one line	Impermeable surfaces cut infiltration, drains speed runoff, lag time shortens and peak discharge rises
Hydraulic action	The force of moving water pushing into cracks and compressing trapped air
Abrasion in a river	The load scraping and grinding against the bed and banks
Attrition	Load particles colliding with each other, becoming smaller and rounder downstream
Four transport processes in a river	Solution, suspension, saltation, traction
What the Hjulström curve shows	The velocity needed to pick up, carry and drop particles of each size
Bradshaw model: what increases downstream	Discharge, width, depth, velocity, load quantity
Bradshaw model: what decreases downstream	Gradient, bed roughness, load particle size
Formation of a waterfall	Hard rock over soft; the soft rock is undercut, the hard cap collapses, and the fall retreats upstream leaving a gorge
Formation of an ox-bow lake	A meander neck narrows, a flood cuts through, and deposition seals off the old loop
Formation of a levée	In a flood the coarsest sediment is dropped first at the channel edge, building a raised bank
Difference between hard and soft flood management	Hard engineering controls the river with structures; soft approaches work with natural processes and land use
Difference between weather and climate	Weather is the atmosphere over hours or days; climate is the average weather over 30 years or more
Instrument for temperature	Maximum-minimum thermometer in a Stevenson screen
Instrument for humidity	Wet-and-dry bulb thermometer (hygrometer)
Siting of a rain gauge	Sunk into short grass, rim above ground, away from buildings and trees
Instrument for wind speed	Anemometer, 10 m above open ground
How wind direction is named	By the direction the wind comes from
Instrument and unit for air pressure	Barometer, millibars
Four features of a Stevenson screen	White paint, louvred sides, legs about 1.25 m above grass, door facing the pole
Main steady-rain cloud	Nimbostratus
Cloud bringing heavy showers, thunder and hail	Cumulonimbus
Formation of rain in one line	Air rises, cools to its dew point, vapour condenses on nuclei, droplets coalesce until heavy enough to fall
Three types of rainfall	Convectional, relief (orographic), frontal
Definition of a rain shadow	The dry leeward side of a mountain range after air has lost its moisture rising over the windward side
Weather in a depression	Rising air, cloud, rain and fronts
Weather in an anticyclone	Sinking air, settled clear weather, frost or fog in winter
Six factors affecting climate	Latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, ocean currents, prevailing winds, relief
Why rainforest soils are poor	Rapid decomposition puts nutrients into the biomass, and heavy rain leaches what remains
Constructive and destructive waves	Constructive: strong swash builds beaches; destructive: strong backwash removes them
Longshore drift	Swash carries sediment obliquely up the beach, backwash pulls it straight down, moving it along the coast
Erosion sequence of a headland	Crack, cave, arch, stack, stump
Formation of a spit	Longshore drift carries sediment past a change in coastline direction, where it builds out into open water
