#deck:Geography::1 Population and settlement
Definition of birth rate	The number of live births per 1000 people per year
Definition of death rate	The number of deaths per 1000 people per year
How to calculate a birth rate from raw numbers	Births ÷ total population × 1000
Definition of natural increase	Birth rate minus death rate; a natural decrease when deaths exceed births
Turn a natural increase per 1000 into a percentage	Divide by 10 — 19 per 1000 is 1.9% a year
Definition of net migration	Immigrants minus emigrants for an area over a given period
Formula for total population change	(Births − deaths) ± net migration
Definition of fertility rate	The average number of children a woman has over her lifetime
Definition of infant mortality rate	Deaths of children under one year old per 1000 live births
Replacement-level fertility	About 2.1 children per woman
Definition of population density	The average number of people per km² — population ÷ area in km²
Density versus distribution	Density is a number (people per km²); distribution is the pattern of where people live
Named area of high population density, with reasons	Bangladesh and the Ganges delta — over 1100 per km²: flat fertile alluvium, plentiful water, monsoon rice with several harvests a year
Named area of low population density, with reasons	The Sahara — under 1 per km²: under 250 mm of rain, extreme heat, no surface water, poor soils
Approximate dates of world population reaching 1, 4 and 8 billion	About 1800; 1974; November 2022
Why world population exploded after about 1750	Agricultural and industrial revolutions raised food supply; clean water, sanitation and vaccination cut the death rate while birth rates stayed high
DTM stage 2, with an example	High birth rate, rapidly falling death rate, very high natural increase — Niger, Chad
DTM stage 3, with an example	Birth rate falling fast, death rate low; population still rising, more slowly — India, Kenya
DTM stage 4, with an example	Low birth and death rates, natural increase near zero — New Zealand, UK
DTM stage 5, with an example	Birth rate below death rate, natural decrease, ageing — Japan, Italy
Four reasons birth rates fall as a country develops	Women educated and working, contraception available, low infant mortality, children expensive and no longer needed as labour
How HIV/AIDS changed southern Africa's population	Raised adult death rates, cut life expectancy by over a decade, lowered births and left many orphans
Credited reasons for natural decrease in HICs such as Japan	Small families, contraception, educated women with careers, children expensive, later marriage, rising death rate as the population ages
Pyramid with a wide base and concave sides	High birth and death rates — youthful, DTM stage 2
Pyramid with a narrow base and wide top	Low birth rate and long life expectancy — ageing, DTM stage 5
Dependency ratio formula	(0–14 + 65 and over) ÷ (15–64) × 100
Dependency ratio: 3.2 m young, 1.1 m elderly, 5.5 m aged 15–64	(3.2 + 1.1) ÷ 5.5 × 100 = 78 dependants per 100 of working age
Definition of over-population	Too many people for the resources and technology available, so living standards fall
Three problems of a youthful population	Pressure on schools and child health, too few jobs, food and water stress (also housing and squatter settlements)
Three problems of an ageing population	Pension and healthcare costs, labour shortages, fewer taxpayers per pensioner
Anti-natalist versus pro-natalist policy	Anti-natalist aims to reduce the birth rate; pro-natalist aims to raise it
China's one-child policy: dates and what replaced it	1979/80 to 2015; two children from 2016; three from 2021
Two side effects of China's one-child policy	A heavily male sex ratio; a rapidly ageing population and shrinking workforce (4-2-1 families)
Japan's ageing population in figures	About 29% aged 65 or over, fertility about 1.2, population falling from a peak of about 128 million
Niger as a youthful country	Fertility about 6–7, the world's highest; about half the population under 15; growth over 3% a year
Why Bangladesh's fertility fell from over 6 to about 2	Door-to-door family planning, free contraception, girls' education and women's garment-industry jobs
Definition of a refugee	A person who has fled their own country because of a well-founded fear of persecution, conflict or disaster
Voluntary versus forced migration	Voluntary: the migrant chooses, usually for work or family; forced: no real choice — conflict, persecution, disaster
Three parts of Lee's push–pull model	Push factors at the origin, pull factors at the destination, intervening obstacles in between
Definition of brain drain	The loss of skilled and educated workers from an area through emigration
Definition of remittance	Money sent home by a migrant to family in the source area
Problems for a country when many people emigrate	Lost workforce and skills, fewer taxpayers, higher dependency ratio, gender imbalance, family break-up, less investment
Samoa–New Zealand flow: the scheme letting Samoans settle in NZ	The Samoan Quota — up to 1100 Samoan citizens a year, chosen by ballot
Samoa–New Zealand flow: one impact on each country	Samoa gains remittances but loses young workers; New Zealand gains labour and a large Pacific community
Syrian refugee flow: cause, start and main hosts	Civil war from 2011; Turkey, Lebanon, Jordan, and Germany in Europe
Definition of site	The actual land on which a settlement is built
Definition of situation	A settlement's position relative to the surrounding region, routes and resources
Dispersed, nucleated and linear settlement	Isolated buildings; buildings clustered round a centre; a line along a road, river or valley
Definition of range (of a service)	The maximum distance people will travel to use a service
Definition of threshold population	The minimum number of customers needed for a service to survive
Definition of sphere of influence	The area served by a settlement or a particular service
Why CBD shops and services have a large sphere of influence	High-order, specialist goods bought infrequently, wide choice, not available near home, focus of transport routes
Top three of NZ's urban hierarchy, with a reason each	Auckland — main port, entry point, largest economy; Wellington — seat of government; Christchurch — dominates the South Island
Definition of urbanisation	An increase in the proportion of a population living in urban areas
Urbanisation versus urban growth	Urbanisation is a rising urban share of the population; urban growth is a rise in urban population or built-up area
When did more than half the world's people live in urban areas?	About 2007 (UN estimate); about 55% now, about two-thirds forecast by 2050
Definition of suburbanisation	The outward growth of housing onto the city edge, with residents commuting back in
Definition of counter-urbanisation	The movement of people out of cities to smaller towns and rural areas
Definition of gentrification	Higher-income residents buying and improving inner-city property, raising prices and displacing poorer residents
Burgess model	Concentric rings round the CBD: zone of transition, low-income, medium-income, high-income commuter zone
Hoyt model	Sectors radiating from the CBD along transport routes; industry and low-income housing follow one corridor
Core–frame model of the CBD	A core of shops, offices and banks; a frame of car parks, stations, warehousing and workshops
Bid-rent theory	Land value falls away from the most accessible point, the CBD; retail outbids offices and industry, and housing takes the cheaper outer land
Latin American city model: where do rich and poor live?	Rich in an élite sector along the commercial spine near the centre; poorest in peripheral squatter settlements
Definition of a squatter settlement	Self-built housing erected without legal title to the land or planning permission
Four characteristics of squatter settlements	Self-built from scrap; steep or flood-prone land; no piped water or sewerage; illegal power connections
Rio de Janeiro: favela population and main upgrading scheme	About 1.4 million people, over a fifth of the city (2010); Favela-Bairro from the mid-1990s
Where Rio's rural migrants come from, and why they leave	Brazil's poor north-east — drought, land held in large estates, mechanisation, poverty
Auckland: three infrastructure responses	Unitary Plan with a Rural Urban Boundary (2016); Waterview tunnels completing the Western Ring Route (2017); the City Rail Link
