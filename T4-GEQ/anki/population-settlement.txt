#deck:Geography::1 Population and settlement
Definition of population density	The average number of people per square kilometre
Formula for population density	\(\text{total population} \div \text{area in km}^2\)
Difference between density and distribution	Density is a number of people per unit area; distribution is the pattern of where they are
Four physical factors encouraging high population density	Flat lowland, deep fertile soil, moderate climate with reliable rainfall, a reliable water supply
Four human factors encouraging high population density	Jobs and industry, ports and trade routes, services, political stability
Formula for population change	\((\text{births} - \text{deaths}) \pm \text{net migration}\)
Definition of birth rate	Live births per 1000 people per year
Definition of death rate	Deaths per 1000 people per year
Formula for a rate per 1000	\(\frac{\text{events}}{\text{population}} \times 1000\)
Definition of natural increase	Birth rate minus death rate
Convert a natural increase per 1000 into a percentage	Divide by 10, so 19 per 1000 is 1.9 per cent
Difference between birth rate and fertility rate	Birth rate is births per 1000 people per year; fertility rate is the average number of children per woman over her lifetime
Four factors that keep birth rates high	Children needed as farm labour, high infant mortality, no pensions, little education or employment for women
Four factors that lower birth rates	Educated women marrying and working later, cheap contraception, state pensions, low infant mortality
Three factors that lower death rates	Clean water and sewerage, vaccination and medical care, peace and stable government
DTM stage 1	High fluctuating: high birth and death rates, natural increase near zero, low stable population
DTM stage 2	Early expanding: high birth rate, rapidly falling death rate, very high natural increase
DTM stage 3	Late expanding: rapidly falling birth rate, low death rate, population still rising more slowly
DTM stage 4	Low fluctuating: low birth and death rates, natural increase near zero, high stable population
DTM stage 5	Declining: very low birth rate, death rate slightly above it, population slowly falling
Why the death rate falls before the birth rate in the DTM	Clean water and medicine cut deaths quickly, while family-size expectations change slowly
Three limitations of the DTM	Based on industrialised countries, ignores migration, stage timing varies between countries
Order for reading a population pyramid	Shape, base, apex, sides, then one figure quoted with its unit
What a wide pyramid base shows	A high birth rate and a youthful population
What a notch in one cohort of a pyramid shows	A past war, epidemic or sharp dip in births
What a male bulge in the working ages shows	In-migration of male labour
Formula for the dependency ratio	\(\frac{(0\text{–}14) + (65+)}{15\text{–}64} \times 100\)
Definition of over-population	Too many people for the resources and technology available, so living standards fall
Definition of under-population	Too few people to use the resources fully, so potential output is lost
Definition of optimum population	The number giving the highest standard of living with the resources available
Three pressures from a youthful population	Schools, child healthcare, housing and future jobs
Three pressures from an ageing population	Pensions, hospitals, residential care
Definition of anti-natalist policy	A policy to reduce the birth rate, such as family planning and educating girls
Definition of pro-natalist policy	A policy to raise the birth rate, such as child payments, parental leave and subsidised childcare
Definition of migration	A permanent or semi-permanent change of residence
Difference between emigration and immigration	Emigration is leaving a country; immigration is entering one
Definition of net migration	Immigration minus emigration
Difference between voluntary and forced migration	Voluntary migrants choose to move; forced migrants have no real choice because of war, persecution or disaster
Definition of a refugee	A person who has fled their country because of a well-founded fear of persecution or conflict
Definition of an internally displaced person	Someone forced to move but still inside their own country
Three parts of Lee's push-pull model	Push factors at the origin, pull factors at the destination, intervening obstacles between them
Four intervening obstacles to migration	Distance, cost, border controls, language
Two positive impacts of emigration on the source area	Remittances sent home, less pressure on jobs, land and services
Two negative impacts of emigration on the source area	Brain drain of young skilled workers, an ageing and unbalanced population structure
Rural-to-urban migration in an LIC in one line	Rural jobs lost, urban opportunity perceived, young adults move, housing lags, informal settlement grows
Four settlement patterns	Dispersed, nucleated, linear, clustered
Difference between site and situation	Site is the land a settlement is built on; situation is its position relative to the surrounding region
Definition of a bridging point site	The lowest or narrowest crossing point of a river
Difference between a wet point and a dry point site	A wet point has a reliable spring in a dry area; a dry point is raised above flood level
Definition of range in the settlement hierarchy	The furthest distance a person will travel to use a service
Definition of threshold population	The minimum number of customers a service needs to survive
How settlements change moving up the hierarchy	Fewer, larger and further apart, with larger spheres of influence and more high-order services
Burgess model in one line	Concentric rings of decreasing age and density outwards from the CBD
Hoyt model in one line	Sectors radiating along transport routes from the CBD
Bid-rent theory in one line	Land value falls with distance from the CBD, so retail outbids industry and industry outbids housing at the centre
Difference between urbanisation and urban growth	Urbanisation is a rising proportion living in urban areas; urban growth is a rise in urban population or area
Definition of counter-urbanisation	Movement of people from cities to smaller towns and rural areas
Definition of gentrification	Higher-income residents buying and improving inner-city housing, raising prices
Five problems of rapid urban growth	Squatter settlements, poor water and sanitation, congestion, pollution, urban sprawl
