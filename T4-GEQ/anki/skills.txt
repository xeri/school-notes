#deck:Geography::4 Geographical skills
Five things to check before answering a map question	Title and date, scale, north arrow, key, grid interval
Where must map-question evidence come from?	The map only, not general knowledge
Order of a grid reference	Eastings before northings: along the corridor, then up the stairs
What a four-figure grid reference identifies	A whole 1 km grid square
How to read a four-figure reference	The easting line to the left of the square, then the northing line below it
What a six-figure grid reference identifies	A point inside a grid square, to the nearest tenth of each side
Formula for ground distance	\(\text{map distance} \times \text{scale factor}\)
1 cm on a 1:50 000 map represents	500 m
1 cm on a 1:25 000 map represents	250 m
Ground distance of 7.4 cm on a 1:50 000 map	\(7.4 \times 500 = 3700\) m, or 3.7 km
How to measure a curved route	Mark a paper edge along each straight section, pivoting at each bend, then read it against the linear scale
How to estimate area from a grid	Count whole squares, then add part-squares as halves
Order of the 16 compass points from N to E	N, NNE, NE, ENE, E
Rule for stating direction	Always from the first place named to the second
Definition of a bearing	The clockwise angle from grid north, written as three digits
Bearings of east, south and west	090°, 180°, 270°
What closely spaced contours show	A steep slope
What contours in a V pointing upslope show	A valley, with the V pointing upstream
What concentric closed contours show	A hill or summit
Formula for gradient	\(\frac{\text{vertical rise}}{\text{horizontal distance}}\), both in metres
Gradient of a 180 m rise over 1500 m	\(180/1500 = 0.12\), or 1 in 8.3, or 12 per cent
Most common gradient error	Forgetting to convert map centimetres into ground metres first
How to join the points of a cross-section	With a smooth curve, never ruled straight segments
Three drainage patterns	Dendritic, radial, trellis
How to describe a distribution from a map	General pattern, then exceptions, anchored with grid references or named places and a direction
How to divide an oblique photograph	Foreground, middle distance and background; left, centre and right
Difference between a label and an annotation	A label names a feature; an annotation explains what it is doing
Five features of a sketch map	Frame, title, north arrow, scale or not-to-scale note, key
Graph for proportions of one whole	Pie graph
Degrees per 1 per cent on a pie graph	3.6°
Graph for a relationship between two variables	Scatter graph
What a scatter graph cannot show	Cause: correlation is not causation
Graph for three components totalling 100 per cent	Triangular graph
Map for rates or densities by area	Choropleth map
Weakness of a choropleth map	It hides variation inside each area
What an isoline joins	Points of equal value
What line width means on a flow-line map	Quantity moving between places
How to read a climate graph	Quote maximum and minimum temperature, annual range and total rainfall
What the longest arm of a wind rose shows	The prevailing wind direction
Formula for percentage	\(\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} \times 100\)
Formula for percentage change	\(\frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\)
Formula for the mean	\(\frac{\sum x}{n}\)
Definition of the median	The middle value when the data are ranked
Definition of the mode	The most frequently occurring value
Formula for the range	\(\text{maximum} - \text{minimum}\)
Formula for the interquartile range	\(Q_3 - Q_1\)
Advantage of the interquartile range over the range	It describes the middle half of the data, so one extreme value does not distort it
Births per 1000 for 1260 births in 45000 people	\(1260 \div 45\,000 \times 1000 = 28\)
Three sampling methods	Random, systematic, stratified
Difference between primary and secondary data	Primary data is collected yourself; secondary data comes from existing sources
Five river fieldwork measurements	Width, depth, velocity, bedload size and roundness, gradient
Three possible fieldwork conclusions about a hypothesis	Supported, partly supported, not supported
Describe versus explain	Describe says what or where with no reasons; explain gives causes and the mechanism
Test of whether a sentence explains	It contains because, so, this causes or as a result
What compare demands	Comparative statements such as whereas or more than, not two separate descriptions
What justify demands	Why you chose that option and why you rejected the others
Three steps for using data as evidence	State the pattern, quote the figure with unit and date, say what it shows
What SDEEL stands for	Statement, describe, explain, example, link
What a strong evaluative conclusion does	Takes a position and names the condition it depends on
