#deck:Geography::4 Geographical skills
Final exam format	One 3-hour paper, 100 marks: 75% core short-answer questions, 25% paragraph/essay — skills tested inside the core
Equipment needed for the map question	Pencil, rubber, ruler, protractor, calculator and a sheet of plain paper
Scales of exam topographic maps	1:25 000 or 1:50 000, always with a full key
Golden rule for map-extract answers	Use map evidence only — not general knowledge
Order of the numbers in a grid reference	Eastings (along the corridor) before northings (up the stairs)
What a four-figure grid reference identifies	A whole grid square: the easting line on its left, then the northing line below it
How to give a six-figure grid reference	Add tenths east of the easting and north of the northing — the first tenth is 0, the last is 9
Six-figure reference: 3 tenths east of line 46 and 4 tenths north of line 65	463654 (in square 4665)
On a 1:50 000 map, 1 cm represents	500 m — so 2 cm is 1 km
On a 1:25 000 map, 1 cm represents	250 m — so 4 cm is 1 km
Ground distance of 7.4 cm on a 1:50 000 map	7.4 × 500 m = 3700 m = 3.7 km
How to measure a curved route on a map	Mark the paper edge, pivot it at each bend along straight sections, then read the total against the linear scale
How to estimate an area on a map	Count whole grid squares, add part squares as halves; each 1 km square is 1 km²
The 16 compass points clockwise from north	N, NNE, NE, ENE, E, ESE, SE, SSE, S, SSW, SW, WSW, W, WNW, NW, NNW
Definition of a bearing	The clockwise angle from grid north, given as three digits (000°–359°)
How to measure a bearing from A to B	Protractor centre on A, 0° along grid north, read clockwise to the line A–B
"Direction of A from B": where do you stand?	At B, facing A
Contours close together versus far apart	Close: steep slope; far apart: gentle slope or flat land
Contours forming a V pointing uphill	A valley — the V points upstream
Definition of contour interval	The vertical difference in height between adjacent contours
Gradient formula	Vertical rise ÷ horizontal distance, both in the same units (often written 1 in x)
Gradient: 180 m rise over 3 cm on a 1:50 000 map	180 ÷ 1500 = 0.12 = 1 in 8.3 (12%)
Most common gradient mistake	Not converting map centimetres to ground metres before dividing
Steps for drawing a cross-section	Mark contour crossings on a paper edge, transfer to labelled axes, plot each height, join with a smooth curve
Definition of vertical exaggeration	The factor by which a cross-section's vertical scale is stretched relative to its horizontal scale
Describing a distribution from a map	The general pattern first, then exceptions, anchored with grid references, names, directions and distances
How to describe an oblique photograph	Divide it into foreground, middle distance and background, and left, centre and right
Label versus annotation	A label names a feature; an annotation explains what it is doing
Five things every sketch map needs	Frame, title, north arrow, scale (or "not to scale"), key
Best graph for three components that total 100%	Triangular graph — e.g. employment by sector
Best graph for a relationship between two variables	Scatter graph with a best-fit line
Best graph for parts of a single whole	Pie graph — 1% = 3.6°
Best map for rates or densities by area	Choropleth map
What a wind rose shows	How often (and how strongly) the wind blows from each direction — the longest arm is the prevailing wind
What a kite diagram shows	The abundance of species along a transect
What the width of a flow line shows	The volume moving between two places
Definition of an isoline	A line joining points of equal value — contours and isobars are isolines
Convert a pie sector of 126° to a percentage	126 ÷ 360 × 100 = 35%
Percentage change formula	(New − old) ÷ old × 100
Mean, median and mode	Mean: total ÷ number of values; median: middle value when ranked; mode: most frequent value
Range and interquartile range	Range: maximum − minimum; IQR: upper quartile − lower quartile, the middle half, unaffected by extremes
Mean, median and mode of 4, 7, 7, 9, 13, 15, 17	Mean 10.3 (72 ÷ 7), median 9, mode 7 (range 13)
What a scatter graph can and cannot show	It shows correlation; it does not prove causation
Primary versus secondary data	Primary: collected yourself in the field; secondary: collected by someone else (census, an earlier survey)
Stages of a fieldwork enquiry	Hypothesis, site selection, sampling, data collection, presentation, analysis, conclusion, evaluation
Three sampling methods	Random, systematic, stratified
River fieldwork measurements	Width, depth, velocity (float and stopwatch), bedload size and roundness, gradient — at several sites downstream
Coastal fieldwork measurements	Beach profile with a clinometer, sediment size, longshore drift with floats, sediment height either side of groynes
Describe versus explain	Describe: what it is like or where it is; explain: why it happens — needs "because" and a mechanism
What earns the marks in a "compare" question	Comparative words (whereas, higher than) and paired data — not two separate descriptions
"Suggest"	Apply your knowledge to an unfamiliar situation where you cannot be certain
"Evaluate" or "to what extent"	Evidence on both sides, stated criteria, and a judgement in the conclusion
"Using Fig. 1 only"	Evidence must come from the resource; general knowledge, or naming places without describing the pattern, is not credited
How a two-mark "identify and explain" point is marked	One mark for naming it in correct terminology, one for explaining how it works
SDEEL paragraph structure	Statement, Describe, Explain, Example, Link back to the question
Essay Level 4 (9–10 marks)	Comprehensive, developed place-specific examples, accurate terminology and processes, clear evaluation and conclusion
Essay Level 3 (6–8 marks)	Thorough explanation, terminology throughout, at least one developed example, but less comprehensive or balanced
Maximum essay mark with no examples	6 out of 10
What makes an example "developed"	A place, a date and a specific fact or figure used in the argument — not a tag like "as seen in Waikato"
Time per mark in the final exam	About 1.8 minutes (100 marks in 3 hours); 15–20 minutes per 10-mark essay
