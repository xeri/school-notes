#deck:Geography::3 Economic development and resources
Definition of development	An improvement in the quality of life and standard of living of a population
Difference between growth and development	Growth is a rise in output or income; development is the improvement in people's lives
Four types of development	Economic, social, political, environmental
Definition of GDP per capita	The total value of goods and services produced in a year divided by population
Three limitations of GDP per capita	An average that hides inequality, excludes the informal economy, distorted by exchange rates
Why use GNI per capita at PPP?	It adjusts income for what money actually buys locally
Definition of infant mortality rate	Deaths under one year of age per 1000 live births
Limitation of people per doctor	It says nothing about distribution between city and countryside
Three components of the HDI	Income, education, life expectancy
Three limitations of the HDI	Ignores the environment, political freedom and internal inequality
Formula for percentage change	\(\frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\)
What the Brandt Line was	A 1980 line separating a richer North from a poorer South, now out of date
What SEEP stands for	Social, economic, environmental, political causes of uneven development
The trade trap in one line	Raw material exports at volatile prices give unreliable revenue, so no capital to build processing industry
Rostow's five stages	Traditional society, pre-conditions for take-off, take-off, drive to maturity, age of mass consumption
Frank's dependency theory	The core developed by extracting surplus from the periphery, so underdevelopment is caused by the relationship
Typical pattern of inequality within a country	Core-periphery: investment and jobs concentrate in the largest city and accessible regions
Difference between formal and informal sector	Formal work is registered, taxed and protected; informal work is unregistered, untaxed and insecure
Primary sector	Extracting or growing raw materials: farming, fishing, forestry, mining
Secondary sector	Processing and manufacturing
Tertiary sector	Providing services
Quaternary sector	Knowledge, research and information services
Employment structure of a post-industrial HIC	Small primary, falling secondary, dominant tertiary, growing quaternary
Economic transition in one line	Mechanisation releases farm labour, factories draw it to towns, rising incomes raise demand for services
Graph for employment by sector	Triangular graph, because the three percentages sum to 100
Definition of globalisation	The increasing interconnection of economies, cultures and populations through trade, investment, migration and communication
Four drivers of globalisation	Containerisation and cheap air freight, the internet, trade liberalisation, growth of TNCs
Definition of a TNC	A company with its headquarters in one country and operations in others
Three benefits of a TNC to a host country	Jobs and skills, tax and export earnings, multiplier effect on local suppliers
Three drawbacks of a TNC to a host country	Profits repatriated, lower wages and conditions, the plant can be closed and relocated
Difference between commercial and subsistence farming	Commercial farming grows for sale; subsistence farming feeds the farmer's own household
Difference between intensive and extensive farming	Intensive uses high inputs per hectare for high yield; extensive uses large areas with low inputs
Four physical factors in farming location	Climate, relief, soil, water
Farming as a system	Inputs, processes, outputs, feedback
How farming causes eutrophication	Nitrate from fertiliser and effluent leaches into water, fuelling algal growth
Three sustainable farming practices	Riparian planting and fencing, nutrient budgeting, minimum tillage or crop rotation
Most modern food shortages are failures of what?	Access and affordability, not global production
Definition of footloose industry	Industry not tied to raw materials or fuel, so free to choose its location
When does an industry locate near its raw materials?	When the inputs are bulky or perishable
Five causes of tourism growth	Rising incomes, paid holidays, cheap air travel, online booking, political stability
Three benefits of tourism	Jobs, foreign currency, a multiplier effect on local businesses
Three problems of tourism	Seasonal low-paid jobs, profits leaking abroad, environmental damage
Definition of leakage in tourism	Profits flowing out to foreign-owned airlines and hotel chains
Three sustainable tourism measures	Visitor caps and permits, boardwalks and marked tracks, local ownership and guides
Four non-renewable energy sources	Coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear
Definition of renewable energy	Energy replenished on a human timescale
Does renewable mean clean and reliable?	No, wind and solar are intermittent and hydro floods valleys
Two disadvantages of hydroelectric power	Floods valleys and displaces people, traps sediment and blocks fish
Main limitation of geothermal power	It is only possible in volcanic regions
Main disadvantage of wind and solar power	Intermittent output needing back-up or storage
Four effects of over-abstracting water	Lower water tables, dried wetlands, saltwater intrusion, conflict between users
Definition of sustainable development	Meeting present needs without preventing future generations from meeting theirs
Definition of the enhanced greenhouse effect	Extra warming from human emissions raising greenhouse gas concentrations
Four greenhouse gases	Carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, halocarbons
Main human sources of methane	Ruminant livestock, rice paddies, landfill, leaks from fossil fuel extraction
Four carbon stores	Atmosphere, oceans, biosphere, geological stores
Two causes of sea-level rise	Thermal expansion of seawater and melting land ice
Cause of acid rain	Sulphur and nitrogen oxides dissolving in cloud water and falling downwind
