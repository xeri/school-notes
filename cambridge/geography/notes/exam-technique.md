# Exam technique: AS Geography

How the AS papers work and how marks are given. The 9696 syllabus is new for 2027, so details marked *(to verify)* need checking against the syllabus and the 2027 specimen papers.

## The papers

Papers 1 (Physical) and 2 (Human) each take 1 hour 30 minutes for 60 marks: 1.5 minutes a mark. You answer four questions in each:

| Section | What it is | Time |
|---|---|---|
| A | compulsory questions across all three topics *(one per topic: to verify)*, built on resources (maps, graphs, tables, photos), with short and extended parts | about 45 min |
| B | one topic of your choice: a 10-mark structured question, then one essay from a choice of two *(essay marks to verify)* | about 45 min |

## Section A: resources

- **Describe** what the resource shows: the overall pattern, then the highest and lowest values with figures and units, then the exception.
- **Explain** with geography, not with more description: why it happens.
- Quote the resource every time: figures, place names, grid references.

## Section B: the essay

- **Plan it in two minutes:** the argument, two or three developed points, a case study for each, and the judgement.
- **Case studies need detail:** the place, when it happened, figures, and what it shows about the question.
- **Judge it:** "to what extent" needs a clear answer, with what it depends on (scale, wealth, time, management).
- Essays are marked in levels; detail and a supported judgement lift the level.

## Command words in this subject

| Word | In geography |
|---|---|
| Describe | the pattern or features, with evidence from the resource |
| Explain | the reasons or processes that cause it |
| Compare | similarities and differences, using both sources |
| Assess · Evaluate · To what extent | weigh the factors or arguments and reach a supported judgement |
| Suggest | a plausible reason applied to an unfamiliar place |

Check the 9696 syllabus's own list *(to verify)*.

## This loses marks

- Describing a resource without figures, or explaining without processes.
- Case studies with no place, date or numbers.
- Essays that are all description, with no judgement.
