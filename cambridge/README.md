## How this folder works

- **One folder per subject, for the whole syllabus.** The AS topics are here now; the A Level topics go in the same folder in 2028. They are already listed in each syllabus page, marked 2028.
- **Files are named the way the syllabus numbers them:**
  - Economics and Business use the topic number, because their A Level topics repeat the AS titles: `economics/notes/02-price-system.md` now, `07-…` to `11-…` in 2028.
  - Maths and English use the paper: `p1-quadratics.md`, `s1-normal-distribution.md`, `p2-prose.md`.
  - Geography uses the topic name: `rivers.md`, `urban.md`.
- **Each subject has:**
  - `SYLLABUS.md`: the papers, every topic, and the checklist of what is examined;
  - `notes/study-guide.md`: the priority order and a progress checklist;
  - `notes/exam-technique.md`: how each paper works and how marks are given;
  - one note per topic;
  - one reference sheet.
- **Skeletons.** Every AS topic note starts as a skeleton. Its headings follow the syllabus, and its first line says *Status: skeleton*.
- **Writing up a topic once it's taught:**
  1. Fill in the sections. Definitions go in as `**Term** = …`, traps under "This loses marks".
  2. Delete the status line.
  3. Start the topic's deck (below).
  4. Tick it in the study guide's checklist.
- **(to verify)** marks a fact not yet checked against the official syllabus PDF, which wasn't available when this was written. Clear the marks once you have it. Check a note's headings against it before you start revising that topic: the study tools use headings as ids.
- **Not wired into the tools yet.** `/study`, the wiki and the PDFs still cover Pre-Q only. This folder gets connected after the Pre-Q exams, when the Term 4 folders are archived.

## How AS and A Level work

- **AS Level is the first half of a Cambridge International A Level, and a qualification in its own right.** It is roughly half the content, examined in two papers.
- **Grades.** AS is graded A–E and A Level A*–E. Each paper's raw mark is turned into a grade with the grade thresholds Cambridge publishes for every series.
- **Routes:**
  - **Staged:** AS in November 2027, then the A Level in November 2028 by sitting only the A Level papers. The AS result carries forward if the A Level comes within 13 months of the AS series (November to November is 12).
  - **AS only:** stop after AS; it still counts.
  - **All at once:** every paper in one series.
  - Maths: Paper 2 (Pure Mathematics 2) cannot count towards an A Level; Papers 1, 4 and 5 can.
- **Series and dates.** Exams run in June and November; this plan assumes October/November 2027. Cambridge publishes the timetable months ahead: the November 2026 timetable came out in late March 2026, and those papers ran from about 28 September to 13 November. Results come in mid-January.

## From IGCSE to AS

What changes in every subject:
- **Depth, not breadth.** Fewer topics, each taken further. An answer is marked on how far it develops a point, not on how many points it lists.
- **Analysis and evaluation carry the marks.** The long answers reward a complete chain of reasoning and a judgement that says what the outcome depends on. Definitions alone earn little.
- **Unseen material.** Data you haven't seen (Economics, Business), resources such as maps, graphs and photos (Geography), and unseen texts (English) test applying what you know to something new.
- **Long answers are marked in levels.** The examiner places the whole answer in a level, then fine-tunes the mark. One developed argument beats several thin ones.
- **Maths wants exact algebra.** Exact answers, "show that" questions, full working, and 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
- **More independent reading.** The textbook, past papers and examiner reports matter as much as class notes.

What carries over from Pre-Q in each subject is in the **From IGCSE** table on its syllabus page.

## How marks are given

- **Assessment objectives.** Every syllabus weights knowledge and understanding, application and analysis, and evaluation (Maths: knowledge and application). The exact objectives and weightings are on each syllabus page *(to verify)*.
- **Levels marking.** Extended answers are marked in levels. The level comes from the answer's best overall quality, such as developed analysis with a supported judgement; the mark within the level comes from how fully it does that.
- **Command words** (the usual meanings; each syllabus has its own list, so check it):

| Word | What it asks for |
|---|---|
| Analyse | examine in detail: causes, effects and the links between them |
| Assess · Evaluate | weigh the evidence or arguments and reach a supported judgement |
| Calculate | work out a value from the information given, showing the method |
| Compare | say how two things are alike and how they differ |
| Define | give the precise meaning |
| Describe | say what something is like, or what happens, in detail |
| Discuss | write about an issue in depth, looking at more than one side |
| Explain | give the reasons why and how, with the links made clear |
| Identify · State | name or give it, briefly |
| Justify | support a choice or a conclusion with evidence and reasons |
| Outline | give the main points only |
| Show that | set out the steps that lead to the given result |
| Suggest | apply what you know to a new situation where more than one answer is possible |

## Finding public material

- **Each subject's page on cambridgeinternational.org** has:
  - the syllabus PDF (content, papers, command words; for Maths, the formula list MF19);
  - specimen papers, which matter where the format is new: Geography 9696 from 2027, and the new Section C in Economics Paper 2;
  - the endorsed textbooks.
- **Past papers, mark schemes, examiner reports and grade thresholds** are named like `9708_w24_qp_21`:
  - `9708` is the syllabus;
  - `w24` is the series and year: `m` = February/March, `s` = May/June, `w` = October/November;
  - `qp` is the document type: question paper (`ms` mark scheme, `er` examiner report, `gt` grade thresholds, `in` insert);
  - `21` means Paper 2, variant 1.
- **Examiner reports** are the best source for each note's "This loses marks": they say exactly what candidates got wrong.
- **Keep downloads in `local/cambridge/`.** It is gitignored, so they are never published.

## Conventions

- **Topic notes.** `# Title`, then one `##` per syllabus subtopic with its number (`## 2.2 Price elasticity…`), then `## This loses marks` and `## Practice`. Never put *(to verify)* in a heading.
- **Diagrams** are SVGs beside the notes, named `<subject>-<what>.svg` (e.g. `economics-ped.svg`) so they never clash with the Pre-Q diagrams.
- **Decks.** A topic's deck is `anki/<same name as the note>.txt`, started with its first cards, and linked from the Cards column of the syllabus page. It starts with these five lines:

  ```
  #separator:tab
  #html:true
  #notetype column:1
  #deck:Cambridge::Economics::02 The price system and the microeconomy
  #tags:cambridge economics as 02-price-system
  ```

  - **Card lines:** each line is note type, front (or cloze text), back (or extra), hint, separated by tabs.
  - **Tags:** A Level decks are tagged `a-level` instead of `as`.
  - **Note types:** **Cambridge Basic** and **Cambridge Cloze**. In Anki, clone Pre-Q Basic and Pre-Q Cloze and rename them; the fields stay the same. Don't reuse the Pre-Q types: Anki updates a note when an imported one has the same type and the same front, so a new "Opportunity cost?" card would overwrite the Pre-Q one.
