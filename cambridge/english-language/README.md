## Exam technique

- **Paper 1:** about 65 minutes a question, including reading time.
  - **Directed response:** write the new text in the form and for the audience asked, using the source's material without copying it.
  - **Analysis:** start from the text's audience, purpose and form, then show how its language and structure serve them. Quote briefly and comment on the effect.
  - **Comparison:** compare point by point, not one text and then the other.
- **Paper 2:** about an hour a question.
  - Get the form's conventions right, and keep a consistent register for the reader.
  - A reflective commentary explains your main choices and their intended effect. It doesn't retell the piece.
- **Marks:** reading for understanding and analysis; writing for how well it suits its form, audience and purpose, and for accuracy and range *(assessment objectives to verify)*. Both are marked in levels.

**This loses marks:**
- features listed with no effect;
- the wrong form or register;
- a commentary that summarises.

## How to study it

- Read widely in the forms you'll meet: articles, speeches, reviews, blogs, letters, narratives.
- For each text, name its audience, purpose and form, then find the language and structure that serve them.
- Write, then comment: after each piece, a few lines on why you made your main choices.
