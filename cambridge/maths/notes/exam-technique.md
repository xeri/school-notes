# Exam technique: AS Maths

How the AS papers work and how marks are given. Details marked *(to verify)* need checking against the syllabus and a recent paper.

## The papers

- **Paper 1, Pure Mathematics 1:** 1 hour 50 minutes, 75 marks, about 10–12 structured questions *(to verify)*: about 1.5 minutes a mark.
- **Paper 5, Probability & Statistics 1:** 1 hour 15 minutes *(to verify)*, 50 marks: about 1.5 minutes a mark.
- Questions usually get harder through the paper; a stuck question should cost no more than its marks' worth of minutes.

## Accuracy and answers

- **Non-exact answers to 3 significant figures, and angles in degrees to 1 decimal place,** unless the question says otherwise. Keep more figures in your working than in the answer.
- **"Exact"** (or "in surd form", "in terms of π") means no decimals.
- **"Show that"** gives you the answer: every step must be written, and the last line must match the given result.
- **Radians:** for calculus and circular measure, set the calculator to radians.

## How marks are given

- **M marks** are for a correct method, applied to the right numbers.
- **A marks** are for an accurate answer, and depend on the M mark before them.
- **B marks** are for a correct result on its own.
- **Follow-through:** a later step can still earn marks after an earlier error, but only if the working is shown. A bare answer earns nothing if it's wrong, and may earn nothing even if it's right.

## The formula list and the calculator

- A list of formulae and statistical tables (MF19) is provided; know what's on it and what isn't ([FORMULAS.md](../FORMULAS.md)) *(to verify)*.
- A scientific calculator is expected; check which calculators are allowed *(to verify)*.

## Paper 5 habits

- Define the random variable and state its distribution with its parameters, e.g. X ~ B(10, 0.3), before calculating.
- Sketch the normal curve, shade the region, and only then standardise.
- Use a continuity correction whenever a discrete distribution is approximated by the normal.

## This loses marks

- Rounding too early, then giving an answer outside the accepted range.
- Losing solutions of a trig equation outside the first quadrant.
- Forgetting the constant of integration, or a ± from a square root.
- Answers in degrees where radians were needed.
