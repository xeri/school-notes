# Maths study guide (AS, Oct/Nov 2027)

What to study first and how, through the AS year. The papers and every topic are on the [syllabus page](../SYLLABUS.md).

## The exam at a glance

| Paper | Content | Marks | Time | Pace |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Pure Mathematics 1: about 10–12 structured questions *(to verify)* | 75 | 1 h 50 | about 1.5 minutes a mark |
| **5** | Probability & Statistics 1 | 50 | 1 h 15 *(to verify)* | 1.5 minutes a mark |

Paper 1 is 60% of the AS. Method marks need visible working, and non-exact answers need 3 significant figures (angles in degrees to 1 decimal place) unless the question says otherwise. Technique: [exam technique](exam-technique.md).

## Priority order

Ranked on marks and on what each topic unlocks. Re-rank as the year goes.

| Rank | Topic | Why it is here | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Exam technique](exam-technique.md) | Small errors cost the most marks at Pre-Q | Read it before every past paper; log each lost mark by type |
| 2 | [Differentiation](p1-differentiation.md) | Several questions every paper, and needed for integration | Past-paper questions by type until each first step is automatic |
| 3 | [Integration](p1-integration.md) | Areas and volumes are long, high-mark questions | Set up the integral first, then evaluate |
| 4 | [Quadratics](p1-quadratics.md) | The algebra under nearly everything | Completing the square and the discriminant until automatic |
| 5 | [Functions](p1-functions.md) | A regular full question; transformations recur | Sketch each transformation |
| 6 | [Trigonometry](p1-trigonometry.md) | Equations and identities appear every paper | Solve in an interval: list every solution |
| 7 | [The normal distribution](s1-normal-distribution.md) | The biggest Paper 5 question type | Standardise, sketch, then read the table |
| 8 | [Discrete random variables](s1-discrete-random-variables.md) | Binomial and geometric questions every paper | Name the distribution and its parameters first |
| 9 | [Coordinate geometry](p1-coordinate-geometry.md) | Lines and circles, often combined | A sketch for every question |
| 10 | [Series](p1-series.md) | Binomial expansions and progressions are reliable marks | Formulas in Anki; practise "find n" |
| 11 | [Probability](s1-probability.md) | Conditional probability is the usual trap | Tree or Venn diagram every time |
| 12 | [Circular measure](p1-circular-measure.md) | One question, usually combined with geometry | Radians only |
| 13 | [Representation of data](s1-data.md) | Routine marks if the calculator work is careful | Summary-statistics formulas |
| 14 | [Permutations and combinations](s1-permutations-combinations.md) | One question; hard to fake | Count the restrictions first |

## How to study maths at AS

- **Past-paper questions by topic,** as soon as a topic is written up. Mark them with the mark scheme, and redo every lost mark two days later.
- **Derive rather than memorise** where you can, and learn by heart only what the formula list doesn't give ([FORMULAS.md](../FORMULAS.md)).
- **Recognising the question type is half the work:** for a batch of questions, name the first step of each before solving any.
