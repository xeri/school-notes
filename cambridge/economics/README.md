## Exam technique

**Paper 1, at 2 minutes a question.**
- Work out the answer before reading the options, then cross out the two that are clearly wrong. Sketch the diagram in the margin.
- Nothing is lost for a wrong answer, so answer every question.
- The traps: shift vs movement, demand vs quantity demanded, nominal vs real, the sign and size of an elasticity.

**Paper 2, at 2 minutes a mark.** Take 10 minutes to read the data and choose your essays, then about 40 minutes for the data response and 35 for each essay.
- **Data response:** quote the figures with units and years, then explain them. Use the extract's own context in the judgement part.
- **Essays:** usually an explain/analyse part and an evaluate part *(8 + 12 marks: to verify)*. Choose the question by its evaluation part.
- **Analysis** is a complete chain, cause → mechanism → effect, with a labelled diagram where one fits.
- **Evaluation** says what the outcome depends on: time, the size of the change, elasticity, the state of the economy. A summary isn't evaluation.
- Long answers are marked in levels: one developed argument with a supported judgement beats a list.

**This loses marks:**
- unlabelled diagrams, or no new equilibrium;
- a chain with a missing link ("rates rise, so inflation falls");
- "however" points with no judgement on which matters more.

## How to study it

- The Pre-Q loop still works: read one chain, close the page, blurt it, draw its diagram, check, fix.
- Add a judgement to every chain: "this works if…, but…".
- From Term 2: plan five past-paper essays for every one written in full, and do ten multiple-choice questions a day on written topics.
