# Exam technique: AS Economics

How each AS paper works and how its marks are given. Details marked *(to verify)* need checking against the syllabus and a recent mark scheme.

## Paper 1: multiple choice

- 30 questions with four options each, in 1 hour: 2 minutes a question. Each correct answer scores one mark and nothing is taken off for a wrong one, so answer every question.
- Work out the answer from the question before you read the options, then cross out the two that are clearly wrong.
- Sketch the diagram in the margin for anything about shifts, elasticity, taxes or AD/AS.
- The traps are precise distinctions: a shift versus a movement, demand versus quantity demanded, nominal versus real, the sign and size of an elasticity.

## Paper 2: data response and essays

2 hours for 60 marks: about 2 minutes a mark, after 10 minutes reading the data and choosing your essays.

| Section | What it is | Time |
|---|---|---|
| A: data response | compulsory short parts on unseen data about a real economy, ending with a judgement part *(mark split to verify)* | 40 min |
| B: microeconomics essay | one question from a choice; usually an explain/analyse part, then an evaluate part *(to verify: 8 + 12 marks)* | 35 min |
| C: macroeconomics essay | the same shape, on macro topics | 35 min |

- **In the data response,** quote the figures (with units and years) before explaining them, and use the extract's own context in the judgement part.
- **Choose an essay by its evaluation part:** pick the question where you can argue both sides with full chains.

## How the long answers are marked

- **Levels.** The examiner reads the whole answer and places it in a level, from simple statements up to developed analysis with a supported judgement, then picks the mark within the level.
- **Analysis** is a complete chain: cause → mechanism → effect, each link stated. Where a diagram fits, draw it fully labelled and refer to it in the text.
- **Evaluation** is a judgement that says what the outcome depends on: the time period, the size of the change, elasticity, the state of the economy, or how the policy is designed. A summary of what you already wrote isn't evaluation.

## Command words in this subject

| Word | In economics |
|---|---|
| Define | the precise meaning in one sentence, with the key words (e.g. "willing **and able**") |
| Explain | the reasons why and how, as a chain |
| Analyse | the chain step by step, usually with a diagram |
| Assess · Evaluate · Discuss | both sides, each developed, and a conclusion that says what it depends on |
| Consider | weigh the factors and say which matters most |

Check the 9708 syllabus's own list of command words *(to verify)*.

## This loses marks

- Diagrams with unlabelled axes or curves, or with no new equilibrium shown.
- A chain that skips a link (e.g. "interest rates rise, so inflation falls").
- Evaluation that only lists "however" points without saying which matters more, and why.
