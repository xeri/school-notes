## 1. Basic economic ideas and resource allocation

*Outline, to verify against the syllabus.*

**1.1 Scarcity, choice and opportunity cost** ([notes](notes/01-basic-ideas.md))
- The basic economic problem: unlimited wants, limited resources, so choices and an opportunity cost.

**1.2 Economic methodology** ([notes](notes/01-basic-ideas.md))
- Economics as a social science; positive and normative statements; ceteris paribus; the short run, long run and very long run.

**1.3 Factors of production** ([notes](notes/01-basic-ideas.md))
- Land, labour, capital and enterprise, and their rewards; specialisation and the division of labour; the role of the entrepreneur.

**1.4 Resource allocation in different economic systems** ([notes](notes/01-basic-ideas.md))
- What, how and for whom to produce; market, planned and mixed economies, and how each answers those questions.

**1.5 Production possibility curves** ([notes](notes/01-basic-ideas.md))
- Points inside, on and outside the curve; constant and increasing opportunity cost; what shifts the curve.

**1.6 Classification of goods and services** ([notes](notes/01-basic-ideas.md))
- Free and economic goods; private and public goods; merit and demerit goods, and information failure.

## 2. The price system and the microeconomy

*Outline, to verify against the syllabus.*

**2.1 Demand and supply curves** ([notes](notes/02-price-system.md))
- Individual and market demand and supply; movements along a curve versus shifts; the causes of shifts.

**2.2 Price elasticity, income elasticity and cross elasticity of demand** ([notes](notes/02-price-system.md))
- Each formula, its calculation and interpretation; the determinants; PED and total revenue; uses for firms and the government.

**2.3 Price elasticity of supply** ([notes](notes/02-price-system.md))
- Formula and interpretation; the determinants (time, spare capacity, stocks).

**2.4 The interaction of demand and supply** ([notes](notes/02-price-system.md))
- Equilibrium and disequilibrium; the effects of shifts; what the price mechanism does; joint, competitive and derived demand, and joint supply.

**2.5 Consumer and producer surplus** ([notes](notes/02-price-system.md))
- Their meaning and how to show them on a diagram; what changes them; how elasticity affects the size of the change.

## 3. Government microeconomic intervention

*Outline, to verify against the syllabus.*

**3.1 Reasons for government intervention in markets** ([notes](notes/03-micro-intervention.md))
- Why markets can give the wrong outcome: merit and demerit goods, public goods, information failure.

**3.2 Methods and effects of government intervention in markets** ([notes](notes/03-micro-intervention.md))
- Indirect taxes and subsidies and their incidence; maximum and minimum prices; direct provision; regulation; information; unintended consequences.

**3.3 Addressing income and wealth inequality** ([notes](notes/03-micro-intervention.md))
- Equity and equality; income and wealth; the policies that redistribute them and their effects.

## 4. The macroeconomy

*Outline, to verify against the syllabus.*

**4.1 National income statistics** ([notes](notes/04-macroeconomy.md))
- GDP, GNI and NNI; nominal and real; per head; what the figures are used for and their limits.

**4.2 Introduction to the circular flow of income** ([notes](notes/04-macroeconomy.md))
- Flows in closed and open economies; injections and withdrawals; how a change affects national income.

**4.3 Aggregate demand and aggregate supply analysis** ([notes](notes/04-macroeconomy.md))
- The components of AD and what shifts them; short-run and long-run AS; macroeconomic equilibrium; the effects of shifts on output and the price level.

**4.4 Economic growth** ([notes](notes/04-macroeconomy.md))
- Actual and potential growth; how growth is measured; its causes and consequences.

**4.5 Unemployment** ([notes](notes/04-macroeconomy.md))
- How unemployment is measured; its types and causes; its consequences.

**4.6 Price stability** ([notes](notes/04-macroeconomy.md))
- Inflation, deflation and disinflation; how prices are measured; demand-pull and cost-push causes; consequences.

## 5. Government macroeconomic intervention

*Outline, to verify against the syllabus.*

**5.1 Government macroeconomic policy objectives** ([notes](notes/05-macro-intervention.md))
- Growth, low unemployment, stable prices and a sustainable current account.

**5.2 Fiscal policy** ([notes](notes/05-macro-intervention.md))
- The budget (deficit and surplus); types of tax; government spending; expansionary and contractionary policy and its effect on AD.

**5.3 Monetary policy** ([notes](notes/05-macro-intervention.md))
- Interest rates and the money supply; expansionary and contractionary policy; the transmission to AD.

**5.4 Supply-side policy** ([notes](notes/05-macro-intervention.md))
- Market-based and interventionist policies (education and training, deregulation, privatisation, incentives) and their effect on long-run AS.

## 6. International economic issues

*Outline, to verify against the syllabus.*

**6.1 The reasons for international trade** ([notes](notes/06-international.md))
- Specialisation; absolute and comparative advantage; the terms of trade; the benefits and costs of trade.

**6.2 Protectionism** ([notes](notes/06-international.md))
- Tariffs, quotas, subsidies, embargoes and other barriers; the arguments for and against.

**6.3 Current account of the balance of payments** ([notes](notes/06-international.md))
- Its components; the causes and consequences of deficits and surpluses.

**6.4 Exchange rates** ([notes](notes/06-international.md))
- Floating, fixed and managed systems; what determines a floating rate; appreciation and depreciation; the effects on the economy.

**6.5 Policies to correct imbalances in the current account of the balance of payments** ([notes](notes/06-international.md))
- How fiscal, monetary, supply-side and protectionist policies affect the current account.
