#deck:Physics::6 Waves, light and sound
What does a wave transfer?	Energy, without transferring matter
Definition of wavelength	The distance between matching points on consecutive waves
Definition of frequency	The number of oscillations per second, in Hz
Definition of period	The time for one oscillation, \(T = 1/f\)
Definition of amplitude	The maximum displacement from the undisturbed position
Definition of a wavefront	A line joining points oscillating in step
Wave equation	\(v = f\lambda\)
A 50 Hz wave of wavelength 0.40 m: speed and period?	\(v = 20\) m/s, \(T = 0.020\) s
Definition of a transverse wave	Oscillation perpendicular to the direction of travel
Definition of a longitudinal wave	Oscillation parallel to the direction of travel
Three transverse wave examples	Water waves, all electromagnetic waves, a wave on a rope
Two longitudinal wave examples	Sound, a slinky pushed along its length
What changes during refraction?	Speed, wavelength and direction — the frequency stays the same
When is diffraction through a gap greatest?	When the gap size is about equal to the wavelength
Which wavelengths diffract most at an edge?	Longer ones — which is why low-pitched sound bends round corners more easily
What apparatus demonstrates reflection, refraction and diffraction of waves?	A ripple tank
State the law of reflection	Angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal
Four properties of a plane-mirror image	Same size, same distance behind the mirror, laterally inverted, virtual
What is a virtual image?	One the rays only appear to come from — it cannot be caught on a screen
Which way does light bend entering a denser medium?	Towards the normal (it slows down)
When does a ray not bend at a boundary?	When it travels along the normal
What happens to a ray through a parallel-sided glass block?	It emerges parallel to its original direction, displaced sideways
Refractive index from angles	\(n = \sin i / \sin r\)
Refractive index from speeds	\(n = \) speed in vacuum / speed in the medium
Refractive index of glass	About 1.5
Two conditions for total internal reflection	Travelling from denser to less dense medium, AND angle of incidence greater than the critical angle
Critical angle formula	\(n = 1/\sin c\)
Critical angle for n = 1.5	42°
How do you demonstrate the critical angle?	Aim a ray at the centre of the flat face of a semicircular glass block and rotate it
Medical use of optical fibres	An endoscope carries light in and an image out of the body without surgery
Communications advantage of optical fibres	Far more data, less signal loss, no electrical interference
Definition of principal focus	The point where a converging lens brings a parallel beam to meet
Definition of focal length	The distance from the lens to the principal focus
Ray rule 1 for a converging lens	A ray parallel to the axis passes through the principal focus
Ray rule 2 for a converging lens	A ray through the centre of the lens continues straight
Image when the object is beyond 2f	Real, inverted, diminished
Image when the object is between f and 2f	Real, inverted, enlarged
Image when the object is inside f	Virtual, upright, enlarged — a magnifying glass
Why does a prism disperse white light?	Each colour has a slightly different refractive index, so each bends by a different amount
Which colour bends most in a prism?	Violet (red bends least)
Seven colours of the spectrum in order	Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet
Meaning of monochromatic	Light of a single frequency
Primary colours of light	Red, green, blue
Red + green =	Yellow
Green + blue =	Cyan
Red + blue =	Magenta
Why does a red book look black under blue light?	It absorbs blue and has no red light to reflect
Electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing frequency	Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma
Speed of all EM waves in a vacuum	\(3.0\times10^8\) m/s
Uses of microwaves	Satellite television, mobile phones, cooking
Uses of infrared	Electrical appliances, TV remote controls, intruder alarms, thermal imaging
Uses of X-rays	Medical imaging, airport security scanning
Safety danger of microwaves	They are absorbed by water and heat body cells
Safety danger of X-rays and gamma rays	They are ionising — they damage DNA and can cause cell mutation
Which EM regions are NOT ionising?	Radio, microwave, infrared (and visible)
How is sound produced?	By a vibrating source
Is sound transverse or longitudinal?	Longitudinal
What are compressions and rarefactions?	Regions where air particles are closer/higher pressure and further apart/lower pressure
Why does a bell in a bell jar go silent as air is pumped out?	Sound is a mechanical vibration of particles, so it needs a medium
Audible range for a healthy human ear	About 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz
Definition of ultrasound	Sound of frequency above the human audible range
Speed of sound in air	About 330–340 m/s
Speed of sound in water	About 1500 m/s
Speed of sound in steel	About 5000 m/s
Why is sound fastest in solids?	The particles are closest together with the strongest forces between them
What does loudness depend on?	Amplitude
What does pitch depend on?	Frequency
Echo method for the speed of sound	Clap at a measured distance d from a wall, time the echo t; \(v = 2d/t\)
What does the eardrum do?	Vibrates in response to pressure variations in the air
What does the cochlea do?	Converts mechanical vibration into electrical impulses carried to the brain by the auditory nerve
