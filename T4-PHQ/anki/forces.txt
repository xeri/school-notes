#deck:Physics::3 Forces and momentum
Definition of mass	A measure of a body's resistance to a change in its state of motion (inertia), in kg — a scalar
Definition of weight	The gravitational force on a mass in a gravitational field, in N, acting vertically downwards — a vector
Formula for weight	\(W = mg\)
Weight of a 60 kg student on Earth	\(60 \times 9.81 = 589\) N
Why does a balance only work in a gravitational field?	It compares masses by comparing their weights, and weight needs a field
Name the experiment showing all objects fall at the same rate in a vacuum	The guinea-and-feather experiment (coin and feather in an evacuated tube)
Three things a force can change	Size/shape, speed, direction (so velocity)
What does zero resultant force mean?	The body stays at rest OR continues at constant velocity in a straight line
Newton's second law	\(F = ma\)
Definition of friction	The force between two surfaces that impedes motion and causes heating
What is drag?	Air resistance — a form of friction
State Hooke's law	Extension is proportional to the stretching force, up to the limit of proportionality
Hooke's law formula	\(F = kx\), where k is the spring constant in N/m
What is x in F = kx?	The extension (stretched length − natural length), NOT the total length
What is the limit of proportionality?	The point beyond which extension stops being proportional to force and the graph curves
How do you find a spring constant experimentally?	Plot force against extension; the gradient of the straight section is k
What force is needed for circular motion?	A resultant force towards the centre, perpendicular to the velocity
Circular motion formula	\(F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}\)
Why does a centripetal force not change speed?	It acts perpendicular to the motion, so it does no work — only the direction changes
What happens if the centripetal force is removed?	The body travels off along the tangent
Definition of the moment of a force	The product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to its line of action
Units of moment	N·m
Two ways to increase a moment	Increase the force, or increase the perpendicular distance from the pivot
State the principle of moments	For a body in equilibrium, total clockwise moments = total anticlockwise moments
4.0 N at 0.30 m left of a pivot; where must 6.0 N hang on the right?	\(6.0d = 4.0(0.30) \Rightarrow d = 0.20\) m
Conditions for equilibrium	No resultant force AND no resultant moment
Definition of centre of mass	The point at which all of a body's weight can be taken to act
How do you find the centre of mass of a plane lamina?	Hang it from a pin at three different holes with a plumb-line each time; the three vertical lines cross at the centre of mass
Stable equilibrium	Displaced slightly, the weight's line of action stays inside the base, so a restoring moment returns it
Unstable equilibrium	Displaced slightly, the weight's line of action falls outside the base and topples it further
Neutral equilibrium	Displacement produces no moment either way, e.g. a ball on a flat table
What makes an object harder to topple?	A wide base and a low centre of mass
Momentum formula	\(p = mv\), in kg·m/s — a vector
Definition of impulse	The change in momentum, \(Ft = mv - mu\)
Why do crumple zones reduce injury?	For the same momentum change, a longer contact time gives a smaller average force
State conservation of momentum	In an isolated system, total momentum stays constant
Definition of an isolated system	One with no external forces acting on the bodies, e.g. no friction with the floor
2.0 kg at 3.0 m/s hits a stationary 1.0 kg and they stick: final speed?	\(6.0 = 3.0v \Rightarrow v = 2.0\) m/s
Are elastic and inelastic collision labels in scope?	No — but momentum calculations on collisions are
State Newton's first law	With no resultant force, an object stays at rest or keeps moving at constant velocity
State Newton's third law	Forces occur in pairs: equal in size, opposite in direction, of the same type, and acting on different objects
Resultant force in terms of momentum	\(F = \Delta p / t\) — the rate of change of momentum
