#deck:Physics::7 Electricity and magnetism
What are magnetic domains?	Small crystal regions that each act as a tiny magnet; aligning them magnetises the material
Why does a magnet attract unmagnetised iron but only repel another magnet?	Attraction only needs the iron's domains to line up (induced magnetism); repulsion needs an existing like pole facing it
Which way do magnetic field lines run outside a magnet?	From N to S
Where is a magnetic field strongest?	At the poles, where the field lines are closest together
Can magnetic field lines cross?	No — magnetic forces arise from field lines that cannot intersect
What do iron filings show, and what do they not show?	The field pattern, but not the direction — use a plotting compass for that
What is a neutral point?	A point where two fields cancel, e.g. between two facing N poles
Three ways to magnetise a material	Stroking with one pole, placing in a coil carrying d.c., hammering inside a magnetic field
Three ways to demagnetise a material	Hammering, heating strongly, placing in a coil carrying a.c.
Property of soft iron	It loses its magnetism as soon as the external field is removed — ideal for electromagnet and transformer cores
Property of a hard magnetic material like steel	It keeps its magnetism — ideal for permanent magnets
Two advantages of an electromagnet over a permanent magnet	It can be switched off, and its strength can be varied
What actually moves when you charge by friction?	Electrons only — protons stay in the nucleus
How does a gold-leaf electroscope detect charge?	The leaf and stem acquire like charges and repel, so the leaf rises
Steps for charging by induction	Bring a charged rod near, earth the far side briefly, remove the earth, then remove the rod — the sphere keeps the opposite charge
Why do metals conduct electricity?	They have free (delocalised) electrons that drift through the lattice
Definition of an electric field	A region in which a charge experiences a force
Direction of an electric field at a point	The direction of the force on a positive charge there
Field pattern between two parallel plates	Uniform — evenly spaced parallel lines from + to −
Field pattern around a charged conducting sphere	Radial outside as if from the centre; zero inside
Definition of current	The rate of flow of charge
Current formula	\(I = Q/t\)
Which way do electrons flow compared with conventional current?	Opposite — electrons go negative to positive, conventional current positive to negative
Definition of e.m.f.	The energy change inside the supply, per unit charge, that drives charge round a complete circuit
Definition of potential difference	The energy transferred per unit charge in a component
What is 1 volt in base terms?	1 J/C
Resistance formula	\(R = V/I\)
Is current used up passing through a resistor?	No — charge is conserved
How does resistance depend on wire length?	Directly proportional — double the length, double the resistance
How does resistance depend on cross-sectional area?	Inversely proportional — double the diameter (×4 area) and the resistance quarters
Resistivity formula	\(R = \rho L / A\)
I–V graph of an ohmic resistor	A straight line through the origin
I–V graph of a filament lamp	An S-shaped curve that flattens
Why does a filament lamp's resistance rise with current?	It heats up, the lattice ions vibrate more, and electrons collide more often
Current in a series circuit	The same at every point
P.d. in a series circuit	The p.d.s across components add to the supply p.d.
Current in a parallel circuit	The source current equals the sum of the branch currents
P.d. in a parallel circuit	The same across each branch
Series resistance formula	\(R_T = R_1 + R_2\)
Parallel resistance formula	\(R_T = \dfrac{R_1R_2}{R_1+R_2}\)
Why is combined parallel resistance less than either resistor?	There are more paths for the charge to take
Three reasons household lamps are wired in parallel	Each can be switched independently, each gets full mains p.d., one failing does not black out the house
Electrical power formula	\(P = IV\)
Electrical energy formula	\(E = IVt\)
6.0 V across 3.0 Ω: current and power?	\(I = 2.0\) A, \(P = 12\) W
What does a potential divider do?	Taps off a fraction of the supply p.d. from two resistors in series
Potential divider formula	\(V_{out} = V_{in}\dfrac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}\)
How does a thermistor's resistance change?	It falls sharply as temperature rises
How does an LDR's resistance change?	It falls as light intensity rises
What does a relay do?	A small coil current closes contacts in a separate circuit, letting a small safe current switch a large one, with electrical isolation
What does a diode do?	Conducts in one direction only; as a rectifier it converts a.c. to d.c.
How do you build a light-sensitive switch?	LDR in a potential divider; the changing output p.d. operates a relay that switches the lamp
Three electrical hazards	Damaged insulation, overheating cables, damp conditions
Plug wire colours	Live brown, neutral blue, earth green/yellow stripes
What does a fuse protect?	The appliance and the wiring — NOT the person
Fuse for a 230 V, 700 W appliance	\(I = 700/230 = 3.0\) A, so a 5 A fuse
How does earthing make an appliance safe?	A fault makes a large current flow to earth through a low-resistance path, blowing the fuse at once so the case cannot stay live
When is an e.m.f. induced in a conductor?	When it moves across a magnetic field, or the field linking it changes
Three factors increasing an induced e.m.f.	Faster rate of change of field, more turns on the coil, a stronger magnet
State Lenz's law	The induced e.m.f. opposes the change that caused it
Fleming's right-hand rule is used for what?	The generator effect — thumb motion, first finger field, second finger induced current
Difference between a.c. and d.c.	d.c. flows one way only; a.c. reverses direction periodically
What do slip rings do in an a.c. generator?	Keep each coil end connected to the same brush, so the output alternates with the coil
When is a generator's output at a peak?	When the coil is parallel to the field, cutting field lines fastest
When is a generator's output zero?	When the coil is perpendicular to the field, momentarily cutting no lines
Why must a transformer use a.c.?	Only a changing magnetic field induces an e.m.f. in the secondary
Transformer turns equation	\(V_p/V_s = N_p/N_s\)
Transformer power equation (100% efficient)	\(I_pV_p = I_sV_s\)
Why is electricity transmitted at high voltage?	For a given power, higher V means lower I, and cable loss is \(I^2R\)
Cable power-loss formula	\(P = I^2R\)
Field pattern around a straight current-carrying wire	Concentric circles, weakening with distance; use the right-hand grip rule
Field pattern around a solenoid	Like a bar magnet's — strong and uniform inside, poles at the ends
Three ways to strengthen a solenoid's field	More current, more turns, an iron core
Fleming's left-hand rule is used for what?	The motor effect — first finger field, second finger current, thumb force
When is the force on a current-carrying wire greatest?	When the wire is perpendicular to the field (zero when parallel)
What happens if you reverse both the current and the field?	The force acts in the original direction
Three ways to increase a motor's turning effect	More turns, larger current, stronger magnetic field
What does a split-ring commutator do?	Reverses the current in the coil every half turn, so the turning effect always acts the same way and rotation continues
State Ohm's law	The current through a metallic conductor at constant temperature is directly proportional to the p.d. across it
