#deck:Physics::8 Atomic physics and space
Four atomic models in order	Hard sphere (Dalton), plum pudding (Thomson), nuclear (Rutherford), Bohr shells
Rutherford result 1: most α-particles pass straight through — conclusion?	The atom is mostly empty space
Rutherford result 2: some are deflected slightly — conclusion?	There is a concentrated positive charge repelling them
Rutherford result 3: a very few bounce back over 90° — conclusion?	That positive charge is in a very small, very massive nucleus
Why did α-scattering kill the plum-pudding model?	Plum pudding predicts only tiny deflections, never a bounce-back
Relative size of nucleus to inner electron orbits	The nucleus is about \(10^5\) times smaller
Charges of proton, neutron, electron	+1, 0, −1
What is Z?	Proton number (atomic number) — it identifies the element
What is A?	Nucleon number (atomic mass) = protons + neutrons
How do you find the number of neutrons?	\(A - Z\)
Nuclide notation	\(^{A}_{Z}X\)
Definition of isotopes	Nuclides of the same element — same Z, different A
Definition of nuclear fission	Splitting a large nucleus into smaller pieces
Definition of nuclear fusion	Joining two small nuclei into a larger one
What must balance in a nuclear equation?	Total A and total Z on both sides
Alpha decay of uranium-238	\(^{238}_{92}\mathrm{U} \rightarrow\ ^{234}_{90}\mathrm{Th} + ^{4}_{2}\alpha\)
Beta decay of carbon-14	\(^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C} \rightarrow\ ^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N} + ^{\ \ 0}_{-1}\mathrm{e}\)
What is an alpha particle?	A helium nucleus — 2 protons and 2 neutrons, charge +2
What is a beta particle?	A fast-moving electron, charge −1
What is a gamma ray?	A high-frequency electromagnetic wave, no charge, no mass
Order of ionising power	α (most) > β > γ (least)
What stops alpha radiation?	Paper, or a few cm of air
What stops beta radiation?	A few mm of aluminium
What stops gamma radiation?	Thick lead or very thick rock
Why is alpha the most ionising?	Large mass and double charge mean strong interaction with every atom it passes — so it loses energy quickly over a short range
Which emissions are deflected by a magnetic field?	α and β (both charged); γ is undeflected
Is β+ (positron) emission in scope?	No — all β here means β⁻
Two properties of radioactive decay	Random and spontaneous
Does temperature or chemistry affect decay rate?	No — decay is unaffected by temperature, pressure or chemical state
Sources of background radiation	Rocks (especially radon), cosmic rays, food, medical and industrial sources
What must you do with background before analysing a count rate?	Subtract it
What detects ionising radiation?	A Geiger–Müller tube with a counter (also film, cloud chamber, spark counter)
Why does a smoke detector use alpha?	It ionises air across a short gap; smoke interrupts the resulting current
Why is beta used for thickness monitoring?	It passes through thin sheets and its absorption varies with thickness
Why is gamma used for sterilising and tracing leaks?	It penetrates packaging, tissue and pipe walls
Definition of half-life	The time for the number of undecayed nuclei, or the count rate, to fall to half
Fraction remaining after 3 half-lives	1/8
Fraction remaining after n half-lives	\((\tfrac12)^n\)
Count falls 800 → 50 in 4 hours: half-life?	4 half-lives in 4 hours, so 1 hour
Background 20; sample 340 → 100 in 90 min: half-life?	Corrected 320 → 80 is 2 half-lives, so 45 minutes
Most common error in half-life questions	Forgetting to subtract the background count
Two biological effects of ionising radiation	Cell death (high dose) and cell mutation leading to cancer (lower dose)
Four radiation safety precautions	Lead-lined storage, long tongs, short exposure time, large distance (plus a film badge)
What process do nuclear power stations use?	Fission of a large nucleus such as uranium
What process powers the Sun?	Fusion of small nuclei such as hydrogen
Why does fusion need extreme conditions?	Enormous temperature and pressure are needed to overcome the repulsion between positive nuclei
Definition of an orbit	The curved path of one body around another under gravity
Shape of a planetary orbit	Elliptical, with the Sun at one focus
Orbital speed formula	\(v = 2\pi r/T\)
Satellite at \(r = 7.0\times10^6\) m, T = 6000 s: speed?	\(7.3\times10^3\) m/s
What force keeps a body in orbit?	Gravity, providing the centripetal force
What does surface gravitational field strength depend on?	The planet's mass (and radius)
How does gravitational field strength vary with distance from a planet?	It decreases as distance increases
Is a light year a time or a distance?	A distance
Value of a light year	About \(9.5\times10^{15}\) m
Diameter of the Milky Way	About 100 000 light-years
How is the distance to a far galaxy measured?	From the observed brightness of a supernova of known actual brightness
Definition of redshift	An increase in the observed wavelength of radiation from a receding star or galaxy
What does the redshift of almost all galaxies show?	The Universe is expanding — evidence for the Big Bang theory
Hubble's law	\(H_0 = v/d\)
Definition of the Hubble constant	The ratio of a galaxy's recession speed to its distance from Earth
Current estimate of \(H_0\)	About \(2.2\times10^{-18}\) s⁻¹
How do you estimate the age of the Universe?	\(1/H_0 \approx 4.5\times10^{17}\) s ≈ 14 billion years
What does one consistent \(H_0\) in all directions imply?	All the matter in the Universe was once at a single point
