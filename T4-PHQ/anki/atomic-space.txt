Pre-Q Basic	Nucleon?	A proton or neutron	
Pre-Q Basic	Nuclide?	One specific nucleus, with given Z and A	
Pre-Q Basic	Proton number (Z)?	Number of protons; identifies the element	Also called atomic number
Pre-Q Basic	Nucleon number (A)?	Number of protons + neutrons	Also called mass number
Pre-Q Basic	Isotopes?	Same proton number, different nucleon number	
Pre-Q Basic	Positive ion vs negative ion	Positive: lost electrons. Negative: gained electrons	
Pre-Q Basic	Radioactive decay?	Unstable nucleus emits α, β and/or γ	
Pre-Q Basic	Background radiation?	Low-level ionising radiation always present	
Pre-Q Basic	Corrected count rate?	Measured count rate − background count rate	
Pre-Q Basic	Half-life?	Time for half the nuclei to decay (count rate halves)	Not time to reach zero
Pre-Q Basic	Nuclear fission?	Splitting of a large nucleus into two smaller ones	
Pre-Q Basic	Nuclear fusion?	Joining of two light nuclei into a heavier one	
Pre-Q Basic	Chain reaction?	Released neutrons split more U-235 nuclei	
Pre-Q Basic	Control rods?	Absorb neutrons to control the reaction rate	
Pre-Q Basic	Moderator?	Slows neutrons so U-235 absorbs them	
Pre-Q Cloze	Atom models in order: {{c1::hard sphere}} → {{c2::plum pudding}} → {{c3::nuclear (Rutherford)}} → {{c4::Bohr shells}}		4 models
Pre-Q Cloze	α-scattering: most pass straight through, so {{c1::atom is mostly empty space}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	α-scattering: some deflected, so {{c1::concentrated positive charge}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	α-scattering: a few bounce back, so {{c1::tiny nucleus with most of the mass}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Why did α-scattering disprove plum pudding?	Plum pudding predicts only tiny deflections	
Pre-Q Basic	Nucleus size vs inner electron orbits?	About 10⁵ times smaller	
Pre-Q Cloze	Relative charge: proton {{c1::+1}}; neutron {{c2::0}}; electron {{c3::−1}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	Nuclide notation \(^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}\): neutrons = {{c1::A − Z}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	Symbols: α {{c1::\({}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}\)}}; β {{c2::\({}^{\ \ 0}_{-1}\mathrm{e}\)}}; γ {{c3::\({}^{0}_{0}\gamma\)}}; neutron {{c4::\({}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}\)}}		β has Z = −1
Pre-Q Cloze	α-particle = {{c1::helium nucleus (2p + 2n)}}, charge {{c2::+2}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	β-particle = {{c1::fast electron from the nucleus}}, charge {{c2::−1}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	γ-ray = {{c1::high-frequency EM wave}}, charge {{c2::0}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	Ionising power: {{c1::α}} &gt; {{c2::β}} &gt; {{c3::γ}}		Penetration is the reverse
Pre-Q Cloze	Stopped by: α {{c1::paper}}; β {{c2::few mm aluminium}}; γ {{c3::thick lead (reduced only)}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Why is α the most ionising?	Large charge (+2) and mass; slow	Loses energy fastest
Pre-Q Cloze	Between charged plates: α bends slightly to {{c1::−}}; β bends strongly to {{c2::+}}; γ {{c3::undeflected}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Why is β deflected more than α?	Much smaller mass	
Pre-Q Basic	Rule for α/β deflection in a magnetic field?	Fleming's left-hand rule	β counts as current the other way
Pre-Q Cloze	Decay is {{c1::random}} and {{c2::spontaneous}}	Unaffected by temperature, pressure, chemistry	
Pre-Q Basic	Why are some nuclei unstable?	Too many neutrons and/or too heavy	
Pre-Q Cloze	Which emissions change the element? {{c1::α and β}}; {{c2::γ}} does not		
Pre-Q Cloze	α-decay: \(^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \rightarrow {{c1::{}^{A-4}_{Z-2}\mathrm{Y} }} + {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}\)		Loses a helium nucleus
Pre-Q Cloze	β⁻-decay: \(^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \rightarrow {{c1::{}^{\ \ \ A}_{Z+1}\mathrm{Y} }} + {}^{\ \ 0}_{-1}\mathrm{e}\)		A neutron turns into a proton
Pre-Q Cloze	γ-emission: \(^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}^{*} \rightarrow {{c1::{}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} }} + {}^{0}_{0}\gamma\)		Only energy is lost
Pre-Q Basic	β⁻-decay: what happens in the nucleus?	A neutron becomes a proton + electron	Not a shell electron
Pre-Q Basic	When is γ usually emitted?	Straight after α or β decay (excited nucleus)	
Pre-Q Cloze	Balancing nuclear equations: total {{c1::A (top)}} and total {{c2::Z (bottom)}} equal on both sides		
Pre-Q Cloze	Background sources: {{c1::radon gas}}, {{c2::rocks and buildings}}, {{c3::food and drink}}, {{c4::cosmic rays}}		Largest is a gas
Pre-Q Basic	Detect radiation with?	Geiger–Müller tube + counter	
Pre-Q Basic	How to identify an emission type?	Add absorbers in turn (paper → aluminium → lead); see which stops it	
Pre-Q Cloze	Fraction left after n half-lives: {{c1::\((\tfrac12)^n\)}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Find half-life from a decay curve?	Halve a reading; read off time taken	Repeat and average
Pre-Q Basic	Find half-life from count data with background?	Subtract background, then count halvings	Commonest error
Pre-Q Basic	Uncorrected decay curve levels off at?	The background count	
Pre-Q Basic	Isotope choice for a use depends on?	Emission type and half-life	
Pre-Q Basic	Why α in smoke alarms?	Ionises air gap; smoke absorbs α, current drops	Long half-life
Pre-Q Basic	Why β for paper thickness control?	Partly passes thin sheets; count varies with thickness	α stopped, γ unaffected
Pre-Q Basic	Why γ for sterilising?	Penetrates sealed packaging, kills bacteria	
Pre-Q Basic	Medical tracer: emission and half-life?	γ, short half-life	Must escape the body
Pre-Q Basic	Does irradiated food become radioactive?	No	
Pre-Q Cloze	Ionising radiation on cells: {{c1::cell death}} or {{c2::mutation (cancer)}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Why is α most dangerous inside the body?	All its ionisation hits nearby cells	
Pre-Q Cloze	Radiation safety: reduce {{c1::time}}, increase {{c2::distance}}, use {{c3::shielding}}		3 principles
Pre-Q Cloze	Fission: \({}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} + {}^{235}_{\ 92}\mathrm{U} \rightarrow\) {{c1::two smaller nuclei}} + {{c2::2–3 neutrons}} + energy		
Pre-Q Cloze	Fusion: \({}^{2}_{1}\mathrm{H} + {}^{3}_{1}\mathrm{H} \rightarrow {{c1::{}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He} }} + {}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}\) + energy		
Pre-Q Basic	Why does fusion need extreme temperature and pressure?	To overcome repulsion of positive nuclei	
Pre-Q Basic	Energy in fission and fusion comes from?	Mass lost, converted to energy	
Pre-Q Basic	Fission vs fusion: where used?	Fission: power stations. Fusion: the Sun and stars	Not the other way round
Pre-Q Basic	Fission vs fusion: waste	Fission: long-lived radioactive waste. Fusion: mainly helium
