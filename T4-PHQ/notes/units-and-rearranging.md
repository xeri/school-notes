# Units, symbols and rearranging

A correct equation still loses marks if a unit is not converted, a symbol is misread, or the rearranging goes wrong. This page covers all three. Choosing the equation: [Which formula when](../notes/formula-chooser.md). Full equation list: [FORMULAS.md](../FORMULAS.md).

## Symbols and units

**Clash** marks a symbol (or letter) with more than one meaning. Decide from the context and the unit.

| $t$ | time | s | capital $T$ is period or temperature |

Units that lose marks: moments in **N m** (never J); momentum and impulse in **kg m/s** or **N s** (never N/s); pressure in **Pa** = N/m²; specific heat capacity in **J/(kg K)**; spring constant in **N/m**; acceleration in **m/s²**.

## Conversions

Convert **before** substituting.

| From | To | Do | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| km/h | m/s | ÷ 3.6 | 72 km/h = 20 m/s |
| m/s | km/h | × 3.6 | 15 m/s = 54 km/h |
| g | kg | ÷ 1000 | 250 g = 0.250 kg |
| cm | m | ÷ 100 | 45 cm = 0.45 m |
| mm | m | ÷ 1000 | 2.5 mm = 0.0025 m |
| cm² | m² | × $10^{-4}$ | 20 cm² = $2.0\times10^{-3}$ m² |
| mm² | m² | × $10^{-6}$ | |
| cm³ | m³ | × $10^{-6}$ | 500 cm³ = $5.0\times10^{-4}$ m³ |
| g/cm³ | kg/m³ | × 1000 | 2.7 g/cm³ = 2700 kg/m³ |
| min | s | × 60 | 5.0 min = 300 s |
| h | s | × 3600 | 2.0 h = 7200 s |
| day | s | × 86 400 | |
| mA | A | × $10^{-3}$ | 35 mA = 0.035 A |
| μA | A | × $10^{-6}$ | |
| kΩ, MΩ | Ω | × $10^3$, × $10^6$ | 4.7 kΩ = 4700 Ω |
| kW, MW, GW | W | × $10^3$, $10^6$, $10^9$ | 2.4 MW = $2.4\times10^6$ W |
| kJ, MJ | J | × $10^3$, $10^6$ | |
| W | kW (for kWh) | ÷ 1000 | 1500 W = 1.5 kW |
| kWh | J | × $3.6\times10^6$ | 2.0 kWh = $7.2\times10^6$ J |
| kPa | Pa | × $10^3$ | |
| N/cm² | Pa | × $10^4$ | |
| nm | m | × $10^{-9}$ | 450 nm = $4.5\times10^{-7}$ m |
| kHz, MHz | Hz | × $10^3$, × $10^6$ | |
| °C | K | + 273 | 27 °C = 300 K |
| a temperature **change** | | no conversion | a rise of 20 °C = a rise of 20 K |
| light-year | m | × $9.5\times10^{15}$ | |

Gas laws are the exception: $p$ and $V$ can stay in any unit, as long as both sides use the same one. $T$ must be in kelvin.

## Rearranging

**The rule:** do the same thing to both sides, and undo the operations in reverse order.

| Situation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Triangle method | Works only for three-quantity equations $a = bc$ or $a = b/c$ ($V = IR$, $F = ma$, $\rho = m/V$, $P = IV$, $v = f\lambda$, $p = F/A$, $Q = It$). It fails for sums, squares, halves, sines and reciprocals. Always write the equation itself in the answer, not the triangle. | |
| Subject in the denominator | Multiply both sides by it first, then divide. | $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V} \Rightarrow \rho V = m \Rightarrow V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}$ |
| Sum or difference | Move the other term across first, then divide. | $v = u + at \Rightarrow v - u = at \Rightarrow a = \dfrac{v-u}{t}$ |
| Square | Isolate the squared term, then square-root at the end. | $E_k = \tfrac12mv^2 \Rightarrow v^2 = \dfrac{2E_k}{m} \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2E_k}{m}}$ |
| Sine | Isolate the sine, then use $\sin^{-1}$. | $n = \dfrac{1}{\sin c} \Rightarrow \sin c = \dfrac{1}{n} \Rightarrow c = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)$ |
| Ratio | Cross-multiply. | $\dfrac{V_p}{V_s} = \dfrac{N_p}{N_s} \Rightarrow N_s = \dfrac{N_pV_s}{V_p}$ |
| Parallel resistors | Add the reciprocals: that sum is $1/R_T$. Then press $x^{-1}$ to get $R_T$. Check $R_T$ is smaller than the smallest resistor. | $\dfrac{1}{R_T} = \dfrac{1}{6.0} + \dfrac{1}{3.0} = 0.50 \Rightarrow R_T = 2.0$ Ω |

### Practice: 10 drills

Cover the answers below and rearrange each one.

| # | Equation | Make the subject |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $v = u + at$ | $t$ |
| 2 | $E_k = \tfrac12mv^2$ | $v$ |
| 3 | $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$ | $V$ |
| 4 | $Q = mc\Delta T$ | $\Delta T$ |
| 5 | $\Delta p = \rho g\Delta h$ | $\Delta h$ |
| 6 | $\dfrac{V_p}{V_s} = \dfrac{N_p}{N_s}$ | $N_s$ |
| 7 | $\dfrac{p_1V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{p_2V_2}{T_2}$ | $T_2$ |
| 8 | $\dfrac{1}{R_T} = \dfrac{1}{R_1} + \dfrac{1}{R_2}$, with $R_T = 2.0$ Ω and $R_1 = 3.0$ Ω | $R_2$ (a number) |
| 9 | $n = \dfrac{1}{\sin c}$ | $c$ |
| 10 | $s = ut + \tfrac12at^2$, with $u = 0$ | $t$ |

**Answers**

| # | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | $t = \dfrac{v - u}{a}$ |
| 2 | $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2E_k}{m}}$ |
| 3 | $V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}$ |
| 4 | $\Delta T = \dfrac{Q}{mc}$ |
| 5 | $\Delta h = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\rho g}$ |
| 6 | $N_s = \dfrac{N_pV_s}{V_p}$ |
| 7 | $T_2 = \dfrac{p_2V_2T_1}{p_1V_1}$ |
| 8 | $\dfrac{1}{R_2} = \dfrac{1}{2.0} - \dfrac{1}{3.0} = \dfrac{1}{6.0}$, so $R_2 = 6.0$ Ω |
| 9 | $c = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{1}{n}\right)$ |
| 10 | $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2s}{a}}$ |

## Answer format and significant figures

| Step | Write |
|---|---|
| 1. Equation | in symbols, as learnt (e.g. $Q = mc\Delta T$) |
| 2. Rearranged equation | if needed (e.g. $c = \dfrac{Q}{m\Delta T}$) |
| 3. Substitution | numbers in SI units (e.g. $c = \dfrac{14\,400}{1.0 \times 16}$) |
| 4. Answer | value + unit + s.f. (e.g. $c = 900$ J/(kg K)) |

**Significant figures.**
- Give the answer to the same number of s.f. as the **least precise** value in the data, usually 2 or 3. Never 1, and never the full calculator display.
- Counting: leading zeros do not count (0.050 has 2 s.f.); zeros after a decimal point do count (2.0 has 2 s.f.).
- Keep the full calculator value between steps. Round only the final answer.
- Use standard form for very large or very small answers: $4.5\times10^{17}$ s, not 450 000 000 000 000 000 s.
- In practical tables, readings go to the instrument's resolution, with the same number of decimal places down a column. Calculated columns go to the same s.f. as the data.

## Top 15 traps

| # | Trap | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | No equation written | Write the equation in symbols before any number. No equation = no marks, even with the right answer. |
| 2 | Not converting before substituting | Convert in the givens list: km/h, g, cm, cm², min, mA, kW. |
| 3 | °C in a gas law | Add 273 to every temperature first. |
| 4 | $mc\Delta T$ during a change of state | Temperature constant while melting or boiling → use $Q = ml$. |
| 5 | Forgetting $2d$ in echoes | The sound goes there and back: $v = 2d/t$, or halve the time. |
| 6 | Total force instead of resultant in $F = ma$ | Resultant = forward forces − backward forces. |
| 7 | Not inverting for parallel resistors | $1/R_T$ is not $R_T$. Press $x^{-1}$. $R_T$ must be below the smallest resistor. |
| 8 | Wrong distance in moments | Use the **perpendicular** distance from the **pivot** to the line of action, not from the end of the beam or along a slanting bar. |
| 9 | Efficiency as a decimal vs a % | 0.25 = 25%. Never above 100%. Use the decimal when rearranging (input = output ÷ 0.25). |
| 10 | Rounding mid-calculation | Keep the calculator value; round only at the end. |
| 11 | Wrong significant figures | Match the least precise data value, usually 2 or 3 s.f. |
| 12 | Mixing symbol clashes | $W$ weight or work; $c$ light, heat capacity or critical angle; $\rho$ density or resistivity; $p$ momentum or pressure. |
| 13 | Forgetting the background count | Subtract the background from **every** reading before halving. |
| 14 | kWh vs J | Cost uses kW × hours. Joules use W × seconds. 1 kWh = $3.6\times10^6$ J. |
| 15 | Sign of a deceleration | $a = (v - u)/t$ comes out negative. Write "$a = -5.0$ m/s²" or "deceleration of 5.0 m/s²", not "acceleration of 5.0 m/s²". |
