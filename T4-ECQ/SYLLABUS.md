## 1. Scarcity, choice and economic systems

- The basic economic problem: unlimited wants against scarce resources; needs and wants.
- Opportunity cost, and applying it to consumers, producers and governments.
- The factors of production: land, labour, capital, enterprise — and their rewards.
- The production possibility curve: efficient and inefficient points, unattainable points, movements along the curve and shifts of the curve; increasing opportunity cost.
- Economic systems: market, planned and mixed economies; how each answers what, how and for whom to produce.
- The role of the price mechanism in allocating resources: signalling, incentive and rationing functions.

## 2. Demand, supply and the market

- Demand: the law of demand, individual and market demand, the demand curve.
- The **conditions of demand** (what shifts the curve): income, tastes and fashion, the price of substitutes and complements, population, advertising, expectations.
- Normal, inferior, substitute and complementary goods.
- Supply: the law of supply, the supply curve.
- The **conditions of supply** (what shifts the curve): costs of production, technology, taxes and subsidies, the number of producers, weather, the price of related goods.
- The distinction between a **movement along** a curve (caused by the good's own price) and a **shift** of a curve (caused by anything else).
- Market equilibrium: equilibrium price and quantity; excess demand and excess supply; how price adjusts to clear the market.
- The effects of a shift in demand or supply on equilibrium price and quantity; the ambiguity when both shift together.
- Consumer and producer pricing strategies; maximum and minimum price controls and their consequences.

## 3. Elasticity, market failure and market structures

- Price elasticity of demand: calculation, interpretation, elastic and inelastic demand, the determinants of PED, and the effect of a price change on total revenue.
- Price elasticity of supply: calculation, the determinants (spare capacity, time, stock levels), elastic and inelastic supply.
- The usefulness of elasticity to firms and to government (especially indirect taxation).
- Market failure: the meaning of an efficient allocation and the ways markets fail.
- External costs and external benefits; private, external and social costs and benefits.
- Merit and demerit goods; public goods and the free-rider problem; information failure.
- Government intervention to correct market failure: indirect taxes, subsidies, regulation, provision, and information campaigns — with the limits of each.
- Market structures: competition, oligopoly and monopoly; barriers to entry; the effects of market power on price, choice, quality and innovation.

## 4. Firms, costs and labour markets

- Production and productivity; the distinction between the two; division of labour and specialisation.
- Capital-intensive and labour-intensive production.
- Types and sizes of business: sole trader, partnership, private and public limited company, franchise, co-operative; the role of the stock exchange.
- Measuring business size, and the reasons for and against the growth of firms.
- Economies and diseconomies of scale: internal and external.
- Costs: fixed, variable, total and average cost; total and average revenue; profit.
- Business aims and objectives: profit maximisation, survival, growth, social objectives.
- The demand for labour as a **derived** demand; the supply of labour and its determinants.
- Wage determination; the causes of wage differentials: skill, qualification, scarcity, danger, bargaining power, discrimination.
- Trade unions and collective bargaining; the effects on wages and employment.

## 5. Macroeconomics and policy

- The functions of money: medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value, standard of deferred payment; the characteristics of good money.
- The role of banks and the central bank; the money market; interest rates.
- Government macroeconomic objectives: growth, full employment, price stability, balance of payments stability, redistribution of income.
- GDP, real GDP, and real GDP per head; **nominal vs real** and why the distinction matters; the limitations of GDP as a measure of welfare.
- Economic growth: its causes, benefits and costs; the difference between growth and development.
- The economic cycle: boom, recession, slump, recovery, and the effect on firms and households.
- Inflation: definition, measurement by a price index, demand-pull and cost-push causes, and the consequences for different groups. Deflation.
- Employment and unemployment: how unemployment is measured; cyclical, structural, frictional and seasonal unemployment; the consequences.
- Fiscal policy: government revenue (direct and indirect taxation, the principles of a good tax, progressive, proportional and regressive taxes), government spending, budget deficit and surplus.
- Monetary policy: interest rates and the money supply; how changes transmit to spending, investment and the exchange rate.
- Supply-side policy: education and training, infrastructure, deregulation, tax incentives.
- Conflicts between policy objectives, and the significance of the economy's starting position.

## 6. International trade and exchange rates

- Specialisation and the gains from trade; absolute advantage; why countries trade.
- The benefits and risks of international trade, including dependence on overseas markets and the adjustment costs borne by particular workers and regions.
- Free trade and protectionism: tariffs, quotas, subsidies and administrative barriers; the arguments for protection (infant industry, employment, dumping, strategic industries) and the counter-arguments, including retaliation.
- Exchange rates: how they are determined; appreciation and depreciation; floating and fixed systems.
- The effect of exchange-rate changes on export and import prices, on firms using imported inputs, and on the trade balance — and why the effect is not automatic.
- The balance of payments: the current account and its components; the causes and consequences of a deficit or surplus, and the policies to correct one.

## 7. Development, population and poverty

- The meaning of economic development and its difference from economic growth.
- Indicators of development: income per head, and the health, education and living-condition measures that income alone conceals; composite indicators and their limitations.
- Comparing countries at different stages of development; the MEDC/LEDC terminology used in the teaching material and its limits.
- Differences in development **within** a country as well as between countries.
- Population: birth rate, death rate, net migration, natural increase; the causes of population change.
- Population structure and the dependency ratio; the consequences of an ageing population and of a young population; over- and under-population.
- Poverty: absolute and relative poverty; the causes of poverty.
- Policies to reduce poverty and promote development: education, healthcare, infrastructure, investment, aid, trade and targeted support — assessed on access, quality, implementation capacity and the time before benefits arrive.
