Pre-Q Basic	Econ: indirect tax on a demerit good — which curve shifts, and which way?	Supply shifts left (up): the tax raises the producer's costs	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ: indirect tax chain — after supply shifts left?	Price rises (by less than the tax) → quantity demanded contracts → less of the external cost	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ: why does a tax on cigarettes cut smoking only a little?	Demand is price inelastic (addictive), so quantity falls by a smaller % than price	Think PED
Pre-Q Basic	Econ: indirect tax — two 'fails because' points besides inelastic demand	Regressive (hits the poor hardest); encourages black markets and smuggling	
Pre-Q Cloze	Econ: an ideal externality tax equals the {{c1::external cost per unit}}, shifting supply from {{c2::MPC to MSC}}	Output then falls to the social optimum Q*	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ: subsidy for public transport — chain	Producer costs fall → supply shifts right → fares fall → more passengers → less congestion and pollution	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ: subsidy — two 'fails because' points	Opportunity cost of government spending; firms may rely on it and become inefficient	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ: why may a subsidy on a good with inelastic demand not raise consumption much?	Most of the subsidy becomes extra producer revenue, not a lower price and more sales	
Pre-Q Basic	Maximum price: where is it set, and what does it create?	Below equilibrium → shortage (excess demand, Qd > Qs)	
Pre-Q Basic	Maximum price: three side effects	Queues and waiting lists; black markets above the ceiling; lower quality and less investment	
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum price: where is it set, and what does it create?	Above equilibrium → surplus (excess supply, Qs > Qd)	
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum wage: chain to unemployment	Wage above equilibrium → firms hire fewer (demand contracts) → more want work (supply extends) → excess supply of labour	
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum wage: when are job losses small?	Demand for labour is inelastic, the wage is only slightly above equilibrium, or productivity rises	
Pre-Q Basic	Regulation (minimum buying age): which curve shifts?	Demand shifts left — fewer people willing and able to buy at each price	Who can buy, not cost
Pre-Q Basic	Regulation: three 'fails because' points (school slides)	Black markets; people break rules; enforcement is expensive and slow	
Pre-Q Basic	Regulation: one advantage over a tax	It works directly, regardless of price elasticity of demand	
Pre-Q Basic	Tradable permits: how do they cut pollution at lowest cost?	Cap total pollution; firms that can cut cheaply sell spare permits to those that cannot	NZ: Emissions Trading Scheme
Pre-Q Basic	Direct (state) provision: two 'fails because' points	Free riders; free supply causes excess demand (waiting lists) — plus opportunity cost	
Pre-Q Basic	Education and advertising campaigns: which curve shifts?	Demand — left for a demerit good, right for a merit good (corrects information failure)	
Pre-Q Basic	Public good: which policy, and why?	Direct provision funded by tax — free riders mean no firm can charge, so the market supplies none	
Pre-Q Basic	Monopoly: three policy tools	Competition policy (Commerce Commission); price regulation; nationalisation or removing barriers to entry	
Pre-Q Basic	Privatisation: main risk	A public monopoly becomes a private monopoly that raises prices; unprofitable services are cut	
Pre-Q Basic	Government failure?	Government intervention that makes resource allocation worse — it costs more than the market failure it fixes	
Pre-Q Basic	Government failure: four causes	Imperfect information; unintended consequences; political self-interest and short-termism; enforcement costs	Also time lags, dependency
Pre-Q Basic	Government failure: example from a maximum price	Rent controls cause housing shortages, black-market rents and poor maintenance	
Pre-Q Basic	Expansionary fiscal policy: government spending chain	More G → firms hire → incomes rise → more spending (multiplier) → AD right → growth, fewer unemployed	
Pre-Q Basic	Expansionary fiscal policy: three side effects	Demand-pull inflation near capacity; more imports (current account worse); budget deficit and debt	
Pre-Q Basic	Company tax cut: effect on AD and AS	More retained profit → more investment → AD right now, AS right later	
Pre-Q Basic	Higher OCR: chain to inflation	Bank rates rise → borrowing dearer, saving more attractive → C and I fall → AD left → demand-pull inflation falls	
Pre-Q Basic	Higher OCR: exchange-rate route	Foreign savings flow in → demand for NZ$ rises → NZ$ appreciates → cheaper imports → lower imported inflation	
Pre-Q Basic	Higher OCR: why does it take a year or more to work in NZ?	Many mortgages are fixed for 1–2 years, so they re-price only when the term ends	
Pre-Q Basic	Higher OCR: why is it weak against cost-push inflation?	It cuts demand, but cost-push inflation comes from rising costs (oil, imports, drought)	
Pre-Q Basic	Quantitative easing: chain	Central bank creates money to buy bonds from banks → banks lend more → C and I rise → AD right	
Pre-Q Basic	Supply-side policy: common end of every chain	AS shifts right → more output at a lower price level (growth with lower inflation)	
Pre-Q Basic	Education and training (supply-side): chain and main weakness	Skills → higher labour productivity → AS right; slow (years) and costly	
Pre-Q Basic	Deregulation: chain and main risk	Lower compliance costs → more firms and investment → AS right; weaker protection may raise external costs	
Pre-Q Basic	AD/AS: what shifts AD right?	Higher C, I, G or (X − M): lower interest rates, tax cuts, more government spending, confidence, depreciation	
Pre-Q Basic	AD/AS: what shifts AS left?	Cost shocks — higher oil or import prices, wages above productivity, indirect taxes, disasters	
Pre-Q Basic	AD/AS: effect of AS shifting right on output and the price level	Output rises and the price level falls	
Pre-Q Basic	AD/AS diagram: axis labels	Vertical: price level; horizontal: real GDP/output	
Pre-Q Basic	Spare capacity: why does it matter for a rise in AD?	With spare capacity AD mainly raises output; at full capacity it mainly raises prices	
Pre-Q Basic	Size of the multiplier: why is it smaller in NZ?	Much extra income is spent on imports, so it leaks abroad instead of being re-spent at home	Extension
Pre-Q Basic	Depreciation of the NZ$: chain to the current account	Exports cheaper abroad, imports dearer at home → more exports, fewer imports → current account improves (if elastic)	
Pre-Q Basic	Depreciation: three reasons it may not help	Inelastic demand for imports and exports; time lags (fixed contracts); no spare capacity	Plus imported inflation
Pre-Q Basic	Tariff: effect on domestic producers, consumers and government	Producers sell more at a higher price; consumers pay more and buy less; government gains revenue	
Pre-Q Basic	Tariff: biggest risk for a small trading economy like NZ	Retaliation — partners tax NZ exports, so export jobs are lost	
Pre-Q Basic	Quota vs tariff: key difference	A quota limits quantity and raises no government revenue	
Pre-Q Basic	Conflict: low unemployment vs price stability	Higher incomes → more demand (demand-pull); less spare labour → wage demands → wage–price spiral	
Pre-Q Basic	Conflict: growth vs balance of payments	Rising incomes → more spending on imports → current account deficit widens	
Pre-Q Basic	Conflict: growth vs the environment	More output and consumption → more resources and waste → external costs (pollution, habitat loss)	
Pre-Q Basic	Conflict: growth vs price stability	Demand grows faster than resources → capacity limits → higher prices instead of more output	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ discuss [8]: what lifts an answer to Level 3 (6–8)?	Both sides developed, plus a judgement naming what the answer depends on	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ analyse [6]: penalty for listing points?	A list of unexplained points is capped at 3 marks	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ evaluation: five 'it depends' factors	PED; size of the change; time lags; spare capacity; cause of the problem	Also cost, confidence, enforcement, who gains
Pre-Q Cloze	Econ exam: {{c1::110}} marks in two papers — Paper 1 MCQ {{c2::30}}, Paper 2 data response {{c3::20}} and structured {{c4::3 × 20}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Econ Paper 1: how many questions, how long, and what then?	30 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes, then the grid is handed in	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ Paper 2: length and sections?	2 h 15 min: Section B data response (compulsory, 20) + Section C three of six structured (20 each)	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ Paper 1: time per question?	1.5 minutes; spare time cannot be moved into Paper 2	45 ÷ 30
Pre-Q Basic	Econ Paper 2: time per 20-mark question?	About 32 minutes, after 5 minutes choosing the Section C questions	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ Section C: how to choose 3 of the 6?	On the (c) analyse and (d) discuss parts — usually 14 of the 20 marks	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ answer booklet: two presentation rules?	Number each question; label its parts (a), (b), (c)…	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ MCQ grid: the slip that costs a run of marks?	Skipping a question but not its row, so every later answer is in the wrong row	
Pre-Q Basic	Econ MCQ: first move, before reading the options?	Answer from the stem, then find your answer among the options	Stops a distractor pulling you in
Pre-Q Basic	Econ MCQ: words to circle in the stem	NOT, LEAST, MOST LIKELY, ALWAYS — and shift or movement, nominal or real	
Pre-Q Basic	Opportunity cost when several options are rejected?	Only the next best one, not the sum of everything rejected	
Pre-Q Basic	Maximum price below equilibrium: quantity actually traded?	Qs, the smaller amount; the shortage is Qd − Qs	Sellers decide how much is sold
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum price above equilibrium: quantity actually traded?	Qd, unless the government buys the surplus	
Pre-Q Basic	Maximum price set above equilibrium: effect?	None — the market price already obeys it	A ceiling bites only below equilibrium
Pre-Q Basic	Indirect tax: does the price rise by the whole tax?	No — by less; producers bear the rest	
Pre-Q Basic	Percentage point change vs percentage change	Points: new rate − old rate. Per cent: (new − old) ÷ old × 100	
Pre-Q Basic	CPI index points vs the inflation rate	Inflation = point change ÷ old index × 100; the two match only from 100	
Pre-Q Basic	Progressive, proportional or regressive: what is the test?	The proportion of income taken in tax, never the dollar amount	
Pre-Q Basic	Output and workforce rise by the same %: labour productivity?	Unchanged — production rose, output per worker did not	
Pre-Q Basic	Nominal GDP rises at the same rate as prices: real GDP?	Unchanged — the whole rise is inflation	
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum wage: how to find the unemployment it creates?	Labour supplied − labour demanded at the minimum wage (not the fall in employment)	
Pre-Q Basic	Unemployment rate: three wrong denominators	Population, working-age population, number employed — divide by the labour force	
Pre-Q Basic	New motorway cuts a firm's delivery costs: which economy of scale?	External (shared infrastructure), not internal	
Pre-Q Basic	Country worse at producing everything: can it gain from trade?	Yes — it still has a comparative advantage where its opportunity cost is lower	
Pre-Q Basic	Dividends paid to foreign shareholders: which part of the current account?	Primary income (a debit)	
Pre-Q Basic	Foreign tourists' spending in NZ: which part of the current account?	Services — an invisible export	
Pre-Q Basic	NZ price of an import rises, its foreign price unchanged: NZ$?	Has depreciated
