Pre-Q Basic	Price elasticity of demand (PED)?	Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price	
Pre-Q Cloze	PED = {{c1::% change in quantity demanded}} ÷ {{c2::% change in price}}		Commonest slip: upside down
Pre-Q Cloze	% change = ({{c1::new − original}}) ÷ {{c2::original}} × 100		Divide by which value?
Pre-Q Basic	Elastic demand?	PED > 1: Qd changes by a bigger % than price	
Pre-Q Basic	Inelastic demand?	PED < 1: Qd changes by a smaller % than price	
Pre-Q Basic	Unitary elasticity?	PED = 1: same % change; spending unchanged	
Pre-Q Basic	Perfectly inelastic vs perfectly elastic demand	PED = 0 (vertical) vs PED = ∞ (horizontal)	
Pre-Q Basic	How to calculate PED from two prices and quantities?	Find each % change from the original; divide Qd change by price change	Ignore the minus sign
Pre-Q Cloze	Total revenue = {{c1::price}} × {{c2::quantity sold}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Elastic demand: price rise effect on revenue?	Revenue falls	
Pre-Q Basic	Inelastic demand: price rise effect on revenue?	Revenue rises	
Pre-Q Basic	How to show PED and revenue on a diagram?	Same price change on steep and flat curves; compare revenue areas	
Pre-Q Cloze	Determinants of PED: {{c1::substitutes}}, {{c2::necessity}}, {{c3::addiction or habit}}, {{c4::share of income}}, {{c5::switching cost}}, {{c6::brand loyalty}}, {{c7::time}}		7 items
Pre-Q Basic	Price elasticity of supply (PES)?	Responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price	
Pre-Q Cloze	PES = {{c1::% change in quantity supplied}} ÷ {{c2::% change in price}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Inelastic vs perfectly inelastic supply	PES < 1: smaller change. PES = 0: no change	
Pre-Q Cloze	Straight supply line from the {{c1::price}} axis is elastic; from the {{c2::quantity}} axis inelastic; through the {{c3::origin}} PES = 1		Slope doesn't matter
Pre-Q Cloze	Determinants of PES: {{c1::time}}, {{c2::spare capacity}}, {{c3::stocks}}, {{c4::production period}}, {{c5::factor mobility}}, {{c6::barriers to entry}}		6 items
Pre-Q Basic	Indirect tax raises most revenue when demand is?	Inelastic	
Pre-Q Basic	Indirect tax cuts consumption most when demand is?	Elastic	
Pre-Q Cloze	Income elasticity of demand (YED) = {{c1::% change in quantity demanded}} ÷ {{c2::% change in income}}	Negative = inferior; above 1 = luxury	Sign matters
Pre-Q Basic	Market failure?	Market misallocates resources, causing a loss of welfare	
Pre-Q Basic	Complete vs partial market failure	Complete: nothing supplied. Partial: wrong quantity or price	
Pre-Q Cloze	Causes of market failure: {{c1::public goods}}, {{c2::information failure}}, {{c3::imperfect market structures}}, {{c4::factor immobility}}, {{c5::externalities}}, {{c6::inequity}}		6 items
Pre-Q Basic	Information failure?	Consumers misjudge the costs or benefits of a good	
Pre-Q Basic	Free-rider problem?	Non-payers still benefit, so the market provides none	
Pre-Q Basic	Externality?	Cost or benefit falling on a third party	
Pre-Q Basic	External cost?	Harm to third parties the decision maker doesn't pay	
Pre-Q Cloze	Social cost = {{c1::private cost}} + {{c2::external cost}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	Social benefit = {{c1::private benefit}} + {{c2::external benefit}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Why do external costs cause over-production?	Only private costs counted; social cost is higher	
Pre-Q Basic	Indirect tax?	Tax on spending, collected from the producer	
Pre-Q Basic	Specific vs ad valorem tax	Specific: fixed amount per unit. Ad valorem: % of price	
Pre-Q Basic	Indirect tax: diagram effect?	Supply shifts left (up); price rises, quantity falls	Producer's cost
Pre-Q Basic	Tax incidence?	Who actually bears the tax	
Pre-Q Basic	Inelastic demand: who bears most of a tax?	Consumers	
Pre-Q Cloze	Per-unit tax: consumers' share = {{c1::new price − old price}}; tax revenue = tax per unit × {{c2::new quantity}}		Not Q1
Pre-Q Basic	Subsidy?	Government payment to producers that lowers their costs	
Pre-Q Basic	Subsidy: diagram effect?	Supply shifts right (down); price falls, quantity rises	
Pre-Q Cloze	Cost of a subsidy to government = {{c1::subsidy per unit}} × {{c2::new quantity}}		
Pre-Q Basic	Maximum price (price ceiling)?	Legal upper limit set below equilibrium	
Pre-Q Basic	Maximum price result?	Shortage: queues and black markets	
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum price (price floor)?	Legal lower limit set above equilibrium	
Pre-Q Basic	Minimum price result?	Surplus; in a labour market, unemployment	
Pre-Q Basic	Regulation such as a minimum buying age: diagram?	Demand shifts left	Changes who can buy
Pre-Q Basic	Tradable permits?	Pollution cap; firms buy and sell allowances	
Pre-Q Basic	Tax on a demerit good: 2 weaknesses	Inelastic demand means little effect; regressive	
Pre-Q Basic	Privatisation?	Transfer of a firm from public to private sector	
Pre-Q Basic	Nationalisation?	State takes ownership of a private firm	
Pre-Q Basic	Price vs non-price competition	Competing on price vs on quality, location or service	
Pre-Q Cloze	Perfect competition: {{c1::many small firms}}, {{c2::identical products}}, {{c3::no barriers to entry}}, {{c4::price takers}}		4 features
Pre-Q Basic	Monopolistic competition?	Many smaller firms selling differentiated products	
Pre-Q Basic	Oligopoly?	A few large firms with strong barriers to entry	
Pre-Q Basic	Monopoly?	One seller of a product with no close substitute	
Pre-Q Basic	Monopsony?	A market with only one buyer	
Pre-Q Basic	Barriers to entry?	Factors that stop or impede new firms entering	
Pre-Q Cloze	Natural barriers to entry: {{c1::economies of scale}}, {{c2::capital size}}, {{c3::history and brand loyalty}}, {{c4::legal requirements}}		4 items
Pre-Q Cloze	Artificial barriers to entry: {{c1::restricting supplies}}, {{c2::predatory pricing}}, {{c3::exclusive dealing}}, {{c4::full-line forcing}}		4 items
Pre-Q Cloze	Monopoly benefits: {{c1::economies of scale}}, {{c2::no wasteful duplication}}, {{c3::profits fund innovation}}		
Pre-Q Cloze	Monopoly harms: {{c1::restricted supply and higher prices}}, {{c2::less choice}}, {{c3::less innovation}}, {{c4::X-inefficiency}}		4 items
Pre-Q Basic	Duopoly?	Market with only two firms	
Pre-Q Basic	Price maker?	Firm with power to set its own price	Opposite of price taker
Pre-Q Basic	Pure monopoly vs dominant firm?	Only one seller vs one firm with monopoly power among others	
Pre-Q Basic	Complete market failure?	The market does not supply the product at all	Public goods
Pre-Q Basic	Direct provision (government)?	State supplies a good itself, funded by tax	Schools, parks, roads
