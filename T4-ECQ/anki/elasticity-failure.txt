#deck:Economics::3 Elasticity, market failure and market structures
Formula for price elasticity of demand	\(\text{PED} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}\)
Why is PED normally negative?	Price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions
PED when price rises from 10 to 12 and quantity falls from 100 to 70	\(-30/20 = -1.5\), magnitude 1.5
PED magnitude greater than 1	Elastic — quantity responds more than proportionately
PED magnitude less than 1	Inelastic — quantity responds less than proportionately
PED equal to 1	Unit elastic — a proportionate response
PED equal to 0	Perfectly inelastic — quantity does not change at all
PED equal to infinity	Perfectly elastic — any price rise kills demand entirely
Effect of a price rise on revenue when demand is elastic	Revenue falls
Effect of a price fall on revenue when demand is elastic	Revenue rises
Effect of a price rise on revenue when demand is inelastic	Revenue rises
Effect of a price fall on revenue when demand is inelastic	Revenue falls
Is a change in revenue the same as a change in profit?	No — profit also depends on costs, and selling fewer units cuts total variable cost
Strongest determinant of PED	The availability of close substitutes
Effect of necessity on PED	Necessities have inelastic demand; luxuries are elastic
Effect of the proportion of income spent on PED	A cheap item is inelastic; an expensive one is elastic
Effect of time on PED	Demand is more elastic in the long run, as consumers find alternatives
Effect of habit or addiction on PED	Demand is inelastic
Effect of brand loyalty on PED	Demand is inelastic
Formula for price elasticity of supply	\(\text{PES} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_s}{\%\Delta P}\)
Sign of PES	Positive
Name five determinants of PES	Spare capacity, time, stock levels, mobility of factors, length of the production period
Effect of spare capacity on PES	Plenty of spare capacity makes supply elastic
Effect of time on PES	Supply is inelastic in the short run and elastic in the long run
PES of agriculture in the short run	Highly inelastic, because a crop takes a season to grow
Why elasticity matters to a firm	It shows whether a price change raises or lowers revenue, and whether a cost rise can be passed on
When does an indirect tax raise the most revenue?	When demand is inelastic
When does an indirect tax cut consumption the most?	When demand is elastic
The conflict in taxing a demerit good	Raising revenue needs inelastic demand, but cutting consumption needs elastic demand
Definition of market failure	Where the free market allocates resources inefficiently, producing too much or too little from society's point of view
Definition of private cost	The cost to the decision-maker
Definition of external cost	A cost falling on a third party, not paid by the decision-maker
Formula for social cost	Social cost = private cost + external cost
Formula for social benefit	Social benefit = private benefit + external benefit
Effect of an external cost on market output	The market over-produces, because the polluter does not pay for the harm
Effect of an external benefit on market output	The market under-produces, because the provider is not paid for the wider gain
Two examples of a negative externality	A factory's pollution harming neighbours, and traffic congestion
Two examples of a positive externality	Vaccination protecting the unvaccinated, and education raising economy-wide productivity
Definition of a merit good	A good with external benefits that consumers undervalue, so it is underconsumed
Definition of a demerit good	A good with external costs whose harm consumers undervalue, so it is overconsumed
Two properties of a public good	Non-rival and non-excludable
Three examples of a public good	Street lighting, national defence, flood defences
Definition of the free-rider problem	Non-excludability means nobody will pay voluntarily, so the market supplies none at all
Definition of information failure	Consumers cannot judge what they are buying, so they choose badly
Two limits of an indirect tax on a demerit good	Ineffective if demand is inelastic, and regressive on low incomes
Two limits of a subsidy	It costs public money with an opportunity cost, and producers may not pass it all on
Two limits of regulation	It needs monitoring and enforcement, and it is blunt, hitting compliant firms too
Limit of an information campaign	It is slow, and often ignored
How tradable permits work	A cap is set on total pollution and firms are free to trade their allowances
Number of firms in competition, oligopoly and monopoly	Many; a few large firms; one dominant firm
Price control in competition	None — firms are price takers
Barriers to entry in a monopoly	Very high
Main form of competition in an oligopoly	Non-price competition: heavy advertising and branding
Name five barriers to entry	Large capital requirements, existing economies of scale, patents, brand loyalty, legal protection
Three arguments against monopoly	Higher prices, less choice, less incentive to innovate or improve quality
Two arguments that monopoly can benefit consumers	Economies of scale can lower unit cost, and large profits can fund research and innovation
Definition of a natural monopoly	A market where a single supplier avoids wasteful duplication, such as water or rail track
