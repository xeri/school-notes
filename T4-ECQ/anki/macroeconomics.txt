#deck:Economics::5 Macroeconomics and policy
Four functions of money	Medium of exchange, measure or unit of account, store of value, standard of deferred payment
Five characteristics of good money	Acceptability, divisibility, portability, durability, scarcity
Four problems with barter	It needs a double coincidence of wants, there is no agreed measure of value, goods perish, and value is subjective
Definition of disposable income	The income left to spend or save after taxes and deductions
Effect of a rise in interest rates on spending, saving and borrowing	Spending falls, saving rises, borrowing falls
Role of a commercial bank	An intermediary that takes deposits, makes loans, and provides credit, payments and foreign exchange
Name five roles of the central bank	Issuing currency, holding government accounts, managing the national debt and reserves, regulating and lending to commercial banks, operating monetary policy
What is the interest rate?	The price of borrowing money and the reward for saving it
Name five government macroeconomic objectives	Economic growth, full employment, price stability, balance of payments stability, redistribution of income
Definition of GDP	The money value of all goods and services produced within a country in a period, usually a year
Formula for GDP by expenditure	\(\text{GDP} = C + I + G + (X - M)\)
Definition of economic growth	The annual percentage change in real GDP
Four causes of economic growth	Better factor endowments, a larger or more skilled labour force, higher labour productivity, investment in capital
Difference between nominal and real GDP	Nominal GDP is valued at current prices; real GDP has the effect of price changes removed
Formula for real GDP	\(\text{real GDP} = \frac{\text{nominal GDP}}{\text{price index}/100}\)
Why use real GDP per head?	It allows for both inflation and population change, so it tracks average output per person
Five limitations of GDP as a measure of welfare	It ignores unpaid work, income distribution, the composition of output, negative externalities, and leisure and health
Difference between economic growth and economic development	Growth is a rise in output; development is an improvement in living standards and opportunity
Four stages of the economic cycle	Growth, boom or peak, recession or downturn, recovery or upturn
What happens at the recession stage?	Growth slows or turns negative, demand and profits fall, investment is cut and redundancies raise unemployment
Definition of a recession	A sustained fall in real GDP
Definition of inflation	A sustained rise in the general price level
How is inflation measured?	By a consumer price index tracking a weighted basket of goods and services bought by a typical household
What is a weighting in a price index?	The share of household spending taken by that item, so food at 20 per cent of spending gets a 20 per cent weighting
Formula for a price index	\(\text{index} = \frac{\text{weighted average price this year}}{\text{weighted average price in the base year}}\times100\)
What does a price index of 120 mean?	Prices are 20 per cent higher than in the base year — not 120 per cent higher, and not 20 per cent inflation this year
Cause of demand-pull inflation	Total demand rising faster than firms can supply, so prices are bid up
Cause of cost-push inflation	Rising production costs that firms pass on in higher prices to protect margins
Effect of inflation on savers and borrowers	Savers lose as the real value of savings falls; borrowers gain as the real value of debt falls
Why does inflation hit fixed and low-income earners hardest?	They cannot negotiate rises and they spend most of their income
Definition of deflation	A sustained fall in the general price level
Difference between deflation and disinflation	Deflation is falling prices; disinflation is inflation slowing down, so prices still rise but more slowly
Definition of the labour force	Those of working age who are both willing and able to work
Formula for the unemployment rate	\(\text{unemployment rate} = \frac{\text{number unemployed}}{\text{labour force}}\times100\)
Four types of unemployment	Cyclical, structural, frictional, seasonal
Cause of cyclical unemployment	Low total demand for goods and services in a recession
Cause of structural unemployment	The long-term decline of an industry, leaving workers with the wrong skills or in the wrong place
Cause of frictional unemployment	The time spent between leaving one job and finding the next
Why can low unemployment be inflationary?	Vacancies are hard to fill, so firms bid wages up and pass the cost on in prices
Definition of fiscal policy	Varying government spending and taxation to influence total demand
Difference between direct and indirect tax	Direct tax is taken from income, profit or wealth; indirect tax is levied on spending
Four principles of a good tax	Equity, non-distortionary, certainty, convenience
Definition of a progressive tax	One where the proportion of income taken rises as income rises
Definition of a proportional tax	One that takes the same proportion of income at every income level
Definition of a regressive tax	One where the proportion of income taken falls as income rises
Why are indirect taxes regressive in effect?	Low-income households spend nearly all their income, so nearly all of it is taxed, while higher earners save part of theirs
Tax due on 60000 at 10 per cent to 20000, 20 per cent to 50000 and 30 per cent above	2000 + 6000 + 3000 = 11000
Definition of a budget deficit	Government spending exceeding government revenue
Definition of expansionary fiscal policy	Raising government spending or cutting taxes to boost total demand
Definition of contractionary fiscal policy	Cutting government spending or raising taxes to slow demand and reduce debt
Three problems with fiscal policy	The size and timing are hard to judge, borrowing leaves less finance for private firms, and higher taxes may blunt incentives
Definition of monetary policy	The central bank's management of interest rates and the money supply
Effect of expansionary monetary policy	A lower interest rate makes borrowing cheaper and saving less attractive, so consumption and investment rise
Effect of a rise in interest rates on the exchange rate	The currency tends to appreciate, as higher returns attract foreign savers
Definition of supply-side policy	Policy designed to raise the economy's productive capacity rather than its demand
Name five supply-side policies	Education and training, infrastructure, tax incentives for investment, deregulation, privatisation
Why do growth and price stability conflict?	If demand rises faster than capacity, the result is higher prices rather than more output
Why do full employment and price stability conflict?	Less spare labour means wages are bid up and feed through into prices
Why do growth and the balance of payments conflict?	Rising incomes raise spending on imports, worsening the current account
Why does the economy's starting position matter?	With spare capacity a demand boost mainly raises output; at full capacity it mainly raises prices
