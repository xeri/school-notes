#deck:Economics::1 Scarcity, choice and economic systems
Definition of scarcity	Limited resources against unlimited wants
The basic economic problem	Unlimited wants against scarce resources, which forces choice
Definition of a need	Something essential for survival, such as food, water or shelter
Definition of a want	Something desired but not essential for survival
Definition of opportunity cost	The next best alternative forgone
Is opportunity cost the sum of everything rejected?	No — only the single next best alternative forgone
Opportunity cost for a government that builds a hospital	The next best public project forgone, such as the school not built
Opportunity cost for a producer using a factory for product A	The output of product B forgone
Name the four factors of production	Land, labour, capital, enterprise
Reward to land	Rent
Reward to labour	Wages
Reward to capital	Interest
Reward to enterprise	Profit
Definition of land as a factor of production	All natural resources: soil, minerals, water, forests
Definition of labour as a factor of production	Human physical and mental effort
Definition of capital as a factor of production	Man-made goods used to produce other goods: machinery, factories, tools
Definition of enterprise	Organising the other three factors and bearing the risk
Does capital mean money in economics?	No — it means productive equipment; money is only a means of acquiring it
What does a production possibility curve show?	The maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with its resources used fully and efficiently
Meaning of a point on the PPC	Efficient — all resources employed
Meaning of a point inside the PPC	Inefficient, or resources unemployed
Meaning of a point outside the PPC	Unattainable with current resources and technology
What does a movement along the PPC show?	Resources reallocated between the two goods, which is where opportunity cost is visible
Four causes of an outward shift of the PPC	More or better resources, new technology, more training, investment in capital
Three causes of an inward shift of the PPC	War, natural disaster, a fall in the workforce
What does a bowed (concave) PPC show?	Increasing opportunity cost
Why does a bowed PPC show increasing opportunity cost?	Resources are not equally suited to both goods, so each extra unit costs more of the other
What does a straight-line PPC assume?	Constant opportunity cost
Opportunity cost when a workshop gives up 4 tables to gain 12 chairs	4 tables
On a bowed PPC, does the next 12 chairs cost the same?	No — it costs more, because opportunity cost increases
Three questions every economic system must answer	What to produce, how to produce it, and for whom
Who decides in a market economy?	Consumers and firms, through prices
Who decides in a planned economy?	The government, centrally
Ownership in a market economy	Private
Ownership in a planned economy	State
Motive in a market economy	Profit and self-interest
Motive in a planned economy	Social and political objectives
Two strengths of a market economy	Strong incentives and efficiency, plus choice and innovation
Three weaknesses of a market economy	Inequality, market failure, public goods undersupplied
Two strengths of a planned economy	It can provide for all and reduce inequality, and it can supply public goods
Three weaknesses of a planned economy	Weak incentives, poor information, shortages and surpluses
Definition of a mixed economy	An economy in which both private firms and the state allocate resources
Four criteria for comparing economic systems	Incentives, information, equity, and the ability to respond to unmet needs
Three functions of the price mechanism	Signalling, incentive, rationing
The signalling function of price	A rising price tells producers buyers want more and tells consumers to look for substitutes
The incentive function of price	A higher price rewards producers for supplying more and draws resources into that market
The rationing function of price	When a good is scarce the price rises, so only those willing and able to pay the most obtain it
Four limits of the price mechanism	It ignores external costs, undersupplies public and merit goods, overprovides demerit goods, and allocates by ability to pay rather than need
