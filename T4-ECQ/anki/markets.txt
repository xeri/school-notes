#deck:Economics::2 Demand, supply and the market
Definition of demand	The quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at a range of prices over a period
Definition of quantity demanded	The amount consumers are willing and able to buy at one particular price
Definition of market demand	The sum of all individuals' demand for a product at each price
Meaning of ceteris paribus	All other factors remain the same
The law of demand	As price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus, and vice versa
Why does quantity demanded rise when price falls?	Consumers are more able to afford it with their limited income, and more willing because it is cheaper
What does a change in the good's own price cause on a demand diagram?	A movement along the curve — a change in quantity demanded
What causes the demand curve to shift?	A change in a condition of demand (a non-price factor)
Meaning of a rightward shift of the demand curve	An increase in demand — more is demanded at each and every price
Name six conditions of demand	Tastes and fashion, income, price of substitutes, price of complements, population, advertising and events
Effect of a cut in direct tax on demand for a normal good	Disposable income rises, so demand increases (shifts right)
Effect of a rise in the price of a substitute	Demand for this good increases, because it is now relatively cheaper
Effect of a fall in the price of a complement	Demand for this good increases, because more of both are bought together
Effect of a rise in income on demand for an inferior good	Demand decreases, as consumers switch to better alternatives
Definition of a normal good	A good whose demand rises as income rises
Definition of a luxury good	A good whose demand rises more than proportionately as income rises
Definition of substitutes	Goods that can be used instead of each other, such as Coke and Pepsi
Definition of complements	Goods used together, such as bread and butter
Effect of an earthquake on demand for bottled water	Demand increases, as people worry about the safety of tap water
Why is "a price rise causes a decrease in demand" wrong?	A price rise causes a decrease in quantity demanded, a movement along the curve; a decrease in demand is a leftward shift
Definition of supply	The quantity producers are willing and able to provide at a range of prices over a period
Definition of quantity supplied	The amount producers are willing and able to provide at one particular price
The law of supply	As price rises, quantity supplied rises, ceteris paribus, and vice versa
Why does quantity supplied rise when price rises?	The product becomes more profitable, so the firm can better cover its costs
Definition of related goods in supply	Goods that can be made with the same inputs, such as bread rolls and loaves
Effect on the supply of loaves when the price of bread rolls rises	Supply of loaves decreases, as resources switch to the more profitable rolls
Difference between price and cost	Price is what the customer pays and the firm receives; cost is what it takes the firm to produce the good
Name eight conditions of supply	Costs of production, productivity and technology, indirect taxes, subsidies, tariffs, number of firms, good or bad season, price of related goods
Which condition of supply usually has the greatest impact?	Costs of production
Effect of a rise in GST on supply	Supply decreases (shifts left), as profitability falls
Effect of removing a subsidy on supply	Supply decreases (shifts left)
Effect of a tariff on imported raw materials on supply	Supply decreases, because costs of production rise
Effect of a bad growing season on supply	Supply decreases (shifts left)
Effect of new technology on supply	Supply increases (shifts right), because costs fall
Four external influences on supply	Environmental, legal, political and cultural factors
Difference between an increase in supply and an increase in quantity supplied	An increase in supply is a rightward shift caused by a condition of supply; an increase in quantity supplied is a movement along the curve caused by a price rise
How to label a price change on a demand or supply diagram	P1 and Q1 for the original, P2 and Q2 for the new, with dotted lines to the axes and arrows
Definition of market equilibrium	The price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied, so there is no surplus or shortage
Situation at a price below equilibrium	Excess demand (a shortage): Qd exceeds Qs, so consumers push the price up
Situation at a price above equilibrium	Excess supply (a surplus): Qs exceeds Qd, so producers cut the price to clear stock
At $4, Qs is 2000 and Qd is 10 000. What is the market situation?	A shortage of 8000 units, so the price rises
At $10, Qd is 4000 and Qs is 8000. What is the market situation?	A surplus of 4000 units, so the price falls
Definition of a market	Anywhere buyers and sellers come together to exchange goods and services
Does equilibrium mean the outcome is fair?	No — it only means the market clears, not that everyone can afford the good
Which shifts create a shortage at the original equilibrium price?	An increase in demand or a decrease in supply
Which shifts create a surplus at the original equilibrium price?	An increase in supply or a decrease in demand
Effect of an increase in demand on equilibrium price and quantity	Both rise
Effect of a decrease in demand on equilibrium price and quantity	Both fall
Effect of an increase in supply on equilibrium price and quantity	Price falls and quantity rises
Effect of a decrease in supply on equilibrium price and quantity	Price rises and quantity falls
Effect when demand and supply both increase	Quantity rises; the price change depends on which shift is larger
Equilibrium when Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = 20 + 2P	P = 20 and Q = 60
Consequence of a maximum price set below equilibrium	Excess demand: shortages, queues, rationing and black markets
Consequence of a minimum price set above equilibrium	Excess supply: surpluses, or unemployment in a labour market
Five factors a firm considers when setting its price	PED, the type of product, customers' bargaining power, the rest of the marketing mix, brand image
Cost-plus pricing	Calculating unit cost and adding a fixed percentage mark-up
Competitor pricing	Setting price around rivals' prices
Price discrimination	Charging different groups different prices, based on what each will pay
Contribution pricing	Covering variable cost, then adding a contribution towards fixed costs
Penetration pricing	A low price to enter the market, raised later
Price skimming	A high launch price, reduced over time
Psychological pricing	Prices such as 99c or $199.99 that make the product feel cheaper
Predatory pricing	Temporarily setting a very low price, even below cost, to force rivals out
Definition of a price war	Rival firms repeatedly cutting prices to gain market share
