# Economics study guide (exam Tue 27 Oct, afternoon)

What to study first, why, and how to study each kind of content. The exam is the same day as Maths, in the afternoon.

## The exam at a glance

One **3-hour** paper, **100 marks** — full details and technique in [SYLLABUS.md](../SYLLABUS.md#exam-technique).

| Section | What you do | Marks | Time in the exam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading time | read the data question; choose your three structured questions | — | about 10 minutes |
| **Multiple choice** | 20 questions | 20 | 20–25 minutes |
| **Data response** | one compulsory question on **unseen real data** | 20 | about 36 minutes |
| **Structured questions** | **three** of six, 20 marks each: (a) 2, (b) 4, (c) 6, (d) 8 | 60 | about 36 minutes each |

The (c) *analyse* and (d) *discuss* parts carry **14 of every 20** structured marks, and the data question ends with a discuss part too. So most of the paper rewards two skills: writing a **cause-and-effect chain** and **evaluating** it. That is why the priority order below puts the chains first.

## How to study economics

**The core loop — for every chain and diagram:**

1. **Read** one chain in [Policy chains](../notes/policy-chains.md) (or the relevant notes page) and its diagram.
2. **Close the page.**
3. **Blurt** it: write the chain from memory as numbered steps, and **draw the diagram** with every label — axes, curves, equilibria, shift arrows.
4. **Write one (c) or (d) answer** on it at exam pace (about 11 minutes for a 6-mark (c), about 16 for an 8-mark (d)).
5. **Compare** with the notes and the mark-scheme points. Anything you missed goes back into the next blurt.

**Method by kind of content:**

| Kind of content | Method |
|---|---|
| **Chains** (policy → effect, cause → effect) | the blurt loop above; say each link with "because… which leads to… so…" |
| **Diagrams** | draw from memory, then check against the "Marks are lost by" lines in [DIAGRAMS.md](../DIAGRAMS.md) — diagram marks are mechanical and the cheapest on the paper |
| **Definitions, lists, diagram labels** | Anki — the decks in [anki/](../anki/), starting with [policy-chains.txt](../anki/policy-chains.txt) |
| **Calculations** (PED, % change, CPI, real GDP, unemployment rate, exchange-rate conversions, current account) | cover the answer in the worked examples, redo them, then check; know each formula cold |
| **Discuss answers** | write in full paragraphs: one side, the other side, a conclusion that says what it **depends on** — then mark against the [worked answers](../notes/policy-chains.md#h-two-worked-discuss-answers) |
| **Data response** | practise reading a table: overall trend → highest and lowest with years and figures → the exception; quote numbers in every answer |
| **Multiple choice** | answer from the stem before looking at the options; eliminate the two clearly wrong ones; sketch the diagram in the margin |

## Priority order

Ranked first to last. Each is ranked on **marks** (how often it is examined, and in the high-mark parts), **difficulty for you** (you named micro and macro policy, externalities and trade as the hardest), and **what it unlocks** for later topics.

| Rank | Topic | Pages | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Demand, supply and elasticity** — shifts vs movements, equilibrium, PED and revenue | [Demand, supply and the market](../notes/markets.md); the PED/PES part of [Elasticity, market failure and market structures](../notes/elasticity-failure.md) | **Unlocks everything else:** every tax, subsidy, price-control, labour and currency diagram is a demand–supply shift, and PED is the most-used evaluation point on the whole paper. Heavy in multiple choice. Quick to secure. |
| 2 | **Market failure, externalities and micro intervention** — taxes, subsidies, max/min prices, regulation, provision, information, government failure | [Policy chains](../notes/policy-chains.md) sections (a)–(c); the market-failure half of [Elasticity, market failure and market structures](../notes/elasticity-failure.md) | **You named it as hard**, and it is a regular structured question with a diagram in (c) and a "should the government…" discuss in (d). Builds directly on rank 1. |
| 3 | **Macroeconomic policy** — AD/AS, fiscal, monetary (OCR), supply-side, objectives and their conflicts | [Policy chains](../notes/policy-chains.md) sections (d), (f), (g); [Macroeconomics and policy](../notes/macroeconomics.md) | **Your hardest area**, and the core of the data response, which is usually about a real economy's inflation, unemployment and growth. "Analyse two conflicts between government aims" is a known school question. |
| 4 | **Measuring the macroeconomy** — inflation and the CPI, unemployment, GDP and real GDP, the economic cycle | [Macroeconomics and policy](../notes/macroeconomics.md) | The **calculations and definitions** that the data response and multiple choice test directly. Easier than rank 3 but needed to read any NZ data table. |
| 5 | **Trade, exchange rates and the balance of payments** — depreciation, protection, current account | [Policy chains](../notes/policy-chains.md) section (e); [International trade and exchange rates](../notes/trade.md) | **You named it as hard.** Exchange-rate conversions are reliable multiple-choice and data marks, and "will a fall in the currency always benefit an economy" is a known school question. Needs ranks 1 and 3. |
| 6 | **Turning knowledge into marks** — evaluation, data response and full 20-mark questions | [Exam practice](../notes/exam-practice.md); [Policy chains](../notes/policy-chains.md) sections (g) and (h) | Not a topic but the step that converts ranks 1–5 into marks. Use it **after each** of ranks 1–5 (do the matching practice question), not just at the end. |
| 7 | **Firms, costs and labour markets** — market structures, costs, economies of scale, the labour market and minimum wage | [Firms, costs and labour markets](../notes/firms-labour.md); market structures in [Elasticity, market failure and market structures](../notes/elasticity-failure.md) | Often one of the six structured questions, so it widens your choice. Mostly definitions and advantages/disadvantages tables — Anki-friendly. Watch the overlap with Business (link below). |
| 8 | **Development, population and poverty** | [Development, population and poverty](../notes/development.md) | Often one of the six structured questions, and a possible data-response context. Mostly descriptive — indicators, causes, policies — so it is quicker to learn than the policy chains. |
| 9 | **Foundations** — scarcity, opportunity cost, the PPC, economic systems | [Scarcity, choice and economic systems](../notes/foundations.md) | Easy (a) and (b) marks and multiple choice; the PPC is a required diagram. Low difficulty, so it comes late. |
| 10 | **Money and exchange** | [Money and exchange](../notes/money.md) | Multiple-choice facts and the occasional (a)/(b) part. Anki covers most of it. |
| 11 | **The NZ economy** | [The New Zealand economy](../notes/nz-economy.md) | Background facts that make examples specific (the Reserve Bank, Stats NZ, export structure). Read for examples rather than learning it as a topic. |
