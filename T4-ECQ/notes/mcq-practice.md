# Multiple-choice practice: a timed Paper 1

Paper 1 is **30 multiple-choice questions in 45 minutes**, worth 30 of the exam's 110 marks. It is sat first and collected before Paper 2. This page is one full practice paper in the same shape. The questions follow the course order, as a real paper's usually do, and were written from these notes in the style of Cambridge IGCSE 0455 Paper 1. They are not past-paper questions. Every distractor is a mistake that really costs marks, and the [answers](#answers) say which trap each one is. For more full papers, any 0455 Paper 1 has exactly this format.

## How to sit it

1. **Set a 45-minute timer.** Write 1–30 down the side of a sheet as your grid, with your name and student ID at the top. Doing that now makes it a habit for the real grid.
2. **First pass, about 30 minutes.** Answer every question you can in about a minute. Cover the options, answer from the stem, then find your answer. Mark any doubtful question and move on.
3. **Second pass, about 10 minutes.** Go back to the marked questions: sketch the diagram, write the formula, eliminate the two clearly wrong options.
4. **Grid check, the last 5 minutes.** Check there is one answer in every row and that each row matches its question number. Leave no blanks.
5. **Mark it with the [answer key](#answer-key)**, then read the reason for every question you got wrong *and* every one you guessed. Name the trap from the [list below](#this-loses-marks), not just the right letter.

Count your misses by topic. The topic with the most misses is your next `/study econ <topic>`. Bring the misses to that session too: say which questions you missed and why, and they come back as mistakes to fix before you would forget them.

## The paper

### Scarcity, choice and economic systems (1–4)

**1.** A town council can afford only one project. It ranks the options: first a new library, second a swimming pool, third a cycle lane. It builds the library. What is the opportunity cost of its choice?

- **A** the library
- **B** the money spent on the library
- **C** the swimming pool
- **D** the swimming pool and the cycle lane

**2.** Which of these is **capital**, as a factor of production, for a florist?

- **A** \$20 000 saved in the florist's bank account
- **B** the delivery van the florist uses
- **C** the florist's skill in arranging flowers
- **D** the land the shop stands on

**3.** An economy is producing at a point **inside** its production possibility curve. Which statement is correct?

- **A** It is using all its resources efficiently.
- **B** The combination cannot be reached with its current resources.
- **C** Its production possibility curve must have shifted inwards.
- **D** It could produce more of both goods without any opportunity cost.

**4.** After a poor harvest the price of avocados rises sharply, so the avocados that are available go to the buyers who are willing and able to pay the most. Which function of the price mechanism is this?

- **A** rationing
- **B** incentive
- **C** signalling
- **D** redistribution

### Demand, supply and the market (5–9)

**5.** Which change would cause a **movement along** the demand curve for butter, rather than a shift of it?

- **A** a fall in the price of margarine
- **B** a rise in consumers' incomes
- **C** a fall in the price of butter
- **D** an advertising campaign for butter

**6.** Bread and butter are complements. The price of bread rises. What happens in the market for butter?

- **A** Demand for butter falls, so its price and the quantity sold both fall.
- **B** Demand for butter rises, so its price and the quantity sold both rise.
- **C** The supply of butter falls, so its price rises and the quantity sold falls.
- **D** The quantity of butter demanded falls along an unchanged demand curve.

**7.** An excellent kiwifruit season produces a record harvest. Ceteris paribus, what happens to the equilibrium price and quantity of kiwifruit?

- **A** price rises, quantity rises
- **B** price rises, quantity falls
- **C** price falls, quantity falls
- **D** price falls, quantity rises

**8.** In the market for electric bikes, demand and supply **both increase** at the same time. Which outcome is certain?

- **A** The equilibrium price rises.
- **B** The equilibrium price falls.
- **C** The equilibrium quantity rises.
- **D** The equilibrium quantity falls.

**9.** The table shows the market for a good.

| Price (\$) | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 800 | 200 |
| 3 | 700 | 400 |
| 4 | 600 | 600 |
| 5 | 500 | 800 |

The government sets a **maximum price** of \$3. What is the result?

- **A** a shortage of 300 units, with 400 units sold
- **B** a shortage of 300 units, with 700 units sold
- **C** a surplus of 300 units, with 400 units sold
- **D** no effect: 600 units are still sold at \$4

### Elasticity, market failure and intervention (10–15)

**10.** A bus company raises its fare from \$4.00 to \$5.00, and the number of trips falls from 1000 to 900 a day. What is the price elasticity of demand (ignoring the sign), and what happens to the company's revenue?

- **A** 2.5, and revenue rises
- **B** 0.4, and revenue rises
- **C** 0.4, and revenue falls
- **D** 2.5, and revenue falls

**11.** Which product is most likely to have **price elastic** demand?

- **A** table salt
- **B** petrol
- **C** cigarettes
- **D** one brand of breakfast cereal

**12.** Why will a free market not provide street lighting?

- **A** People can benefit from it without paying, so no firm can charge for it.
- **B** It is a merit good that consumers undervalue.
- **C** Demand for it is price inelastic.
- **D** It creates external costs for third parties.

**13.** A factory's private cost of production is \$50 a unit, and its pollution imposes an external cost of \$20 a unit on local residents. What is the social cost per unit, and how does free-market output compare with the socially best output?

- **A** \$30, and output is too high
- **B** \$70, and output is too low
- **C** \$70, and output is too high
- **D** \$50, and output is about right

**14.** The same indirect tax per unit is placed on two goods. Demand for the first is price **inelastic**; demand for the second is price elastic. Compared with the second good, what is most likely for the first?

- **A** Consumers pay more of the tax, and the quantity sold falls by less.
- **B** Producers pay more of the tax, and the quantity sold falls by more.
- **C** The price rises by more than the tax.
- **D** The supply curve shifts to the right.

**15.** The government pays bus operators a subsidy of \$1.50 for every trip. What happens in the market for bus trips?

- **A** Demand shifts to the right, and fares rise.
- **B** Supply shifts to the left; fares rise and trips fall.
- **C** Demand shifts to the left, and fares fall.
- **D** Supply shifts to the right (down); fares fall and trips rise.

### Money and banking (16–17)

**16.** A shop lets customers take furniture home now and pay for it in monthly instalments over the next year. Which function of money makes this possible?

- **A** medium of exchange
- **B** measure of value
- **C** store of value
- **D** means (standard) of deferred payment

**17.** Which is a function of a **central bank** but **not** of a commercial bank?

- **A** accepting deposits from households
- **B** lending to small firms
- **C** setting the official interest rate (in New Zealand, the OCR)
- **D** selling foreign currency to importers

### Firms, costs and labour (18–21)

**18.** Which of these is **not** an internal economy of scale?

- **A** bulk-buying discounts as the firm grows
- **B** cheaper loans, because lenders see the larger firm as less risky
- **C** a new motorway built near the firm's factory, cutting its delivery costs
- **D** employing specialist managers

**19.** A firm's fixed costs are \$6000 a month. It sells each unit for \$10, and its variable cost is \$4 a unit. What is its break-even output each month?

- **A** 600 units
- **B** 1000 units
- **C** 1500 units
- **D** 429 units

**20.** A factory's output rises from 1000 to 2000 units a week when its workforce rises from 10 to 20 workers. Which statement is correct?

- **A** Production has doubled, but labour productivity is unchanged.
- **B** Production and labour productivity have both doubled.
- **C** Labour productivity has doubled, but production is unchanged.
- **D** Production and labour productivity are both unchanged.

**21.** In a labour market the equilibrium wage is \$20 an hour and 5000 people are employed. A minimum wage of \$24 is introduced. Firms now want to employ 4400 workers, while 5800 people want jobs. How much unemployment does the minimum wage create?

- **A** 600
- **B** 800
- **C** 1400
- **D** 5800

### Macroeconomics (22–26)

**22.** Nominal GDP rises from \$200bn to \$220bn while the general price level rises by 10%. What has happened to real GDP?

- **A** It has risen by 10%.
- **B** It is unchanged.
- **C** It has risen by 20%.
- **D** It has fallen by 10%.

**23.** A consumer price index was 100 in its base year, 120 in 2024 and 126 in 2025. What was the rate of inflation between 2024 and 2025?

- **A** 6%
- **B** 20%
- **C** 26%
- **D** 5%

**24.** A country has a working-age population of 4 million, a labour force of 3 million and 2.7 million people in work. What is its unemployment rate?

- **A** 7.5%
- **B** 10%
- **C** 11.1%
- **D** 25%

**25.** The table shows the tax paid at three levels of income under four tax systems. Which system is **progressive**?

| System | Income \$20 000 | Income \$50 000 | Income \$100 000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | \$2000 | \$5000 | \$10 000 |
| **B** | \$2000 | \$4000 | \$6000 |
| **C** | \$2000 | \$6000 | \$15 000 |
| **D** | \$3000 | \$3000 | \$3000 |

**26.** The Reserve Bank raises the Official Cash Rate. Which sequence is most likely?

- **A** borrowing becomes cheaper → spending rises → inflation rises
- **B** borrowing becomes dearer → spending falls → aggregate demand falls → inflation falls
- **C** saving becomes less attractive → spending rises → aggregate demand rises
- **D** the NZ dollar depreciates → imports become dearer → inflation falls

### Development and trade (27–30)

**27.** Which three measures make up the Human Development Index?

- **A** income per head, years of schooling, life expectancy at birth
- **B** income per head, the Gini coefficient, the literacy rate
- **C** GDP growth, the unemployment rate, the inflation rate
- **D** life expectancy, infant mortality, doctors per 1000 people

**28.** The table shows what one worker can produce in a day in two countries.

| | Wool (kg) | Wine (bottles) |
|---|---|---|
| Country X | 20 | 40 |
| Country Y | 10 | 30 |

Which statement is correct?

- **A** X should specialise in wine and Y in wool.
- **B** Y cannot gain from trade, because X is better at producing both goods.
- **C** X has a comparative advantage in both goods.
- **D** X should specialise in wool and Y in wine.

**29.** The exchange rate changes from NZ\$1 = US\$0.60 to NZ\$1 = US\$0.50. A laptop made in the USA costs US\$900. What happens to its price in New Zealand dollars?

- **A** It rises from NZ\$1500 to NZ\$1800.
- **B** It falls from NZ\$1800 to NZ\$1500.
- **C** It falls from NZ\$540 to NZ\$450.
- **D** It rises from NZ\$450 to NZ\$540.

**30.** Which item is recorded in the **primary income** part of New Zealand's current account?

- **A** dairy exports to China
- **B** dividends paid to foreign owners of shares in NZ companies
- **C** spending by Australian tourists in Queenstown
- **D** foreign aid given by New Zealand to Pacific countries

### Why: scarcity, markets and elasticity (1–11)

**1 C.** Opportunity cost is the **next best** alternative forgone, so it is one option: the pool. D adds up everything rejected; A and B describe what was chosen. [Opportunity cost](../notes/foundations.md#opportunity-cost)

**2 B.** Capital is man-made equipment used to produce, here the van. Money (A) is only a means of buying capital; the skill (C) is labour; the land (D) is land. [Factors of production](../notes/foundations.md#factors-of-production)

**3 D.** A point inside the curve means resources are unused or badly used, so both goods can rise at no cost. A describes a point on the curve and B one outside it. A recession puts the economy inside its curve without the curve itself shifting (C). [Production possibility curve](../notes/foundations.md#production-possibility-curve)

**4 A.** Scarce goods go to those willing and able to pay the most: rationing. Signalling is the price *telling* producers and consumers something; incentive is the price *drawing resources in*. [The price mechanism](../notes/foundations.md#the-price-mechanism)

**5 C.** Only the good's **own price** moves along its demand curve. A substitute's price (A), income (B) and advertising (D) are conditions of demand, so they shift it. [Demand, supply and the market](../notes/markets.md)

**6 A.** Dearer bread means less bread bought, so less butter is wanted at every price: demand for butter **shifts** left, and its price and quantity fall. D is the movement-vs-shift trap, because butter's own price did not change first. [Demand, supply and the market](../notes/markets.md)

**7 D.** A good season shifts **supply** right: a surplus at the old price pushes the price down, and quantity demanded extends. [A new equilibrium](../notes/markets.md#a-new-equilibrium)

**8 C.** Both shifts raise quantity, so that much is certain. The price depends on which shift is larger, which is why A and B are only possible. [A new equilibrium](../notes/markets.md#a-new-equilibrium)

**9 A.** At \$3, $Q_d$ = 700 and $Q_s$ = 400, so there is a shortage of 300. Only **400** can be bought, because sellers supply only $Q_s$ (B is the trap). A maximum price below equilibrium does bind (D). [Maximum and minimum prices](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#maximum-and-minimum-prices)

**10 B.** Price +25% (1 ÷ 4) and quantity −10% (100 ÷ 1000), so PED = 10 ÷ 25 = 0.4: inelastic. Revenue rises from \$4000 to \$4500. A and D put the formula upside down (25 ÷ 10). [Price elasticity of demand](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#price-elasticity-of-demand)

**11 D.** One brand among many close substitutes means buyers switch easily. Salt (a tiny share of income), petrol (a necessity) and cigarettes (addictive) are inelastic. [Determinants of PED](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#determinants-of-ped)

### Why: market failure, money and firms (12–21)

**12 A.** Street lighting is **non-excludable**: free riders mean no firm can charge. B confuses a public good with a merit good; merit goods are excludable and rival. [Market failure](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#market-failure)

**13 C.** Social cost = private cost + external cost = \$70. Firms decide on the \$50 private cost alone, so they over-produce. [Externalities](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#externalities)

**14 A.** With inelastic demand, buyers keep buying as the price rises, so more of the tax is passed on to them and quantity falls little. The price rises by **less** than the tax (C), and a tax shifts supply **left** (D). [Indirect taxes and their incidence](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#indirect-taxes-and-their-incidence)

**15 D.** A subsidy cuts producers' costs, so **supply** shifts right (down) by the subsidy. Demand does not move (A, C). [Subsidies](../notes/elasticity-failure.md#subsidies)

**16 D.** "Buy now, pay later" relies on money keeping its value until the debt is paid: the means (standard) of deferred payment. [Money](../notes/money.md#money)

**17 C.** The central bank runs monetary policy, so it sets the OCR. Commercial banks take deposits, lend and supply foreign exchange. [Banks and the money market](../notes/money.md#banks-and-the-money-market)

**18 C.** A motorway is shared infrastructure outside the firm, so it is an **external** economy: every firm in the area gains. A, B and D come from the firm's own growth. Circle the **not**. [Economies and diseconomies of scale](../notes/firms-labour.md#economies-and-diseconomies-of-scale)

**19 B.** Break-even = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit = 6000 ÷ (10 − 4) = 1000. A divides by the price, C by the variable cost, and D by price plus variable cost. [Costs, revenue and profit](../notes/firms-labour.md#costs-revenue-and-profit)

**20 A.** Output per worker is 100 both before and after, so production has doubled but productivity has not changed. [Production and productivity](../notes/firms-labour.md#production-and-productivity)

**21 C.** Unemployment is the **excess supply of labour** at the minimum wage: 5800 − 4400 = 1400. A (600) is only the fall in employment, and B (800) only the extra people looking for work. [Minimum wage and the role of government](../notes/firms-labour.md#minimum-wage-and-the-role-of-government)

### Why: macroeconomics, development and trade (22–30)

**22 B.** Real GDP = 220 ÷ 1.10 = \$200bn: unchanged, because the whole rise was inflation. A treats the nominal rise as real growth. [Nominal and real GDP](../notes/macroeconomics.md#nominal-and-real-gdp)

**23 D.** Inflation = (126 − 120) ÷ 120 × 100 = 5%. A (6%) is the rise in index points, which equals the inflation rate only when the old index is 100. C and B compare with the base year. [Inflation](../notes/macroeconomics.md#inflation)

**24 B.** 0.3 million unemployed ÷ 3 million in the labour force = 10%. A divides by the working-age population and C by the number employed; D is the share outside the labour force. [Employment and unemployment](../notes/macroeconomics.md#employment-and-unemployment)

**25 C.** The **proportion** of income taken rises: 10%, 12%, 15%. A is proportional (10% each time). B pays more dollars at higher incomes but a falling proportion (10%, 8%, 6%), so it is regressive; so is D. [Fiscal policy](../notes/macroeconomics.md#fiscal-policy)

**26 B.** A higher OCR raises borrowing costs and makes saving more attractive, so spending and aggregate demand fall. It also tends to make the NZ dollar **appreciate**, not depreciate (D). [Monetary policy](../notes/macroeconomics.md#monetary-policy)

**27 A.** The HDI combines income (GNI per head), education (years of schooling) and health (life expectancy at birth). [Composite and distribution measures](../notes/development.md#composite-and-distribution-measures)

**28 D.** One kg of wool costs X 2 bottles of wine and Y 3, so X gives up less: X has the comparative advantage in wool and Y in wine. X has the **absolute** advantage in both (C confuses the two), and Y still gains from trade (B). [Absolute and comparative advantage](../notes/trade.md#absolute-and-comparative-advantage)

**29 A.** Foreign price ÷ rate gives the NZ price: 900 ÷ 0.60 = NZ\$1500 before, 900 ÷ 0.50 = NZ\$1800 after. Each NZ dollar now buys fewer US dollars, so the NZ dollar has depreciated and imports are dearer. C and D multiply instead of dividing. [Exchange rates](../notes/trade.md#exchange-rates)

**30 B.** Dividends, interest and profits paid across borders are primary income; this one is a debit. Dairy exports (A) are goods; tourists' spending (C) is a service, an invisible export; aid (D) is secondary income. [The balance of payments](../notes/trade.md#the-balance-of-payments)

## This loses marks

The traps this paper sets are the ones real multiple-choice papers set again and again.

- **Adding up the rejected options.** Opportunity cost is the single next best alternative, not everything given up. *(Q1)*
- **Calling money capital.** Capital is man-made productive equipment; money in the bank is not capital. *(Q2)*
- **Demand or quantity demanded.** Only the good's own price moves along its curve; anything else shifts it. When a complement's price rises, demand for the other good **shifts** left. *(Q5, Q6)*
- **Claiming both price and quantity when both curves shift.** Only one of them is certain. *(Q8)*
- **The quantity traded under a price control.** Under a maximum price only $Q_s$ is sold; under a minimum price only $Q_d$ is bought, unless the government buys the surplus. A ceiling **above** equilibrium, or a floor **below** it, has no effect. *(Q9)*
- **PED upside down.** Quantity goes on top: % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price. *(Q10)*
- **Mixing up public and merit goods.** Public goods are non-excludable and non-rival (free riders); merit goods are undervalued but can be charged for. *(Q12)*
- **Expecting the price to rise by the whole tax.** It rises by less, because producers bear part of it. The more inelastic the demand, the more falls on consumers. *(Q14)*
- **Internal or external economies.** Savings from the firm's own growth are internal. Savings from its industry or area, such as a new motorway or a local training school, are external. *(Q18)*
- **The wrong divisor for break-even.** Divide fixed costs by the contribution per unit (price − variable cost per unit), not by the price. *(Q19)*
- **Production or productivity.** More output from more workers is more production, not more productivity. *(Q20)*
- **The fall in jobs or the unemployment.** Under a minimum wage, unemployment is labour supplied minus labour demanded at that wage. *(Q21)*
- **Nominal or real.** If prices rose as fast as nominal GDP, real output did not grow. *(Q22)*
- **Index points or per cent.** A CPI rising from 120 to 126 is 6 points but 5% inflation. In the same way, unemployment rising from 4% to 5% is 1 percentage point but 25%. *(Q23)*
- **The wrong denominator for unemployment.** Divide by the **labour force**, not the population, the working-age population or the number employed. *(Q24)*
- **Judging a tax by dollars.** Progressive, proportional and regressive depend on the **proportion** of income paid. *(Q25)*
- **The exchange-rate direction.** A higher OCR attracts foreign savings, so the currency appreciates. Multiply to turn NZ dollars into foreign currency, divide to turn foreign currency into NZ dollars, and write the quotation down first. *(Q26, Q29)*
- **Absolute or comparative advantage.** Comparative advantage means the lower opportunity cost, so a country that is worse at everything still has one. *(Q28)*
- **The wrong part of the current account.** Goods are visible trade. Services such as tourism, education and insurance are invisible trade. Primary income is interest, dividends, profits and wages; secondary income is aid, remittances and gifts. *(Q30)*
- **Missing the small word.** Circle **not**, **least**, **most likely** and **always** before reading the options. *(Q18)*
- **A shifted grid.** Skipping a question without skipping its row moves every later answer into the wrong row.
