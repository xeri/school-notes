#deck:Mathematics::3 Coordinate geometry and graphs
Equation of a straight line	\(y = mx+c\)
What are m and c?	m is the gradient, c is the y-intercept
Gradient from two points	\(m = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)
Length of a line segment	\(\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\)
Midpoint of a line segment	\(\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)\)
Midpoint of (1,3) and (5,6)	(3, 4.5)
Length from (1,3) to (5,6)	\(\sqrt{16+9} = 5\)
Gradient relationship for parallel lines	Equal gradients
Gradient relationship for perpendicular lines	\(m_1m_2 = -1\), so \(m_2 = -1/m_1\)
Gradient of a line perpendicular to \(y = 3x+1\)	\(-1/3\)
Line parallel to \(y = 4x-1\) through (0,-3)	\(y = 4x-3\)
How do you find c once you have m?	Substitute the coordinates of a known point
What does the gradient of a distance–time graph give?	Speed
What does the gradient of a speed–time graph give?	Acceleration
What does the area under a speed–time graph give?	Distance travelled
How do you find the area under a linear speed–time graph?	Split it into triangles and rectangles
Shape of \(y = ax^2\)	A parabola — minimum if a > 0, maximum if a < 0
Shape of \(y = ax^3\)	A cubic with rotational symmetry about the origin
Shape of \(y = a/x\)	A reciprocal curve with two branches and both axes as asymptotes
Shape of \(y = a/x^2\)	Two branches, both above the x-axis when a > 0
Shape of \(y = a^x\)	For a > 1: exponential growth — rises steeply and never reaches zero
Shape of \(y = ax^0\)	The horizontal line y = a
What is an asymptote?	A line the curve approaches but never meets
What do you write in a table of values at x = 0 for \(y = a/x\)?	Nothing — the function is undefined there
Which powers of \(ax^n\) appear in the syllabus?	n = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, in sums of up to three terms
How should you join points on a curve?	With a single smooth curve — never dot-to-dot
How do you solve \(f(x) = k\) graphically?	Draw the line y = k and read the x-coordinates where it cuts the curve
How do you solve \(f(x) = g(x)\) graphically?	Draw both and read the x-coordinates of the intersections
How do you estimate the gradient of a curve at a point?	Draw a tangent at that point and find its gradient with a large triangle
Gradient of the tangent at a maximum or minimum	Zero
Shape of an exponential decay curve	Falls steeply then flattens towards the x-axis without reaching it
Real-world example of exponential growth	Compound interest, bacterial growth, population growth
Real-world example of exponential decay	Depreciation, radioactive decay
Equation of a line with gradient m through \((x_1, y_1)\)	\(y-y_1 = m(x-x_1)\)
Differentiate \(ax^n\)	\(anx^{n-1}\)
Differentiate \((2x+1)^2\)	Expand first: \(4x^2+4x+1\), so the derivative is \(8x+4\)
Differentiate \(2x^{-1}\)	\(-2x^{-2}\)
What does \(f'(a)\) give?	The gradient of the curve (of its tangent) at x = a
Tangent to \(y = 2x^2-8x+3\) at x = 3	\(f'(3) = 4\), point (3, −3), so \(y = 4x-15\)
Integrate \(ax^n\) (n ≠ −1)	\(\frac{ax^{n+1}}{n+1} + C\)
Why must you add C when integrating?	A constant differentiates to 0, so it is unknown until you substitute a given point
\(V'(x) = 4x-3\) and \(V(0) = 2\) — find \(V(x)\)	\(V(x) = 2x^2-3x+C\), and \(V(0) = 2\) gives \(C = 2\): \(V(x) = 2x^2-3x+2\)
