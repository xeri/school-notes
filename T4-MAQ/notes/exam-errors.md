# Exam errors: the small mistakes that cost marks

Most lost marks in this paper are not from topics you don't know. They come from rounding, a missing second root, a missing $+C$ or an unfinished statement. This page lists the mistakes the 2022–2025 final mark schemes penalise. Each item gives **the error → the fix → the source**. "2025 Q1(a)" means the 2025 final mark scheme, question 1(a).

Read this page before every practice paper and again on the morning of the exam.

## How marks are awarded

The front page of every final mark scheme uses the same four letters.

| Mark | What earns it | How you lose it |
|---|---|---|
| **M** — Method | A correct step written down: substituting into a formula, "Factorise", "Expand", "Swap" (for an inverse), drawing a line with the right gradient | Answer only, with no working. With working, a slip still keeps the method marks; with none, a wrong answer scores 0 |
| **A** — Answer | The correct value, **correctly rounded and in an accepted form**. Often split as "Each term", "Each factor" or "Both solutions" | Wrong rounding, a missing second root, a missing $+C$, a form the scheme rejects |
| **S** — Statement | A written statement: "Shown" at the end of a show-that, a geometric reason, each fact in a transformation, the variation equation $y = \tfrac{k}{\ldots}$, a yes/no with a reason | Leaving the statement out, even when the numbers are right |
| **C** — Calculation | An intermediate step, listed in the key on every scheme | Rarely used in 2022–2025, but the same principle: write the step down |

**Follow-through.** The schemes give later marks for your own earlier value: "$f(x) = 12x-2x^3-7$ [or, their 'c' value] Statement consistent" (2023 Q6(a)(v), and again in 2024 Q6(b)). Finish every part, even if an earlier answer looks wrong.

**Statements in context.** 2025 Q5(b) gave 1 A + 2 S for "NO" plus the reason: he would need to score 24 in the last game, and the maximum is 21. A bare "No" can earn at most 1 of the 3 marks.

## Accuracy and rounding

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding to whatever looks nice | Non-exact answers to **3 s.f.** unless the question says otherwise | Every front cover; 2022 Q12(b): "$r = 3.68$ cm (as per instructions on the front cover = 3sf)" |
| Angles to 3 s.f. or a whole number | Angles in degrees to **1 d.p.** | 2025 Q3(b)(ii): $48.18968\ldots = 48.2°$ (1dp) |
| Money to 1 d.p. | Money to **2 d.p.**, always | 2025 Q1(a): "\$57.60 [do not accept \$57.6]"; 2024 Q1(d) \$2.40; 2025 Q1(b)(iii) \$4455.00 |
| Dropping a trailing zero | 3 s.f. of $4.39923$ is $4.40$, not $4.4$; 1 d.p. of $39.996$ is $40.0$ | 2025 Q12(b)(ii) $r = 4.40$ cm; 2025 Q11(b)(i) $\theta = 40.0°$; 2024 Q8(c) 1.40 hours |
| Using 3 s.f. when the question asks for something else | **A stated accuracy overrides 3 s.f. and the 1 d.p. angle rule** | 2022 Q1(d) 9.7% (2 s.f.); 2025 Q6(g)(i) angle $60.14°$ (4 s.f.) and (ii) $140$ km² (2 s.f.); 2024 Q8(c) 1.40 h (2 d.p.) |
| Rounding a value, then reusing it | Keep the full calculator value (or the exact surd) for the next step; round only the final answer | 2023 Q9(c) keeps $k = \sqrt{226/94}$ exact and gets 182 cm³. Rounding $k$ to 1.55 first gives 183 — wrong |
| Writing only the rounded answer | Write the long value, then the rounded one: "$= 60.1442\ldots = 60.14°$" | Every scheme shows the unrounded line before the answer |
| Using 3.14 for $\pi$ | Use the calculator $\pi$ | Every front cover: "For $\pi$, use the calculator value" |
| Rounding an exact answer | If the answer is exact ($\pm2\sqrt3$, $\tfrac{1}{14}$, $\sqrt[3]{3}$), leave it exact unless a decimal is asked for | 2023 Q6(a)(iv) "$\pm2\sqrt3$ [Accept $\pm\sqrt{12}$]"; 2023 Q6(e)(iv) "$\sqrt[3]{3}$ [accept 1.442249… rounded & stated]" |

When you choose your own accuracy, say what it is: 2022 Q12(c) accepted "9.8 cm (1 d.p.)" or "9.79 cm (3 s.f.)" — "any appropriate rounding correctly stated".

## "Show that" questions

The answer is printed, so **every mark is for working**, and the last mark is often an S for the final line.

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Writing the target value straight away | Show the **unrounded** value, then the target: "$AC = 15.0079\ldots$, so $AC = 15.0$ (1 d.p.)" | 2025 Q11(a)(i): M, M, A for "15.0079… Shown ∴ $AC = 15.0$" |
| Skipping algebra steps | One step per line — multiply, expand, collect — ending with **exactly** the given equation | 2025 Q2(b)(i): $300 = \tfrac12\cdot6(x+2)\cdot4(x+2) \to 600 = 24(x+2)^2 \to 25 = x^2+4x+4 \to x^2+4x-21 = 0$ "Shown" (M, M, S) |
| No conclusion | End with a statement line: "$= x^2+4x-21 = 0$ as required" or "Shown" | 2025 Q2(a) "$h = 4x$ Shown" (S) |
| Starting from the given answer | Start from the information in the question. Never use the printed result to prove itself | — |
| Giving up on the next part | If you can't show it, **use the given value** in the later parts anyway | 2025 Q11(a)(ii) uses $AC = 15.0$ from (i) |

## Roots, signs and ±

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Giving one root of a quadratic | "Solve" means **both roots**, then decide | 2025 Q2(b)(ii) "$x = 3$ and $-7$ — Both Solutions"; 2022 Q2(c)(ii) "Solutions ×2"; 2023 Q2(b) $2.87$ and $-0.871$ (A, A) |
| Keeping an impossible root in a context | Reject it **with a reason** (a length can't be negative; a speed $x-40$ must be positive) | 2024 Q3(c) "Reject $x = -3$", sides 6, 8, 10; 2025 Q8(c) "$x = 50$, $18.18\ldots$ [Reject 18.18…]" |
| One angle for a trig equation | Find **every** solution in the range — sketch the graph | 2022 Q2(d) and 2023 Q10(c)(ii): $\cos x = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ gives $30°$ **and** $330°$ ("Both Solutions") |
| $x^2 = 12 \Rightarrow x = \sqrt{12}$ | $x^2 = k$ gives $x = \pm\sqrt k$ | 2023 Q6(a)(iv) $x = \pm2\sqrt3$; 2024 Q6(a)(iv) $x = \pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$; 2023 Q10(a)(ii) $y = \pm16$ |
| ± on a quantity that must be positive | A length, area or "$A$ is positive" takes the positive root only | 2022 Q6(a)(i): $A = \sqrt{9t^2} = 3t$ "(do not accept $\pm3t$, as $A$ is positive)" |
| Losing a minus in the formula | Bracket every negative before squaring: $(-750)^2$, not $-750^2$ | 2025 Q8(c) |
| Turning point sign | $a(x-2)^2-5$ turns at $x = +2$; $(x+3)^2$ at $x = -3$ | 2025 Q3(c): $2(x-2)^2-5$, vertex $(2,-5)$ |

## Units and conversions

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2 h 25 min written as 2.25 h | Convert to one unit: 2 h 25 min $= 145$ min; 2 h 48 min $= 2.8$ h | 2024 Q1(e) $145 : 180 = 29 : 36$; 2024 Q8(a) "A for 2.8 (correct conversion)" |
| Decimal hours left unexplained | $1.40$ h $= 84$ min; $1.2$ h $= 1$ h 12 min (multiply the decimal by 60) | 2024 Q8(c); 2025 Q8(c) |
| Forgetting to convert to litres | $1$ litre $= 1000$ cm³; per-second rate × $(60\times\text{minutes})$ | 2024 Q12(b): $(\pi\times4.5^2\times200)\times(60\times25)\div1000 = 19\,085 \approx 19\,100$ L |
| cm² to m² by dividing by 100 | Square the length factor: $1$ m² $= 10\,000$ cm²; cube it for volume: $1$ m³ $= 1\,000\,000$ cm³ | Syllabus E4.1; [Mensuration](../notes/mensuration.md) |
| km/h mixed with seconds or minutes | km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s; 30 minutes = 0.5 h | [Graphs](../notes/graphs.md); 2025 CT3 speed–time question in km/h with 30-minute stages |
| No unit on the answer | Every final answer gets its unit: m, cm², cm³, g, L, \$, °, hours | 2025 Q10(c) "2325 metres"; 2022 Q12(a)(iii) "1010 grams" |

## Graph drawing and reading

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Table values at the wrong accuracy | Match the table (usually 2 d.p. or 3 s.f.) | 2023 Q4(a): $2.13$ [2.125], $1.38$ [1.375], $8.88$ [8.875] |
| Points joined with a ruler, or curve stopping early | Plot every point; **one smooth curve** through all of them, from the first to the last $x$ in the table | 2023–2025 Q4(b): "Points plotted & connected, Curve, End points" (3 M) |
| Guessing the line to draw | Rearrange so one side is the plotted function; the other side is the line | 2023–2025 Q4(d); see [Graphs](../notes/graphs.md) |
| Line drawn freehand | Plot 3 points of the line with a ruler — marks are for **gradient** and **$y$-intercept** | 2023–2025 Q4(d)(ii) |
| Roots read as whole numbers or to 2 d.p. | Read every intersection to **1 d.p.** unless told otherwise | 2025 Q4(d)(iii): $-0.3, 0.6, 2.8$ (±0.1), "Not 1dp = 2A" (lose 1 of 3) |
| Missing an intersection | A cubic and a line usually cross **three** times — 3 A marks | 2023, 2024, 2025 Q4(d)(iii) |
| Not naming a tangent | A line that touches the curve and doesn't cross it is a **tangent** — say so | 2024 Q4(c)(iii) and 2025 Q4(c)(iii) |
| Bare sketch of a parabola | Label the **vertex** coordinates and the **$y$-intercept**, right shape | Every year: "Vertex, y-intercept, Shape" (3 M) |
| Trig sketch with wrong height | Mark the amplitude on the $y$-axis: $2\cos x$ goes from $-2$ to $2$ | 2025 Q10(d) "Cos graph, Amplitude" |

## Statistics traps

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong midpoints for whole-number classes | Class 0–19 has boundaries $-0.5$ and $19.5$, so midpoint $9.5$ | 2024 Q7(a): midpoint $\tfrac{-0.5+19.5}{2} = 9.5$; using 10 = 3 marks, not 4. 2022 Q7(a)(ii) 9.5, 29.5, … ("2 marks only" with 10, 30); 2023 Q7(a) 150.5 not 150 |
| Dividing by the number of classes | Mean $= \dfrac{\sum f\times\text{mid}}{\sum f}$ — divide by the total frequency | 2022 Q7(a)(ii) $4084 \div 72$; 2024 Q7(a) $\div 2500$ |
| Histogram bar height = frequency | Height = **frequency density** = frequency ÷ class width; no gaps | 2023 Q7(b) f.d. 0.08, 0.40, 0.24, 0.06; 2024 Q7(b) |
| Cumulative frequency at midpoints | Plot at the **upper class boundary**; start at the first lower boundary with c.f. 0 | 2022 Q7(c)(ii) "Start @ 20" (M) |
| Reading the curve by eye | Draw the reading lines on the graph; quartiles at $\tfrac n4$, $\tfrac n2$, $\tfrac{3n}{4}$ | 2023 Q7(c): quartile 29.5 (±0.5), IQR $= 29.5-17.5 = 12$ (±1.0) — readings are marked with a tolerance |
| "More than" read straight off | Subtract from the total | 2023 Q7(c)(v) "59 days [100 − 41]" |
| Percentile with no meaning | Give the value **and** say what it means in context | 2022 Q7(c)(iv): $35\times0.9 = 31.5 \to 91$, "90% of 4B scored 91% or less" (A + S) |
| Changing mean with no totals | total = mean × number of values | 2025 Q5(b): $11\times18 = 198$, $12\times18.5 = 222$, needs 24 |
| Tree diagram paths added along | Multiply **along** a path, add **between** paths | 2022 Q5(a)(iii) $\tfrac37\times\tfrac35+\tfrac47\times\tfrac25 = \tfrac{17}{35}$ |
| "Exactly one" with only one order | Multiply by the number of arrangements | 2023 Q5(b) $0.6\times0.4^4\times5 = 0.0768$ |

## Algebra traps

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| One end of an inequality | A "between" answer needs both ends: $-3 \leqslant x \leqslant 3$ | 2022 Q2(a): "$x \leqslant 3$ — 1A ONLY" (of 2) |
| Sign not reversed | Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a **negative** reverses the sign | [Algebra](../notes/algebra.md) |
| Minus sign reaching only the first term | Bracket the whole numerator: $3(x-1)-x(2x-5) = 3x-3-2x^2+5x$ | 2024 Q10(b) gives $\dfrac{-2x^2+8x-3}{x(x-1)}$ |
| Surd products | $\sqrt3\times\sqrt3 = 3$, and watch the sign: $(1-2\sqrt3)(1+5\sqrt3) = 1+5\sqrt3-2\sqrt3-30 = 3\sqrt3-29$ | 2023 Q6(b) |
| Inverse in an untidy form | Write "$g^{-1}(x) = \ldots$" in terms of $x$, with a positive denominator: $\dfrac{5-x}{2}$ | 2025 Q6(a)(ii) "accept $\dfrac{-(x-5)}{2}$, not $\dfrac{x-5}{-2}$" |
| Composite in the wrong order | $hg(x) = h(g(x))$: put $g(x)$ into $h$ | 2023 Q6(a)(iii) $h(4-2x^2) = 5-x^2$ |
| Partial factorising | Common factor first, then factorise the bracket | 2022 Q6(b)(i) "$gh(g^2+7h)$ — Common factor plus factor" |
| Cancelling terms in a fraction | Factorise top and bottom, cancel **factors** | 2022 Q2(b) $\dfrac{(x+2)(x+2)}{(x+2)(x-2)} = \dfrac{x+2}{x-2}$; 2025 Q6(b)(ii) |
| Completing the square with $a \neq 1$ | Take $a$ out of the $x$ terms only, and multiply the subtracted number by $a$: $2(x^2-4x+4)+3-8 = 2(x-2)^2-5$ | 2025 Q3(c)(i) (M, M, M); 2024 Q3(d)(i) with $a = -2$: $-2(x-2)^2+3$ |
| Simultaneous equations left as $x$ and $y$ | Finish with the point or the answer in context | 2022 Q6(h) "Point of intersection $(4,-3)$"; 2024 Q3(b) "Bucket = 2 kg" |
| Variation started with numbers | Write $y = \dfrac{k}{\sqrt{x+3}}$ **first** — that line is an S mark — then find $k$ | 2023 Q10(d), 2024 Q10(d), 2025 Q10(e) |

## Calculus traps

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No $+C$ | Every indefinite integral ends in $+C$ — it is a mark on its own | 2022 Q6(f)(ii) "Correct integral plus, Integration Constant" (A, A) |
| Differentiating a bracket directly | **Expand first**: $(2x+1)^2 = 4x^2+4x+1 \to 8x+4$ | 2023 Q6(c)(ii) and 2025 Q6(c)(ii); 2022 Q6(e)(ii); 2024 Q6(d)(ii) $(3x-1)^2 \to 18x-6$ ("Expand" is the M mark) |
| Integrating a bracket or fraction directly | Expand or simplify first: $g'(x) = (x-1)^2 = x^2-2x+1$ | 2025 Q6(e)(ii); 2024 Q6(b) $3(5-3x^2) = 15-9x^2$; 2022 Q6(f) cancel $(2x-1)$ first |
| Negative powers going the wrong way | The power goes **down**: $x^{-5} \to -5x^{-6}$; integrating $-2x^{-2} \to +2x^{-1}$ | 2024 Q6(d)(i); 2025 Q6(e)(i) $\tfrac16x^6+2x^{-1}+C$ |
| Roots left as roots | Rewrite first: $\sqrt x = x^{1/2} \to \tfrac12x^{-1/2}$ | 2022 Q6(e)(i) |
| Point substituted into $f'(x)$ | Integrate, then substitute the point into $f(x)$ to find $C$, then **write the full function** | 2025 Q6(f): $V = 2x^2-3x+C$, $V(0) = 2 \Rightarrow C = 2$, $V(x) = 2x^2-3x+2$ (M, A, A) |
| Using $f'(3)$ as the $y$-value | Gradient from $f'(a)$, point from $f(a)$, then $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ | 2025 Q3(c)(iv)–(vi): $f'(3) = 4$, point $(3,-3)$, $y = 4x-15$ |

## Geometry reasons

| Error | Fix | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Answer with no reason | Every angle step gets a reason in its formal name | 2024 CT4: "State reasoning for each of your steps, including any circle theorems you use" |
| Informal names ("Z angles", "cyclic thing") | Use the full names: **opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral**, **adjacent angles on a straight line**, **angle in a semicircle**, **base angles of an isosceles triangle**, **alternate** / **corresponding** angles | 2025 Q9(a); 2022 Q8(c) "Angle on a diameter"; 2024 Q11(a)(i) "Base angles of isos triangle" |
| "They look similar" | Similar triangles: show all angles are equal and say so | 2025 Q9(b) (S, S) |
| Area or volume scaled by $k$ | Lengths × $k$, areas × $k^2$, volumes (and mass, capacity) × $k^3$ | 2025 Q3(a): $V = 4^3\times125 = 8000$ cm³, and $k^2 = 400 \Rightarrow k = 20$; 2023 Q9(c) |
| Number of sides from the interior angle | Exterior angle first: $180-160 = 20$, then $360\div20 = 18$ sides | 2022 Q8(a) |
| Two-figure or wrong-way bearing | Three figures, clockwise from North; back bearing = bearing ± 180° | 2025 Q10(g): $360-(180-097) = 277°$ |

| Transformation | Every fact you must write | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reflection | "Reflection" + the mirror line as an equation | 2025 Q7(a)(i) "Reflection, in the $x$-axis [or $y = 0$]" (S, S) |
| Rotation | "Rotation" + centre + angle + direction (180° needs no direction) | 2022 Q3(a)(iii) "Rotation, 90° [or 270° clockwise], centre $(0,0)$" (S, S, S); 2025 Q7(a)(iii) "centre $(1,0)$, by 180°" |
| Enlargement | "Enlargement" + centre + scale factor (a fraction for a reduction) | 2022 Q3(a)(ii) SF 2, centre $(-2,0)$ (S, S, S); 2025 Q7(a)(ii) SF $\tfrac12$, centre $(0,0)$ |

## One-liners by topic

The "Mark-losing errors" lines from every notes page, in one list.

**[Number](../notes/number.md)**
- Venn diagrams: subtract the overlap before filling in $A$ and $B$; $\in$ is "belongs to", $\subset$ is "is a subset of"; fill the region outside the circles.
- Indices: $5^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{25}$, not $-25$; $(2x)^3 = 8x^3$; $a^m\times a^n$ adds the indices; $12\times10^3$ is not standard form.
- Surds: $\sqrt{a+b} \neq \sqrt a+\sqrt b$; $(\sqrt5)^2 = 5$; $(1+\sqrt3)^2 = 4+2\sqrt3$; multiply top **and** bottom by the conjugate.
- Bounds: the upper bound of 4.6 is 4.65; for $a-b$ and $a\div b$ use the upper bound of $a$ with the lower bound of $b$; "nearest 10" means ±5.
- Variation: "the square of $x$" means $kx^2$; write the equation after finding $k$; inverse is $y = \tfrac kx$.
- Finance: interest = final value − $P$; $n$ is a power, not a multiplier; a 15% decrease uses 0.85.

**[Algebra](../notes/algebra.md)**
- Fractions: cancel factors, not terms; $-(x-1) = -x+1$; multiply **every** term, including the right-hand side, by the common denominator.
- Completing the square: $(x+3)^2-10$ turns at $x = -3$; multiply $-\tfrac{b^2}{4}$ by $a$; say maximum or minimum.
- Sequences: going up in 4s means $4n+\ldots$; the $n^2$ coefficient is half the second difference; check against a given term.
- Functions: $f^{-1}(x)$ is not $\tfrac{1}{f(x)}$; $gf$ means $f$ first; write the inverse in terms of $x$.

**[Coordinate geometry and graphs](../notes/graphs.md)**
- Travel graphs: the gradient of speed–time is acceleration; convert km/h to m/s (÷3.6); state a deceleration as a positive number.
- Tangents: the tangent must not cut the curve; use a large gradient triangle; a curve going down to the right has a negative gradient.
- Exponential: growth multiplies (simple interest adds); 6% is 1.06; "first exceeds" means the first whole year above, not a rounded one.
- Regions: test a point; solid for $\leqslant$/$\geqslant$, dashed for $<$/$>$; read whether to shade the wanted or unwanted region.

**[Geometry](../notes/geometry.md)**
- Polygons: 360 ÷ **exterior** angle; $(n-2)\times180$ is the total, not one angle; "alternate" and "corresponding", not Z and F.
- Circle theorems: centre angle only when both stand on the same arc; a cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle; tangent ⟂ radius; always give the reason.
- Similar figures: compare whole corresponding sides; area $k^2$, volume, mass and capacity $k^3$.

**[Trigonometry](../notes/trigonometry.md)**
- Trig equations: give every angle in the range, none negative; calculator in degrees ($\sin30 = -0.988$ means radians).
- Sine and cosine rule: check the obtuse option; find the largest angle with the cosine rule.
- 3-D: use the projection on the base, half the diagonal, and unrounded intermediate lengths.

**[Mensuration](../notes/mensuration.md)**
- Calculator $\pi$; round only at the end; a unit on every answer that matches the dimension; convert lengths to one unit before substituting.

**[Vectors, transformations and matrices](../notes/vectors-matrices.md)**
- One single transformation when asked; formal words only; always the centre for rotation and enlargement; translations as column vectors; $EF$ means $F$ first.

**[Statistics and probability](../notes/statistics.md)**
- Conditional probability: divide by the "given" group; without replacement, the second branches change; multiply along paths.
- Histograms: height is frequency density; no gaps; frequency = f.d. × width.
- Cumulative frequency: plot at upper boundaries; median at $\tfrac n2$; IQR, not range; compare with numbers and context.

**[Calculus](../notes/calculus.md)**
- Multiply by the old power, then reduce it; a constant differentiates to 0.
- $-2-1 = -3$; $\tfrac{2}{3x^3} = \tfrac23x^{-3}$; $\sqrt x = x^{1/2}$.
- Expand brackets before differentiating: $(3x-1)^2 \to 18x-6$, not $6x-2$.
- Tangent: $f'(a)$ is the gradient, $f(a)$ is the $y$-coordinate; normal gradient is $-\tfrac1m$.
- Integration: $+C$; divide by the **new** power; expand or simplify first.
- Finding $C$: substitute into the integrated $f(x)$, not $f'(x)$.
- Stationary points: $f''>0$ is a minimum; find the $y$-coordinate too.

## 5-minute final check

Use the last 5 minutes of the paper for this list, not for new questions.

- [ ] Every non-exact answer is **3 s.f.**, or the accuracy the question asked for. Trailing zeros kept (4.40, 40.0°).
- [ ] Angles **1 d.p.**; money **2 d.p.** (\$57.60).
- [ ] Every answer has a **unit**, and hours/minutes are converted properly.
- [ ] Every quadratic has **both roots**, and a context answer has the invalid root **rejected with a reason**.
- [ ] Every trig equation has **all solutions** in the range; every $x^2 = k$ has **±** (unless the quantity must be positive).
- [ ] Every integral has **$+C$**, and every "find $f(x)$" has $C$ worked out and the full function written.
- [ ] Every show-that ends with the unrounded value and the **"Shown"** line.
- [ ] Every geometry step has a **formal reason**; every transformation has **all its facts**.
- [ ] Graph roots read to **1 d.p.**, all intersections found; curves smooth to the end points; tangents labelled.
- [ ] Inequalities have **both ends**; inverse functions start "$f^{-1}(x) =$".
- [ ] No part left blank — follow-through marks need an answer, even from a wrong earlier value.
