# Mensuration

## Unit conversion

**Length** — ×10 per step: mm, cm, dm, m. 1 km = 1000 m.

**Area** — square the length factor:

$$1\ \text{cm}^2 = 10^2 = 100\ \text{mm}^2 \qquad 1\ \text{m}^2 = 100^2 = 10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$$

**Volume** — cube the length factor:

$$1\ \text{cm}^3 = 10^3 = 1000\ \text{mm}^3 \qquad 1\ \text{m}^3 = 100^3 = 1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$$

**Capacity** — 1 litre = 1000 cm³, 1 ml = 1 cm³, 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

Forgetting to square or cube the factor is the commonest error in this topic.

## Area and perimeter

| Shape | Area |
|---|---|
| Rectangle | $lw$ |
| Triangle | $\tfrac12 bh$ |
| Parallelogram | $bh$ |
| Trapezium | $\tfrac12(a+b)h$ |
| Circle | $\pi r^2$ |

Circumference of a circle: $C = 2\pi r = \pi d$.

In a triangle or parallelogram, $h$ is the **perpendicular** height, not a slant side.

**Compound shapes.** Split into standard shapes and add, or take a large shape and subtract a cut-out. State which you are doing.

*Example.* An L-shape: a $10\times6$ rectangle with a $4\times3$ rectangle removed from one corner. Area $= 60 - 12 = 48$ cm². The **perimeter** is not reduced by the cut — add every edge you actually walk along.

## Arcs and sectors

A sector of angle $\theta$ is the fraction $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$ of the whole circle:

$$\text{arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r \qquad \text{sector area} = \frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2$$

The **perimeter** of a sector is the arc **plus the two radii** — a common dropped mark.

*Example.* A sector of radius 9 cm and angle 80°:

- arc $= \tfrac{80}{360}\times2\pi(9) = 12.6$ cm
- area $= \tfrac{80}{360}\times\pi(81) = 56.5$ cm²
- perimeter $= 12.6 + 9 + 9 = 30.6$ cm

A **segment** area = sector area − triangle area, using $\tfrac12 ab\sin C$ for the triangle.

## Volume and surface area

**To recall:**

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid | $lwh$ | $2(lw+lh+wh)$ |
| Prism | cross-section area × length | sum of all faces |
| Cylinder | $\pi r^2h$ | $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$ |

The curved surface of a cylinder alone is $2\pi rh$ — it unrolls into a rectangle of width $2\pi r$ and height $h$. Read the question: "open-topped" means one circle, not two.

**Given in the exam:**

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Sphere | $\tfrac43\pi r^3$ | $4\pi r^2$ |
| Cone | $\tfrac13\pi r^2h$ | $\pi rl$ curved, $+\pi r^2$ base |
| Pyramid | $\tfrac13\times\text{base area}\times h$ | sum of faces |

For a cone, $l$ is the **slant** height, found from $l = \sqrt{r^2+h^2}$, while $h$ is the perpendicular height. Using the wrong one is the usual error.

*Example.* A cone with $r = 5$ cm and $h = 12$ cm:

- $l = \sqrt{25+144} = 13$ cm
- volume $= \tfrac13\pi(25)(12) = 314$ cm³
- total surface area $= \pi(5)(13) + \pi(25) = 204+78.5 = 283$ cm²

## Compound solids

Add or subtract whole solids, and watch which faces remain on the **outside**.

*Example.* A cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm topped by a hemisphere of the same radius.

- Volume $= \pi(9)(10) + \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac43\pi(27) = 283 + 56.5 = 339$ cm³
- Surface area $= \underbrace{2\pi(3)(10)}_{\text{curved}} + \underbrace{\pi(9)}_{\text{base}} + \underbrace{2\pi(9)}_{\text{hemisphere}} = 188 + 28.3 + 56.5 = 273$ cm²

The circle where the hemisphere joins the cylinder is **not** part of the surface — it is internal.

## Working accurately

- Keep $\pi$ on the calculator rather than using 3.14; round only at the end.
- Give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
- Always state the unit, and make sure it matches the dimension: cm for length, cm² for area, cm³ for volume.
- Convert all lengths to the same unit **before** substituting.
