# Maths study guide (exam Tue 27 Oct)

This page tells you **what to study first and how to study it**. It is a priority order, not a timetable: work down the list, and move on when a topic's past-paper questions come out at full marks.

## The exam

| | |
|---|---|
| Paper | *Paper 1: Advanced Mathematics* — one paper |
| When | Tuesday 27 October 2026, 9 am |
| Length | 3 hours, 180 marks (178 in 2024) — about **one mark per minute** |
| Shape | 12 questions, each with many parts, in a very stable order (see [Big ideas](../notes/big-ideas.md)) |

| Accuracy | 3 s.f. unless told otherwise; angles 1 d.p.; money 2 d.p.; calculator $\pi$ |

## How to study maths

Reading notes feels productive but does not stop small errors. Doing questions and marking them strictly does.

1. **Read [Exam errors](../notes/exam-errors.md) before every practice session**, and again on the morning of the exam.
2. **Practise by question type, not by paper.** Take the same question from several years in a row — for example Q4 from 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 — at exam pace, about a mark a minute.
3. **Mark with the mark scheme, line by line.** Award yourself each M, A and S separately. A right answer without the "Shown" line or the reason still loses the S mark.
4. **Log every lost mark in an error log.** One row per mark:

   | Question | Mark lost | Type | Fix (one line) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 2025 Q4(d)(iii) | A | accuracy | read roots to 1 d.p., not 2 |
   | 2024 Q3(c) | A | root/sign | reject $x = -3$ and say why |

   Use these types: *accuracy*, *root/sign*, *units*, *statement*, *method*, *knowledge*. After a few sessions the log shows your personal top three errors — add them to the top of your final check.
5. **Redo every wrong question later**, from a blank page and without the scheme, until it is full marks.
6. **Use Anki only for facts:** formulas, rules, the names of theorems, and traps — the [exam-errors deck](../anki/exam-errors.txt) plus the topic decks. Questions are practised on paper, not on cards.
7. **Finish with a mixed paper.** Once the question types are done, do a mixed set under exam conditions so you practise switching topics and pacing.

**What kind of content needs what method**

| Content | Example | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed chains of steps | completing square → sketch → $f'$ → tangent; table → curve → line → roots; grouped mean | Do the same question from all four years back to back until the layout is automatic |
| Word problems | quadratic from an area or a journey; reverse percentage | Practise *only the forming step* on several questions in a row, then solve one fully; check the answer against the context |
| Facts and formulas | sine rule, cone volume, circle theorem names, $k^2$ and $k^3$ | Anki, plus writing [FORMULAS.md](../FORMULAS.md) out from memory and checking it |
| Statements and reasons | show-that, geometry reasons, transformation facts, collinearity | Write the full sentence every time; compare your wording with the mark scheme's |
| Traps | rounding, ±, $+C$, units | [Exam errors](../notes/exam-errors.md) and its deck; the error log |

## Priority order

Ranked first to last. The reason for each is the marks it carries every year (from the 2022–2025 finals), your own weak points (small errors and big ideas), and what it unlocks.

| # | Study | Why here | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Exam errors](../notes/exam-errors.md) | Your main weakness. Every question has a rounding, sign, unit or statement trap, and each slip is a mark | Read it, learn the [deck](../anki/exam-errors.txt), and start your error log with the first practice |
| 2 | [Big ideas](../notes/big-ideas.md) | Your other weakness. Shows which six question types make up about half the paper, and the ideas that connect them | Before each past question, name the big idea it uses |
| 3 | Quadratics — [Algebra](../notes/algebra.md): quadratic equations, completing the square, forming quadratics from a context, "show that" | Needed in the completing-square chain (12–13 marks), the context quadratics (11–25 marks in 2023–2025), the graph question and functions. Unlocks 3, 4 and 5 | Form the equation from each context in 2023 Q3, 2024 Q2, Q3(c), Q8 and 2025 Q2, Q8; then solve, reject, answer in context |
| 4 | [Calculus](../notes/calculus.md) sections 1–6, plus the completing-square chain | About 12 marks in Q6 every year plus 3 in the chain; the same layout every year — the fastest marks on the paper | Q6 calculus parts and the chain (2022 Q2(e), 2023 Q2(d), 2024 Q3(d), 2025 Q3(c)) from all four mark schemes |
| 5 | The graph question — [Graphs](../notes/graphs.md): drawing a curve from a table, solving equations graphically, rearranging to find the line to draw | Q4 is 13–17 marks every year, mostly method marks for plotting and drawing | Rearrange all four years' equations; plot on graph paper; read roots to 1 d.p. |
| 6 | [Trigonometry](../notes/trigonometry.md) | Q11 is 13–17 marks every year, plus 3-D trig, bearings and trig equations elsewhere | "Which rule" table first; then Q11 from the schemes and the trig sections of the Term 2 exams |
| 7 | [Mensuration](../notes/mensuration.md) | Q12 is 12–14 marks every year; sectors appear in Q11 too. Sphere, cone and pyramid formulas are given | Formulas into Anki; Q12 from all four schemes; watch units (cm³ → litres) and rounding |
| 8 | [Number](../notes/number.md) | Q1 is 12–14 marks every year, mostly Term 1 skills you know — the risk is accuracy (money 2 d.p.) and reverse percentages | Term 1 exams and 2022 CT1; then Q1 from the schemes |
| 9 | [Statistics and probability](../notes/statistics.md) | 9–22 marks a year; very procedural (midpoints, frequency density, cumulative frequency, trees) | Q7 and Q5 from 2022–2024, then 2025 Q5 |
| 10 | Functions — [Algebra](../notes/algebra.md) | 7–9 marks in 2023–2025; short and predictable (evaluate, inverse, composite, solve) | Q6(a) from 2023–2025 and the functions sections of the Term 2 exams |
| 11 | [Geometry](../notes/geometry.md) | 6–10 marks a year; reasons must use formal names; similar figures ($k$, $k^2$, $k^3$) also appear in other questions | Theorem names into Anki; 2024 CT4 and the circle-theorem sections of the Term 2 exams |
| 12 | [Vectors and transformations](../notes/vectors-matrices.md) | Vectors 6–8 marks every year; transformations 10–12 marks in 2022 and 2025 only; no matrix questions in 2022–2025 | Vectors from the schemes; transformations as a checklist of facts (one S mark each) |
| 13 | Everything else | Sequences, sets, bounds, regions, exponential graphs, constructions and loci, stationary points, matrices — small or rare in the finals | Anki decks and one pass through each notes page |

The graphs page as a whole is tier 2 below, but its Q4 sections (row 5) are worth as much as a tier-1 topic, which is why they sit high in this order.

## Past papers to use

The school's **final question papers are not held** — only their mark schemes. The mark schemes show every step, so use them as worked solutions: cover the working, read the question's answer line, and rebuild the method. **Term exams and common tests are full question papers** (no mark schemes held, so check answers against the notes). Term exams cover Terms 1–2 only, so Term 3 topics (mensuration, vectors, transformations, statistics, calculus) can only be practised from the final mark schemes and the textbook.
