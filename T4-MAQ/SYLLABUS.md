# Mathematics — Pre-Q Syllabus

Pre-Q Advanced follows the **IGCSE Extended Mathematics** objectives (codes E1–E9 below). Term 4 is the terminal examination covering the whole year.

Course outline by term:

| Term | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Number, Algebra |
| 2 | Coordinate Geometry, Graphs, Geometry, Trigonometry |
| 3 | Measurement, Vectors, Transformation, Statistics & Probability, Calculus |
| 4 | Terminal examinations |

Formulae for the surface area and volume of a **sphere, pyramid and cone** are given in the exam. Everything else must be recalled — see [FORMULAS.md](FORMULAS.md).

Textbook: Rayner, *Extended Mathematics*, 5th edition, plus the Extras manual. Past papers are a reliable guide to the style of future ones.

## 1. Number

- **E1.1** Identify and use natural numbers, integers, prime numbers, square numbers, common factors and multiples, rational and irrational numbers, real numbers. Express a number as a product of prime factors. Find the LCM and HCF of two or more numbers.
- **E1.2** Use set language, notation and Venn diagrams: $\in$, $\notin$, $A'$, $\varnothing$, $\mathcal{E}$, $\subseteq$, $\subset$, $\cup$, $\cap$, $n(A)$, and set-builder definitions.
- **E1.3** Calculate squares, square roots, cubes and cube roots.
- **E1.4** Use directed numbers in practical situations (temperature changes, flood levels).
- **E1.5** Use vulgar and decimal fractions and percentages; recognise equivalence and convert between the forms, including recurring decimals to fractions.
- **E1.6** Order quantities by magnitude using $=$, $\neq$, $>$, $<$, $\geqslant$, $\leqslant$.
- **E1.7** Understand the meaning and rules of indices. Use standard form $A\times10^n$; convert into and out of it; calculate with it.
- **E1.8** Use the four rules with whole numbers, decimals and mixed fractions, with correct order of operations and brackets.
- **E1.9** Estimate numbers, quantities and lengths; approximate to a given number of significant figures or decimal places; round to a reasonable accuracy for the context.
- **E1.10** Give upper and lower bounds for data stated to a given accuracy, and for solutions of simple problems using such data.
- **E1.11** Ratio and proportion: increase and decrease by a ratio, divide a quantity in a ratio, direct and inverse proportion, common measures of rate, average speed, scales.
- **E1.12** Percentage of a quantity, one quantity as a percentage of another, percentage increase and decrease, reverse percentages.
- **E1.13** Use a calculator efficiently and apply checks of accuracy.
- **E1.14** Calculate times in 24-hour and 12-hour clock; read clocks, dials and timetables.
- **E1.15** Calculate with money and convert currency.
- **E1.16** Personal and small-business finance: earnings, simple interest, compound interest, discount, profit and loss. The compound interest formula is required: $\text{value} = P\left(1+\tfrac{r}{100}\right)^n$.
- **E1.17** Use exponential growth and decay in population and finance contexts (depreciation, bacterial growth).

## 2. Algebra

- **E2.1** Use letters for generalised numbers; substitute into complicated formulae; construct and transform formulae, including where the subject appears twice.
- **E2.2** Manipulate directed numbers; use brackets; extract common factors; expand products. Factorise expressions of the form $ax+bx+kay+kby$, $a^2x^2-b^2y^2$, $a^2+2ab+b^2$, $ax^2+bx+c$.
- **E2.3** Manipulate algebraic fractions; factorise and simplify rational expressions, e.g. $\dfrac{x^2+2x}{x^2+4x+4}$.
- **E2.4** Use positive, negative, zero and fractional indices and the rules of indices.
- **E2.5** Solve linear equations in one unknown; simultaneous linear equations in two unknowns; quadratic equations by factorisation, completing the square or the formula; simple linear inequalities.
- **E2.7** Continue a number sequence; recognise patterns; find the $n$th term of linear, quadratic, cubic and exponential sequences and simple combinations.
- **E2.8** Express direct and inverse variation algebraically and use it to find unknown quantities.
- **E2.12** Use function notation $f(x) = 3x-5$; find inverse functions $f^{-1}(x)$; form composite functions $gf(x) = g[f(x)]$.

## 3. Coordinate geometry and graphs

- **E2.9** Interpret and use graphs in practical situations including travel and conversion graphs. Draw graphs from given data. Apply rate of change to simple kinematics: distance–time and speed–time graphs, acceleration, deceleration, distance as the area under a linear speed–time graph.
- **E2.10** Construct tables of values and draw graphs of $ax^n$ for $n = -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3$ and simple sums of up to three such terms, and of $a^x$ for positive integer $a$. Solve the associated equations graphically. Draw and interpret exponential growth and decay graphs.
- **E2.11** Estimate the gradient of a curve by drawing a tangent.
- **E5.1** Use Cartesian coordinates in two dimensions.
- **E5.2** Find the gradient of a straight line, including from the coordinates of two points.
- **E5.3** Calculate the length and midpoint of a line segment from its end coordinates.
- **E5.4** Interpret and obtain the equation of a straight line in the form $y = mx + c$.
- **E5.5** Determine the equation of a line parallel to a given line.
- **E5.6** Find the gradients of parallel and perpendicular lines, and the equations of such lines.

## 4. Geometry

- **E3.1** Use and interpret geometrical terms: point, line, parallel, bearing, right angle, acute, obtuse, reflex, perpendicular, similarity, congruence. Use the vocabulary of triangles, quadrilaterals, circles, polygons and simple solids including nets.
- **E3.2** Measure lines and angles. Construct a triangle from three sides with ruler and compasses only. Construct other figures from given data. Construct angle bisectors and perpendicular bisectors with straight edge and compasses only.
- **E3.3** Read and make scale drawings.
- **E3.4** Calculate lengths in similar figures. Use the relationships between areas of similar triangles and figures, extending to surface areas and volumes of similar solids.
- **E3.5** Recognise rotational and line symmetry in two dimensions, including the order of rotational symmetry. Recognise symmetry properties of prisms (including the cylinder) and pyramids (including the cone). Use the circle symmetry properties: equal chords are equidistant from the centre; the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre; tangents from an external point are equal in length.
- **E3.6** Calculate unknown angles using: angles at a point; angles on a straight line and at intersecting lines; angles in parallel lines; angle properties of triangles, quadrilaterals, regular and irregular polygons; angle in a semicircle; angle between tangent and radius; angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary. **Correct geometrical terminology is required when giving reasons.**
- **E3.7** Use loci and intersecting loci for points that are: a given distance from a point; a given distance from a straight line; equidistant from two points; equidistant from two intersecting lines.

## 5. Mensuration

- **E4.1** Use current units of mass, length, area, volume and capacity; express quantities in larger or smaller units; convert between units, including units of area and volume.
- **E4.2** Calculate the perimeter and area of a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and trapezium, and of compound shapes derived from these.
- **E4.3** Calculate the circumference and area of a circle. Solve problems with arc length and sector area as fractions of the whole.
- **E4.4** Calculate the volume of a cuboid, prism and cylinder, and the surface area of a cuboid and cylinder. Calculate the surface area and volume of a sphere, pyramid and cone — **these formulae are given in the exam**.
- **E4.5** Calculate areas and volumes of compound shapes.

## 6. Trigonometry

- **E6.1** Interpret and use three-figure bearings, measured clockwise from North, 000°–360°.
- **E6.2** Apply Pythagoras' theorem and the sine, cosine and tangent ratios for acute angles to find a side or an angle of a right-angled triangle. Solve 2-D problems involving angles of elevation and depression. Extend sine and cosine values to angles between 90° and 180°. **Angles are quoted and required in degrees to one decimal place.**
- **E6.3** Solve problems using the sine rule, the cosine rule, and area of triangle $= \tfrac12 ab\sin C$.
- **E6.4** Solve simple 3-D trigonometry problems, including the angle between a line and a plane.

## 7. Vectors, matrices and transformations

- **E7.1** Describe a translation by a vector. Add and subtract vectors. Multiply a vector by a scalar.
- **E7.2** Reflect plane figures. Rotate plane figures through multiples of 90°. Construct translations and enlargements. Recognise and describe reflections, rotations, translations and enlargements, with positive, fractional and negative scale factors.
- **E7.3** Calculate the magnitude of a vector as $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Represent vectors by directed line segments. Use sums and differences of two vectors to express a given vector in terms of two coplanar vectors. Use position vectors. **Indicate a vector clearly in answers — by an arrow or by underlining.**
- **E7.4** Display information as a matrix of any order. Calculate the sum and product of two matrices and the product of a matrix and a scalar. Use the algebra of $2\times2$ matrices including the zero and identity matrices. Calculate the determinant $|A|$ and the inverse $A^{-1}$ of a non-singular matrix.
- **E7.5** Use the transformations reflection (M), rotation (R), translation (T), enlargement (E) and their combinations. Identify and precisely describe the transformation connecting two figures. Describe transformations using coordinates and matrices (singular matrices excluded). Notation: if $M(a)=b$ and $R(b)=c$ then $RM(a)=c$.

## 8. Statistics and probability

- **E8.1** Calculate the probability of a single event as a fraction, decimal or percentage, including from tables and graphs.
- **E8.2** Use the probability scale from 0 to 1.
- **E8.3** Understand that $P(\text{event}) = 1 - P(\text{not event})$.
- **E8.4** Understand relative frequency as an estimate of probability.
- **E8.5** Calculate probabilities of simple combined events using possibility diagrams and tree diagrams.
- **E9.1** Collect, classify and tabulate statistical data. Read, interpret and draw inferences from tables and statistical diagrams.
- **E9.2** Construct and read bar charts, pie charts, pictograms, frequency distributions, histograms with equal **and unequal** intervals, and scatter diagrams. For unequal intervals, area is proportional to frequency and the vertical axis is **frequency density**.
- **E9.3** Calculate the mean, median, mode and range for individual and discrete data, and distinguish their purposes.
- **E9.4** Estimate the mean for grouped and continuous data; identify the modal class.
- **E9.5** Construct and use cumulative frequency diagrams; estimate and interpret the median, percentiles, quartiles and interquartile range.
- **E9.6** Understand positive, negative and zero correlation on a scatter diagram.
- **E9.7** Draw a straight line of best fit by eye.
