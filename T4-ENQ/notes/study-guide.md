# English study guide (exam Wed 28 Oct)

What to study for English, in what order, and how. Work down the priority list: each item is ranked by how many marks it protects. Syllabus and mark schedules: [SYLLABUS.md](../SYLLABUS.md). Terms: [TERMS.md](../TERMS.md).

## The exam

One sitting, three papers, one essay each. The time is not printed on each paper, so one hour each is the working split.

| Paper | Text | Choice | Marks | Time in the exam |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paper 1** | Unseen commentary | Prose **or** poem; both contain a **biblical or mythical allusion** | 25 | About 1 hour: read and choose quickly, plan, write, proofread |
| **Paper 3** | *Othello* (closed book) | General question **or** passage question. Never use the passage in the general answer | 25 | About 1 hour: plan in the first 10 minutes, about 650 words, 3 minutes to proofread |
| **Paper 4** | *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy* (Tomas Alfredson, 2011) | One of 4–5 questions that are **not** text-specific | 25 | About 1 hour: same shape as Paper 3 |

Every essay: name the author or director, write in the present tense, give a thesis that answers the exact question, use short embedded quotations, and analyse more than you describe.

## Priority order

### 1. Othello quotations: [Othello quote bank](othello-quotes.md)

**Why first:** Paper 3 is closed book. Without memorised quotations you cannot score on understanding or conventions, whatever question appears. Quotes also take the longest to stick.

**Method (quotes):**
- Start with the **Top 25**. For each: read it, cover it, say it aloud with **act.scene, speaker, technique, effect**, then check.
- Then the **theme × act matrix**, one theme at a time: cover the quote column and recall from the act and effect, then cover everything and **blurt** the whole theme onto blank paper.
- Then the **by-character** tables, in play order.
- Use the Anki deck (below) every day you study English, so review is spread out rather than crammed.
- Mark any quote you get wrong twice and say those first next time.

### 2. Othello scenes and plot: [Othello key scenes](othello-scenes.md)

**Why second:** the passage question needs you to place an extract instantly and link it to the rest of the play, and the general question needs evidence from every act.

**Method (scenes):**
- Read the one-paragraph plot, then retell it from memory.
- For each scene block: read it, then close the page and list the beats in order and the six quotes.
- For the likely extracts (I.i, III.iii, IV.i, IV.iii, V.i, V.ii), also recall the verse/prose point and the tension methods.
- Background and the techniques: [Othello](othello.md).

### 3. Film story and dialogue: [Film story](film-story.md)

**Why third:** a character question and a technique question appear every year, and you can only answer them with sequences you can describe precisely.

**Method (sequences):**
- Learn the story in **both orders** (chronological and as the film tells it).
- Pick **five or six sequences** you know best (suggested: Budapest, Tarr's story, the Christmas party, Guillam's theft and break-up, the Karla monologue, Haydon's confession and the ending). For each, recall: context in one sentence, **three details from three different categories**, the effect of each, and the theme.
- Learn the confirmed dialogue lines first; treat the *(check against the film)* lines as optional, and rewatch the scenes if you can.
- Film technique vocabulary: [Film analysis](film.md).

### 4. Essay plans (both texts)

**Why fourth:** plans turn knowledge into marks for response to the question and organisation (15 of the 25 on Papers 3 and 4).

**Method (plans):**
- Read the prepared plans first: [Othello quote bank, section (d)](othello-quotes.md), [Othello notes](othello.md) (pain and power, reputation, tension in III.iii), [Film story](film-story.md) and [Film analysis](film.md) (the 2025 questions).
- Then write your **own** plan for each past question below, **without notes**, within the exam's planning time (the first 10 minutes of the hour): thesis, three or four paragraph points in order, two quotes or shots per paragraph. Check it against the notes and fill gaps.

**Past Othello questions to plan**
- *"Othello is either a malevolent villain or the victim of terrible circumstances."* How far do you agree? (2022)
- Passage: Othello's state of mind at the opening of V.ii (2022)
- Examine the ways Shakespeare presents reputation (2023)
- Passage: the relationship between Othello and Desdemona in III.iv (2023)
- Coleridge's *"motiveless malignity"*: how valid is this assessment of Iago? (2024)
- Passage: the characters in I.iii, the Senate (2024)
- Examine the ways imagery and/or symbolism is used throughout the play (2025 T2)
- Passage: the featured characters in IV.ii (2025 T2)
- *"Pain is the unmistakable proof of Iago's own power over his victim."* Examine pain and/or power (2025 T4)
- Passage: tension and suspense in III.iii, *"Did Michael Cassio"* (2025 T4)
- Examine the ways language is used to exploit and/or demean others (2026 T2)
- Passage: relationships between men and women, III.iv (2026 T2)
- Likely new ones: jealousy; deception; women; Iago as a dramatic device; identity

**Past film questions to plan**
- 2021: universal and timeless, or specific to a time and place · transgression of boundaries · characters interest us only when they transform · cinema challenges conventional values · visual symbols
- 2022: a subjective perspective · characters as representatives of a group · the search for a sense of purpose · freedom and/or constraint · place as almost another character
- 2023: the antihero · allusion · power and persuasion in a relationship · material wealth as motivation · sources of conflict
- 2024: symbolism · setting · agency · active and passive characters · conflict furthering the ideas
- 2025: sound · a supporting character · conflict driving narrative and character · cultural and/or historical context · a relationship evolving over time

### 5. Paper 1 allusions and commentary: [Allusions](allusions.md) · [Unfamiliar texts](unfamiliar-texts.md)

**Why fifth:** the allusion has its own 5-mark criterion, and you cannot predict the text, so the method and the allusion bank are what you can prepare.

**Method:**
- Learn the allusions at a glance, then the full explanations of the most common ones (Eden, Cain and Abel, the Flood, the Prodigal Son, Judas, the Crucifixion, Prometheus, Icarus, Narcissus, Orpheus, Pandora, Sisyphus).
- Practise one commentary on a past or practice text using the eight-step method in [Unfamiliar texts](unfamiliar-texts.md): identify the allusion, map the parallel, and say where the text departs from the source.

### 6. Timed essays

**Why sixth (but do not skip):** timing is a skill. A timed essay shows which quotes and sequences you cannot actually recall under pressure.

**Method (timed essays):**
- Write **one timed essay for each paper** before the exam, under exam conditions: one hour, no notes, a question you have not planned.
- Then mark it against the criteria in [SYLLABUS.md](../SYLLABUS.md): did the thesis answer the exact question? Were there quotes from every act (Paper 3) or details from three categories (Paper 4)? Was it about 650 words?
- Add every quote or detail you could not recall to your list for priority 1 or 3.

### 7. Context: [Authors and context](context.md)

**Why seventh:** context helps, but only in one or two sentences per essay, tied to a point. Learn it after the quotes and sequences.

**Method:** for each fact, learn the "how to use it" line, not just the fact. Learn two critics for Othello (Coleridge and Garber first) and the Philby link for the film.

### 8. Writing technique: [Writing](writing.md)

**Why last:** read it, don't card it. Re-read it before any timed essay: thesis shape, paragraph structure, SICK quotation, the banned list.

### Last look before the exam

A **quote blurt**: blank paper, write every Top 25 quote with act.scene, then the six film sequences with three details each. Then reread your essay plans. Nothing new.

## Using Anki for quotes

- Import [anki/quotes.txt](../anki/quotes.txt) with **File → Import** (deck *English::0 Quote bank*). The other English decks are listed in [SYLLABUS.md](../SYLLABUS.md).
- **Answer out loud before revealing.** For a Basic card, say the full quote, then the technique and effect. For a Cloze card, say the missing word **and** the rest of the line.
- Rate honestly: **Again** if any word was wrong. Exact wording matters in a closed-book exam.
- Cards are shuffle-safe: each begins with *Othello:* or *TTSS film:*, so mixing them with other decks is fine.
- Anki keeps quotes in memory; it does not teach you to use them. Pair it with the blurts and plans above.
