# Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy: the full story

*Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy* (Tomas Alfredson, 2011), from John le Carré's 1974 novel. This page covers the plot in both orders, about twenty sequences with what to say about each, the characters, a dialogue bank, the symbols, and plans for the Paper 4 question types. The film technique terms are in [Film analysis](film.md) and [TERMS.md](../TERMS.md); the director and context are in [Authors and context](context.md).

## The story in chronological order

1. **1955, Delhi.** Smiley tries to persuade a captured Soviet officer to defect. The officer, later known to be **Karla**, says nothing, keeps Smiley's cigarette lighter (a gift from Ann), and flies home. *(Told only in Smiley's monologue.)*
2. **The years before 1973.** **Bill Haydon**, a senior Circus officer, has been recruited by Karla and passes British secrets to Moscow. He also has an affair with Smiley's wife **Ann**, which, as he later says, Karla encouraged so that Smiley could not *"see me straight"* *(check against the film)*.
3. **The Circus Christmas party.** The whole service drinks and sings, including the Soviet anthem. Smiley sees Ann in an embrace with another man.
4. **Operation Witchcraft.** **Percy Alleline**, with Haydon, **Roy Bland** and **Toby Esterhase**, runs a secret source of Soviet intelligence through a contact, **Polyakov**. Witchcraft is traded to the Americans. **Connie Sachs** warns that Polyakov is a Moscow agent and is sacked.
5. **Budapest, 1973.** **Control**, the dying head of the Circus, suspects a mole among his five senior men. He sends **Jim Prideaux** to Budapest to meet a Hungarian general who will name the mole. It is a trap: Prideaux is shot and captured. Control and Smiley are forced out, and Control dies soon after.
6. **Istanbul.** **Ricki Tarr**, a field agent, falls in love with **Irina**, the wife of a Soviet trade official, who says she knows the name of the mole and wants to defect. Tarr signals London; the Russians move; Irina is taken. Tarr goes into hiding, hunted by both sides.
7. **The present.** Tarr reaches the civil servant **Oliver Lacon**. Lacon recalls Smiley to investigate, working from outside the Circus with **Peter Guillam** inside it and **Mendel**, a retired policeman.
8. **The investigation.** Smiley reads the files, visits Connie, Prideaux and others, sends Guillam to steal the record from the night of Tarr's signal, and breaks Esterhase.
9. **The trap.** Smiley makes the mole call an emergency meeting with Polyakov at the Witchcraft safe house. Haydon walks in.
10. **The end.** Haydon is held at Sarratt, the Circus training centre, to await exchange to Moscow. He explains himself to Smiley. Prideaux shoots him. Smiley returns to the Circus and takes Control's chair.

## The order the film tells it

The film opens in **Budapest** (event 5), cuts to the forced retirement, then moves forward through the investigation (7–10). Everything else arrives in **flashback**: the Christmas party in fragments, Tarr's Istanbul story told in Smiley's house, Connie's memories, Prideaux's account, and Smiley's Karla story told to Guillam. Smiley's **glasses** help mark the time shifts.

**Why it matters for an essay:** the viewer gets fragments out of order and has to assemble them, **as an intelligence analyst does**. This turns structure into a filmic convention you can analyse for almost any question: subjective perspective, technique, relationship or conflict.

## Essay plans

Each plan: a thesis about the **director's** method, then three or four sequences in film order, each with three details from different categories. Name *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy* (italicised) and Tomas Alfredson in the introduction. More plans (sound, supporting character, conflict, context, relationship) are in [the film notes](film.md), under *The 2025 Term 4 questions*.

### Symbolism

**Thesis:** Alfredson places meaning in objects rather than speech, so that the glasses, the chess pieces, the owl and the Christmas party carry the emotions his characters are trained to hide.

1. **Glasses (retirement):** new glasses and the time shifts; seeing clearly as the film's theme.
2. **Chess pieces (Control's flat):** close-ups of photos taped to pieces; men as pieces in a game.
3. **The owl (school):** sudden violence, reaction shots; innocence destroyed by reflex, foreshadowing Haydon.
4. **The party and *"La Mer"* (ending):** the anthem and the song as aural symbols; the game continues.

### Setting and context

**Thesis:** The film is set in 1973, a Britain in decline whose Establishment had been betrayed from within, and Alfredson's drab, enclosed settings turn that history into an atmosphere of gloom and suspicion.

1. **The Circus room:** windowless, orange-lined, sealed; an institution that hears only itself.
2. **The Christmas party:** the Establishment at play, singing the Soviet anthem.
3. **Haydon's confession:** the Philby case and the Cambridge Five; *"the West has grown so very ugly"* (wording *check against the film*).
4. **Istanbul against London:** colour and warmth abroad; grey London; *"A real war. Englishmen could be proud then"* (*check against the film*). Optional: one hedged sentence on the 2011 context.

### Antihero

**Thesis:** Smiley is an antihero not because he is wicked but because he is the opposite of the spy hero: old, silent, cuckolded and passive, and Alfredson makes stillness itself the source of his power.

1. **Retirement:** silence, swimming alone, glasses; the anti-Bond.
2. **Tarr's story:** *"Hello Ricki"*; he wins by noticing, not acting.
3. **Karla monologue:** the empty chair; Smiley's own *"secret doubt"* (*check against the film*).
4. **The trap and the ending:** he waits in the dark; he takes Control's chair, which is a victory without joy. (Haydon is a second antihero: the charming traitor.)

### Agency, active and passive

**Thesis:** In a world of institutions, characters who act on feeling (Tarr, Prideaux) cause harm or grief, while Smiley, who seems passive, has the most agency because he chooses when to act.

1. **Tarr:** acts against orders (*"I just wanted to bring this one in myself"*) and Irina is taken.
2. **Guillam:** agency surrendered to the service: the theft and the break-up.
3. **Smiley:** apparently passive; reads, listens, waits; the safe house trap.
4. **Prideaux:** the one free act of the film is a killing, and it is grief, not duty.

### Allusion

**Thesis:** Alfredson uses allusion to a children's rhyme, to real history and to popular song to show a deadly game played by men who behave like boys.

1. **The nursery rhyme:** the code names on chess pieces; a counting game turned into a manhunt.
2. **History:** Haydon as a Philby figure; the Cambridge Five.
3. **The Soviet anthem and *"La Mer"*:** borrowed music as irony.
4. **The *"rotten apple"*** (*check against the film*): the Fall in Eden; one rotten man spoils the barrel.

### Subjective perspective

**Thesis:** The film keeps the viewer close to Smiley's restricted point of view and gives the past only in fragments, so we experience the hunt as he does and assemble the truth as an analyst would.

1. **Budapest:** Prideaux's point of view; we read danger from details.
2. **Tarr's flashback:** a story within a story, told by an unreliable narrator.
3. **The Christmas party:** Smiley's memory returns in fragments, each showing more.
4. **Karla monologue:** the enemy exists only in Smiley's telling; the empty chair.

### Conflict

**Thesis:** Alfredson demotes open conflict between East and West in favour of hidden conflict, within the institution, within friendships and within Smiley himself.

1. **Budapest:** the Cold War, and its failure.
2. **The Circus room:** rival men in a sealed room.
3. **Karla monologue:** Smiley against an absent mirror of himself.
4. **Haydon and Prideaux:** the conflict that kills is personal.

### Relationship

**Thesis:** Through the non-linear structure, Alfredson reveals the friendship of Haydon and Prideaux in fragments, so that the viewer pieces together a love and its betrayal the way Smiley pieces together the case.

1. **The Christmas party:** the look between them; warmth and ease.
2. **Budapest:** the betrayal that put Prideaux in danger.
3. **The owl:** Prideaux's trained violence, foreshadowing the end.
4. **Sarratt and *"La Mer"*:** the killing as grief; the montage returns them to the party. (Alternative: Smiley and Ann, through the party, the lighter and Haydon's confession.)
