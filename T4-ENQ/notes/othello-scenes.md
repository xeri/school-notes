# Othello key scenes, beat by beat

One block for each scene likely to be printed as the passage on Paper 3. Each block gives who is on stage, what happens in order, five or six quotations in order, why the scene is in verse or prose, how it builds tension, and what a passage question on it would ask. The quotations by theme are in the [Othello quote bank](othello-quotes.md); the act table and the techniques are in [Othello](othello.md); the terms are in [TERMS.md](../TERMS.md).

**Passage questions so far:** V.ii *"It is the cause"* (2022, state of mind) · III.iv the handkerchief (2023, relationship) · I.iii the Senate (2024, characters) · IV.ii Desdemona kneels to Iago (2025 T2, featured characters) · III.iii *"Did Michael Cassio"* (2025 T4, tension and suspense) · III.iv *"Something, sure, of state"* to Bianca (2026 T2, men and women). Expect **I.i, III.iii, IV.i, IV.iii, V.i or V.ii**.

## The whole plot in one paragraph

In Venice, **Iago**, passed over when **Othello**, the Moorish general, promotes **Cassio**, wakes the senator **Brabantio** at night with the news that his daughter **Desdemona** has eloped with Othello. Before the Senate, Othello answers the charge of witchcraft with the story of their courtship, Desdemona defends her choice, and both are sent to **Cyprus** against the Turks. A storm destroys the Turkish fleet, so the war ends before it begins. On the night of celebration Iago gets Cassio drunk and into a brawl, and Othello dismisses him. Iago advises Cassio to ask Desdemona to plead for him, then uses her pleading to suggest an affair (**III.iii**). **Emilia**, Iago's wife, picks up the handkerchief Desdemona drops and gives it to Iago, who leaves it in Cassio's room. Cassio gives it to his mistress **Bianca**. Othello, convinced by an invented dream, a staged conversation and the handkerchief in Bianca's hand, has a fit, strikes Desdemona in public, calls her a whore, and plans her death while Iago sets **Roderigo** on Cassio. Roderigo fails; Iago kills him. Othello smothers Desdemona in their bed. Emilia exposes Iago, who kills her. Othello recognises his error, tells the Venetians how to remember him, and kills himself. Iago refuses to speak, and Cassio is left to govern Cyprus.

## Double time

The play runs on **two clocks**, a term coined by the critic John Wilson in 1849–50.

- **Short time.** From the landing in Cyprus to the murder takes about **two nights and a day**: the arrival and the brawl (night 1), the temptation (the next day), the murder (night 2). The speed is the point, because Othello is never allowed time to think.
- **Long time.** The dialogue implies weeks: Emilia says her husband *"hath a hundred times / Wooed me to steal it"* (III.iii); Bianca complains, *"What, keep a week away?"* (III.iv); Lodovico arrives from Venice with Othello's recall; Othello claims Desdemona and Cassio have *"the act of shame / A thousand times committed"* (V.ii).
- **How to use it:** the audience feels the rush, while the long-time hints make the alleged affair seem just possible. On the page the affair is impossible, which exposes how little proof Othello needs.

## I.i: night in Venice, Iago wakes Brabantio

**On stage:** Iago, Roderigo; Brabantio *above*, at a window; servants with torches.

**Beats**
- Roderigo complains that Iago took his money and never told him of the marriage.
- Iago's grievance: Cassio, *"a great arithmetician"*, has been promoted over him.
- Iago explains his false service: *"I follow him to serve my turn upon him"*.
- They shout up at Brabantio's house in the dark: *"Thieves, thieves, thieves!"*
- Iago's animal slanders; Brabantio refuses to believe them at first.
- Iago slips away before he can be seen with the plotters; Brabantio calls for torches and his officers.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"Tush, never tell me!"* (Roderigo)
2. *"Preferment goes by letter and affection, / And not by old gradation"*
3. *"I follow him to serve my turn upon him"*
4. *"I am not what I am"*
5. *"an old black ram / Is tupping your white ewe"*
6. *"This accident is not unlike my dream"* (Brabantio)

**Verse and prose:** Iago moves between verse and coarse prose to suit his audience. The bawdiest slanders (*"making the beast with two backs"*) are prose shouted into the dark; Brabantio answers in outraged verse.

**Tension and suspense:** darkness and shouting; a voice that cannot be identified (Garber: *"Iago cannot be seen as he speaks"*); the hero is discussed but **not named** and not on stage; the audience is told *"I am not what I am"* before it meets anyone else.

**A passage question would ask:** how Shakespeare creates tension and suspense; how Iago is presented; the presentation of race; the dramatic effect of the opening.

## I.iii: the Senate

**On stage:** the Duke, senators, Brabantio, Othello, Desdemona (sent for), Iago, Roderigo, officers.

**Beats**
- The Senate weighs reports of the Turkish fleet and sees through the feint on Rhodes.
- Brabantio accuses Othello of winning Desdemona by *"spells and medicines"*.
- Othello's defence: *"a round unvarnished tale"* of how she fell in love with his stories.
- Desdemona arrives and speaks of a *"divided duty"*; Brabantio gives her up.
- Othello is ordered to Cyprus that night; Desdemona asks to go with him.
- Brabantio's parting warning; Othello leaves her in *"Honest Iago"*'s care.
- In prose, Iago tells Roderigo to *"Put money in thy purse"*; then, alone, plans in verse.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"'Tis a pageant / To keep us in false gaze"* (First Senator)
2. *"Most potent, grave, and reverend signiors"* · *"Rude am I in my speech"*
3. *"She loved me for the dangers I had passed, / And I loved her that she did pity them"*
4. *"I do perceive here a divided duty"*
5. *"Look to her, Moor, if thou hast eyes to see: / She has deceived her father, and may thee"*
6. *"I hate the Moor"* ... *"Hell and night / Must bring this monstrous birth to the world's light"*

**Verse and prose:** the Senate speaks formal verse, with the Duke's and Brabantio's sententious rhyming couplets. Iago and Roderigo drop into prose (gulling, money, low comedy). Iago's soliloquy returns to verse and ends on a rhyming couplet.

**Tension and suspense:** a public trial with a racial charge; Desdemona's entrance delayed until Othello has spoken; the state's competence (it reads the Turks' trick) set against its blindness to Iago; Brabantio's couplet planted for later; the soliloquy turns public triumph into private threat.

**A passage question would ask:** the presentation of the characters (2024); Othello's confidence; Desdemona as active or passive; love and marriage.

## II.i: arrival in Cyprus

**On stage:** Montano and gentlemen; Cassio; Desdemona, Emilia, Iago, Roderigo; Othello.

**Beats**
- A storm; news that the Turkish fleet is wrecked; Cassio lands and praises *"The divine Desdemona"*.
- Desdemona, Emilia and Iago land; Iago's rhymed jokes against women while they wait for Othello.
- Cassio takes Desdemona's hand; Iago, aside, plans to trap him.
- Othello arrives: the rapturous reunion.
- Iago persuades Roderigo (prose) that Desdemona loves Cassio.
- Soliloquy: Iago suspects Othello *and* Cassio with Emilia, and plans to make Othello mad with jealousy.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"letting go safely by / The divine Desdemona"* (Cassio)
2. *"You are pictures out of doors, / Bells in your parlors, wildcats in your kitchens"* (Iago)
3. *"With as little a web as this will I ensnare as great a fly as Cassio"* (aside)
4. *"O my fair warrior!"* · *"If it were now to die, / 'Twere now to be most happy"*
5. *"O, you are well tuned now, / But I'll set down the pegs that make this music"* (aside)
6. *"I do suspect the lusty Moor / Hath leaped into my seat"*

**Verse and prose:** courtly verse for the arrivals; Iago's misogynist rhymes; prose to Roderigo; a verse soliloquy.

**Tension and suspense:** the storm as an omen (Garber: storms carry *"an emotional resonance"*); the **asides** let the audience watch Iago watching; the peak of happiness is placed exactly where the fall begins.

**A passage question would ask:** the Othello–Desdemona relationship at its height; Iago's attitude to women; atmosphere.

## II.iii: the brawl and Cassio's dismissal

**On stage:** Othello and Desdemona (briefly); Iago, Cassio, Montano, Roderigo, gentlemen; then Othello again.

**Beats**
- Othello leaves Cassio in charge of the watch and goes to bed with Desdemona.
- Iago gets Cassio drunk; Roderigo provokes him; Cassio wounds Montano.
- The alarm bell; Othello, woken again, demands to know who started it.
- Iago "reluctantly" blames Cassio; Othello dismisses him.
- In prose, Cassio laments his reputation; Iago tells him to petition Desdemona.
- Soliloquy: *"And what's he then that says I play the villain"*; the plan to use Desdemona's goodness.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"I have very poor and unhappy brains for drinking"* (Cassio)
2. *"Are we turned Turks, and to ourselves do that / Which heaven hath forbid the Ottomites?"* (Othello)
3. *"My blood begins my safer guides to rule"* (Othello)
4. *"Cassio, I love thee, / But nevermore be officer of mine"*
5. *"Reputation, reputation, reputation!"* · *"Reputation is an idle and most false imposition"*
6. *"I'll pour this pestilence into his ear"* · *"out of her own goodness make the net / That shall enmesh them all"*

**Verse and prose:** drinking songs and Cassio's drunk talk are prose; Othello restores order in verse; the reputation dialogue is prose (Cassio's disgrace, Iago's cynicism); Iago's soliloquy is verse.

**Tension and suspense:** a night alarm again (like I.i); Othello's marriage interrupted a second time; the first crack in his calm (*"My blood begins"*); Iago's public honesty and private plotting shown side by side.

**A passage question would ask:** reputation; Iago's manipulation; Othello's authority; tension.

## III.iii, part 1: Cassio's suit and *"Ha! I like not that"*

**On stage:** Desdemona, Cassio, Emilia; then Othello and Iago.

**Beats**
- Desdemona promises Cassio she will not give up his cause.
- Cassio leaves as Othello and Iago approach. Iago: *"Ha! I like not that."*
- Desdemona pleads for Cassio; Othello indulges her, then sends her away.
- *"Excellent wretch!"*: Othello's love speech, with its foreboding.
- The echo sequence (*"Indeed?"*, *"Honest, my lord?"*, *"Think, my lord?"*), the 2025 passage.
- Iago's refusal to speak; the *"Good name"* speech.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"Thy solicitor shall rather die / Than give thy cause away"* (Desdemona)
2. *"Ha! I like not that"* (Iago)
3. *"Excellent wretch! Perdition catch my soul / But I do love thee! And when I love thee not, / Chaos is come again"*
4. *"Think, my lord?"* · *"By heaven, thou echo'st me, / As if there were some monster in thy thought / Too hideous to be shown"*
5. *"Men should be what they seem"*
6. *"Good name in man and woman, dear my lord, / Is the immediate jewel of their souls"*

**Verse and prose:** all verse. The verse **splits into shared and broken lines** as Iago echoes and Othello presses him.

**Tension and suspense:** the audience knows the plan from II.iii and waits for **when**, not **whether**; withheld statements; repetition of *honest* and *think*; Othello's own words supply the content (*"some monster in thy thought"*). The dramatic irony is heaviest in *"Men should be what they seem"*.

**A passage question would ask:** tension and suspense (2025); Iago's methods; Othello's state of mind; the relationship before the fall.

## III.iii, part 2: *"O, beware, my lord, of jealousy"* to the handkerchief

**On stage:** Othello and Iago; then Othello alone; Desdemona and Emilia; then Emilia and Iago; Iago alone.

**Beats**
- Iago warns against jealousy, and so plants it.
- He borrows Brabantio's line: Desdemona deceived her father.
- Iago leaves; Othello, alone, already doubts: *"Haply, for I am black"*.
- Desdemona offers her handkerchief for his aching head; he pushes it away and it falls.
- Emilia picks it up and gives it to Iago (*"to please his fantasy"*).
- Iago, alone: he will drop it in Cassio's lodging. *"Trifles light as air."*

**Quotes in order**
1. *"O, beware, my lord, of jealousy! / It is the green-eyed monster which doth mock / The meat it feeds on"*
2. *"I'll see before I doubt; when I doubt, prove"*
3. *"She did deceive her father, marrying you"*
4. *"Haply, for I am black"* · *"O curse of marriage"*
5. *"Your napkin is too little. / Let it alone"* (Othello)
6. *"Trifles light as air / Are to the jealous confirmations strong / As proofs of holy writ"*

**Verse and prose:** verse throughout, including **Othello's first soliloquy**: we now hear his private thoughts, and they sound like Iago's.

**Tension and suspense:** the prop falls unnoticed by the people who matter, and **the audience sees it**; Emilia's choice to hand it over; Iago announces in advance how he will use it, so the dread is about timing.

**A passage question would ask:** jealousy; Othello's state of mind; the presentation of Emilia; symbolism of the handkerchief.

## III.iii, part 3: *"Farewell the tranquil mind"* to *"I am your own for ever"*

**On stage:** Othello and Iago.

**Beats**
- Othello returns, tormented: *"Thou hast set me on the rack."*
- He bids farewell to his military life.
- He seizes Iago by the throat and demands *"ocular proof"*.
- Iago invents Cassio's dream, then claims he saw Cassio wipe his beard with the handkerchief.
- Othello vows revenge (the Pontic Sea); both men kneel.
- Othello makes Iago lieutenant; Iago: *"I am your own for ever."*

**Quotes in order**
1. *"Not poppy nor mandragora ... Shall ever medicine thee to that sweet sleep"* (Iago, as Othello enters)
2. *"Farewell the tranquil mind! Farewell content! ... Othello's occupation's gone"*
3. *"Villain, be sure thou prove my love a whore! / Be sure of it. Give me the ocular proof"*
4. *"In sleep I heard him say 'Sweet Desdemona, / Let us be wary, let us hide our loves'"* (Iago)
5. *"Like to the Pontic Sea, / Whose icy current and compulsive course"*
6. *"Now art thou my lieutenant."* · *"I am your own for ever."*

**Verse and prose:** grand, rhetorical verse. Othello's voice recovers its epic sweep, but now in the service of revenge.

**Tension and suspense:** violence threatened against Iago; evidence that is pure narrative; the kneeling vow staged as a **parody of a marriage** (Garber); the promotion that was Iago's first grievance granted at the moment of his triumph.

**A passage question would ask:** Othello's state of mind; power; the Othello–Iago relationship; tension.

## III.iv: the handkerchief interrogation and Bianca

**On stage:** Desdemona, Emilia, the Clown; Othello; Iago and Cassio; Bianca.

**Beats**
- The Clown's puns on *lie*; Desdemona wonders where the handkerchief is. Emilia: *"I know not, madam."*
- Othello takes her hand (*"This hand is moist, my lady"*) and asks for the handkerchief.
- He tells its magic origin; she says it is not lost and returns to Cassio's suit.
- *"The handkerchief!"* three times; he storms out.
- Emilia on men as stomachs; Desdemona blames *"something ... of state"*; Emilia on jealous souls.
- Cassio gives the handkerchief to Bianca to copy; she suspects *"a newer friend"*.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"Where should I lose that handkerchief, Emilia?"* · *"I know not, madam"*
2. *"That handkerchief / Did an Egyptian to my mother give"* · *"there's magic in the web of it"*
3. *"It is not lost. But what an if it were?"* (Desdemona)
4. *"The handkerchief!"* · *"I pray, talk me of Cassio"*
5. *"They are all but stomachs, and we all but food"* (Emilia)
6. *"This is some token from a newer friend"* · *"I must be circumstanced"* (Bianca)

**Verse and prose:** the Clown speaks prose; the rest is verse. Othello's lines **shorten into exclamations** against Desdemona's longer pleas.

**Tension and suspense:** dramatic irony (we know where the handkerchief is, and so does Emilia); cross-purpose dialogue, so that each speech worsens the other's case; a prop moving into the wrong hands at the end.

**A passage question would ask:** the Othello–Desdemona relationship (2023); men and women (2026 T2); jealousy; symbolism.

## IV.i: the fit and the eavesdropping

**On stage:** Othello and Iago; Cassio; Bianca; Lodovico, Desdemona, attendants.

**Beats**
- Iago's obscene suggestions; Othello breaks into prose and falls into a fit.
- Iago gloats over him: *"Work on, / My medicine, work!"*
- Iago hides Othello to overhear Cassio laughing (about Bianca, though Othello thinks it is Desdemona).
- Bianca throws the handkerchief back at Cassio.
- Othello and Iago plan the murders: *"Strangle her in her bed."*
- Lodovico brings letters recalling Othello; Othello strikes Desdemona in public.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"Lie with her? Lie on her? ... Handkerchief—confessions—handkerchief!"*
2. *"Work on, / My medicine, work! Thus credulous fools are caught"* (Iago)
3. *"Look how he laughs already!"* (Othello, aside)
4. *"I will chop her into messes! Cuckold me!"* · *"But yet the pity of it, Iago!"*
5. *"Devil!"* · *"I have not deserved this"* (Desdemona)
6. *"Is this the noble Moor whom our full Senate / Call all in all sufficient?"* (Lodovico)

**Verse and prose:** Othello collapses into **prose**, Iago's register, which is the clearest sign of the takeover. Lodovico's verse frames the public verdict on him.

**Tension and suspense:** a hidden listener with asides; a staged scene that the audience knows is misread; a public blow witnessed by Venice's ambassador.

**A passage question would ask:** Othello's state of mind; Iago's power; dramatic irony; violence.

## IV.ii: the brothel scene; Desdemona kneels to Iago

**On stage:** Othello and Emilia; Desdemona; then Iago; then Roderigo.

**Beats**
- Othello questions Emilia, who swears Desdemona is honest; he dismisses her as a bawd.
- He accuses Desdemona: the fountain and the toads; *"whore"*.
- He pays Emilia as if leaving a brothel.
- Desdemona asks Iago for help: *"Am I that name, Iago?"*
- Emilia describes the slanderer exactly; Iago: *"there is no such man"*.
- Desdemona kneels and swears her love; Iago soothes her, then sets Roderigo on Cassio.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"This is a subtle whore, / A closet lock and key of villainous secrets"*
2. *"a cistern for foul toads / To knot and gender in"*
3. *"Was this fair paper, this most goodly book, / Made to write 'whore' upon?"*
4. *"Am I that name, Iago?"*
5. *"I will be hanged if some eternal villain, / Some busy and insinuating rogue ... Have not devised this slander"* (Emilia)
6. *"Unkindness may do much, / And his unkindness may defeat my life, / But never taint my love"*

**Verse and prose:** verse for Othello's accusation and Desdemona's vow; prose returns with Roderigo's complaints.

**Tension and suspense:** dramatic irony at its height, as Emilia describes the villain to his face; Desdemona kneels to the man destroying her; the word *whore* repeated until it is unbearable.

**A passage question would ask:** the featured characters (2025 T2); language that demeans; women; Iago's hypocrisy.

## IV.iii: the willow scene

**On stage:** Othello, Lodovico (briefly); Desdemona and Emilia.

**Beats**
- Othello orders Desdemona to bed and to dismiss Emilia.
- Desdemona asks to be shrouded in her wedding sheets if she dies first.
- She remembers her mother's maid Barbary and sings the willow song.
- *"Wouldst thou do such a deed for all the world?"*: the talk turns to unfaithful wives.
- Emilia's speech on husbands' faults.
- Desdemona's closing couplet.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"We must not now displease him"*
2. *"If I do die before thee, prithee shroud me / In one of these same sheets"*
3. *"My mother had a maid called Barbary"* · *"Sing all a green willow"*
4. *"The world's a huge thing. It is a great price / For a small vice"* (Emilia)
5. *"I do not think there is any such woman"*
6. *"Let husbands know / Their wives have sense like them"* (Emilia)

**Verse and prose:** a quiet, domestic verse scene, with song and some prose banter; it ends on Desdemona's rhyming couplet (*"Not to pick bad from bad, but by bad mend!"*).

**Tension and suspense:** **delay**: a calm, intimate scene placed just before the murder; the sheets, the song and the itching eyes as **omens**; the audience knows what Desdemona does not.

**A passage question would ask:** women and marriage; atmosphere; the relationship between Desdemona and Emilia.

## V.i: the ambush in the dark

**On stage:** Iago, Roderigo, Cassio; Othello (briefly, apart); Lodovico, Gratiano; Bianca, Emilia.

**Beats**
- Iago places Roderigo to ambush Cassio; aside, he wants both dead.
- Roderigo attacks; Cassio wounds him; Iago wounds Cassio from behind.
- Othello, hearing Cassio's cries, praises *"brave Iago"* and goes to kill Desdemona.
- Iago returns with a light and kills Roderigo, as if he were a stranger.
- Bianca arrives and is blamed.
- *"This is the night / That either makes me or fordoes me quite."*

**Quotes in order**
1. *"I have rubbed this young quat almost to the sense, / And he grows angry"* (Iago)
2. *"He hath a daily beauty in his life / That makes me ugly"* (Iago)
3. *"Thy bed, lust-stained, shall with lust's blood be spotted"* (Othello)
4. *"O damned Iago! O inhuman dog!"* (Roderigo)
5. *"I am no strumpet, but of life as honest / As you that thus abuse me"* (Bianca)
6. *"This is the night / That either makes me or fordoes me quite"* (Iago)

**Verse and prose:** mostly broken verse, full of cries and short lines in the dark.

**Tension and suspense:** darkness, confusion and voices without faces (like I.i); the plotter must now kill with his own hands; the final couplet tells us that everything is at stake.

**A passage question would ask:** tension and suspense; Iago's desperation; atmosphere.

## V.ii, part 1: the murder

**On stage:** Othello and Desdemona (asleep in bed); Emilia at the door.

**Beats**
- Othello enters with a light: *"It is the cause"*; he kisses her.
- She wakes; he tells her to pray.
- He accuses her of giving the handkerchief to Cassio; she begs him to ask Cassio.
- Told that Cassio is dead, she weeps; he takes it as guilt.
- She begs for time; he smothers her as Emilia knocks.
- Desdemona revives briefly and blames herself, then dies.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"It is the cause, it is the cause, my soul"*
2. *"Put out the light, and then put out the light"* · *"Promethean heat"*
3. *"Have you prayed tonight, Desdemona?"* · *"I would not kill thy soul"*
4. *"That death's unnatural that kills for loving"* (Desdemona)
5. *"Kill me tomorrow; let me live tonight!"* · *"It is too late"*
6. *"Nobody. I myself. Farewell"* · *"She's like a liar gone to burning hell! / 'Twas I that killed her"*

**Verse and prose:** solemn verse. Othello's control is recovered after IV.i, but it is a **ritual** control. The verse fractures into short pleas and broken lines at the killing.

**Tension and suspense:** the bed (marriage bed turned deathbed); the candle as a prop for the life; Desdemona's pleas as **delay**; the knocking at the door just too late.

**A passage question would ask:** Othello's state of mind (2022); the relationship; tension.

## V.ii, part 2: Emilia's revelation to Othello's final speech

**On stage:** Othello, Emilia; Montano, Gratiano, Iago; later Lodovico, Cassio (wounded), officers.

**Beats**
- Othello claims Cassio slept with her: *"Ask thy husband."* Emilia: *"My husband?"*
- Emilia cries murder; the others enter; she accuses Iago and refuses to be silent.
- She reveals the handkerchief; Iago stabs her and runs. She dies singing *"Willow"*.
- Othello's grief: *"O ill-starred wench!"*; Iago is brought back; Othello wounds him.
- Iago refuses to speak; letters prove the plot. *"O fool, fool, fool!"*
- *"Soft you"*: the final speech; he stabs himself and dies upon a kiss. Cassio is made governor.

**Quotes in order**
1. *"My friend, thy husband; honest, honest Iago"* (Othello)
2. *"O gull! O dolt! / As ignorant as dirt!"* (Emilia)
3. *"'Tis proper I obey him, but not now"* (Emilia)
4. *"I look down towards his feet; but that's a fable"*
5. *"Demand me nothing. What you know, you know"* (Iago)
6. *"Speak of me as I am ... Of one that loved not wisely, but too well"* · *"I kissed thee ere I killed thee"*

**Verse and prose:** verse throughout. Othello's last speech is his grandest blank verse since Act I, a **self-portrait in verse** before his death.

**Tension and suspense:** the truth emerging line by line (*"My husband?"* repeated); the woman ordered to be silent who will not be; the villain who stops speaking; the bed displayed: *"Look on the tragic loading of this bed"* (Lodovico).

**A passage question would ask:** Othello's state of mind; Emilia as the moral voice; recognition (anagnorisis); hero or victim.
