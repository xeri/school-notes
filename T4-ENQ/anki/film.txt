#deck:English::3 Paper 4 film analysis
The studied film, its director and year	<i>Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy</i>, directed by Tomas Alfredson, 2011
Paper 4 format	One hour, 25 marks, one essay. The 2026 details give four questions; every paper from 2021 to 2025 gave five
Are the Paper 4 questions text-specific?	No. They suit any studied film, so you memorise sequences and re-angle them
Paper 4 question types set every year, 2021 to 2025	A character question and a technique question; conflict also appeared in 2023, 2024 and 2025
The five suspects and their code names	Tinker Alleline, Tailor Haydon, Soldier Bland, Poorman Esterhase, Beggarman Smiley
Who is the mole in <i>Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy</i>?	Bill Haydon (Colin Firth), code name Tailor
The two contexts of <i>Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy</i>	Set in 1973: the Cold War, British decline, the Philby case. Made in 2011
The real spy behind Bill Haydon	Kim Philby, the MI6 insider exposed as a Soviet agent, who defected in 1963
Budapest opening: what happens	Prideaux meets a contact in a café, reads the signs of a trap (a sweating waiter, a face at a window), and is shot
Budapest opening: what it establishes	Surveillance is mutual, and information arrives as small visual detail rather than speech
Low-key lighting: effect and sequence	Threat and concealment; Tarr's face hidden in the dark when Smiley finds him waiting at home
Frame within a frame: effect and sequence	Surveillance and entrapment; Circus figures seen through glass, doorways and corridors
Production design: the soundproofed room	Windowless and padded in orange: an institution that can hear only itself, so it cannot detect the mole
Props: Control's chess pieces	Suspects' faces taped on: men reduced to pieces in a game
Symbol: Smiley's glasses	Seeing clearly; his change of frames separates past from present
Performance: Smiley's stillness	He barely speaks in the opening stretch; patience and watching are his weapons
Non-linear structure: the Christmas party	A flashback that returns in fragments, revealing more each time, ending with a look between Haydon and Prideaux
Diegetic sound as irony: the Christmas party	The Circus sings the Soviet anthem, led by Father Christmas in a Lenin mask
Non-diegetic irony: the ending	Julio Iglesias's <i>La Mer</i> over the closing montage: romantic warmth laid over loneliness and death
Absent antagonist: Karla	Never seen. Smiley describes him to an empty chair, so the great enemy is an absence
Long take: Smiley's Karla monologue	An unbroken, unscored take that makes the viewer place Karla in the empty chair
Story within a story: Ricki Tarr	Tarr recounts Istanbul and Irina in flashback from Smiley's house; love is punished
Elliptical editing: Guillam's break-up	The conversation itself is omitted; the cost of secrecy shown through what is not seen
Suspense through duration: the logbook theft	Guillam takes the file from the Circus archive in near silence, under his colleagues' eyes
Symbol: the owl in the classroom	A trapped bird killed by Prideaux's trained reflexes; it foreshadows his killing of Haydon
Circular structure: the final shot	Smiley takes the head chair in the Circus room, as Control did: the game continues
Symbol: Ann's lighter	Smiley's gift from Ann, kept by Karla: love as Smiley's weak point
Haydon's stated motive	A choice that was aesthetic as much as moral, because the West had grown ugly
Best supporting characters for a Paper 4 question	Ricki Tarr (the catalyst), Peter Guillam (the cost), Connie Sachs (memory), Jim Prideaux
Effect of a long lens	It compresses depth and feels distant and observational: the look of surveillance
Effect of shallow focus	It isolates a figure and makes the surroundings irrelevant or unknowable
Effect of a rack focus	It redirects attention without a cut, implying a link between two planes
Effect of a high angle and a low angle	A high angle diminishes; a low angle confers stature or threat
Diegetic or non-diegetic: the test	Could a character in the film hear it?
Effect of a sound bridge	Sound carried across a cut stitches scenes or timelines and implies a link
Effect of a match cut	It joins two shots by visual or aural likeness, asserting an equivalence
Effect of cross-cutting	Alternating between simultaneous actions builds tension and forces comparison
Effect of a withheld reverse shot	It denies the answering view, so the viewer is refused a reaction
The shape of an analytical sentence about film	Name the technique, describe it precisely, state the effect on the viewer, connect it to the question
What to record for each prepared sequence	Context in one sentence, three details from three categories, one effect each, one thematic link
How to phrase a Paper 4 thesis	As a claim about the director's method, not about what the characters do
