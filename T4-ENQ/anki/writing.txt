#deck:English::4 Writing and argument
Which papers are sat in Term 4?	Papers 1, 3 and 4, one hour and 25 marks each; Paper 2 is sat in Term 2
Weight of each paper in the final Pre-Q English grade	25% each: Paper 2 from Term 2, Papers 1, 3 and 4 from Term 4
Word target for a one-hour essay	About 650 words. The literature schedule caps essays under 500 words at 12/25
Marks decided by how you write on Papers 3 and 4	Response to the question, organisation and development, expression: 15 of the 25
The three parts of every question	The command word, the focus noun, and the stated limits
What must you do with an and/or question?	Choose deliberately, and say in the thesis which you have chosen
How to treat a quoted critical comment	Agree, disagree, or agree with qualifications; qualifying scores best
What does examine the ways demand?	The methods and the effect of each; ways is plural, so at least three
What does to what extent or how far do you agree demand?	A judgement of degree: largely, because of X, but not in respect of Y
What is a thesis?	A statement of informed opinion that answers the question and could be argued with
Where does the thesis go, and how does it open?	The last sentence of the introduction, opened with Ultimately, Thus or Therefore
Three steps to building a thesis	Write the focus noun, ask what the text finally claims about it, phrase it with an intention verb
Topic or theme in a thesis?	Theme. A topic is one word, such as race; a theme says what the text claims about race
The four moves of an introduction	Hook, transition, rationale, thesis
The hook, in the school's definition	An opening statement introducing a broad, universally applicable idea about a facet of human experience
What must the transition contain?	The title and the writer, playwright or director, named accurately, narrowing the hook to the text
What does the rationale avoid?	Listing devices you will analyse, and firstly or secondly
Extra move in a passage-based introduction	One or two sentences placing the extract in the plot, without retelling it
The five moves of a body paragraph	Point, evidence, analysis, explanation, link
The school's P.E.E. ratio	Point and evidence carry 25% of the weight; exploration carries 75%
Analysis and explanation: the difference	Analysis shows how conventions imply meaning beyond the surface; explanation shows how that proves the point, at theme level
What the link sentence does	Shows how the paragraph answers the question and develops the thesis, reusing the question's words
Features and quotations per paragraph	At least two language features from different categories; three to five short quotations
The colon trick	After a vague abstract word, add a colon and say exactly what you mean
Three levels every paragraph must pass through	Language, then literary or filmic element such as character or structure, then theme
The how? failure	Wiring a device straight to a theme without passing through character or structure
The so what? failure	Stopping the analysis at the character and never reaching the theme
SICK quotations	Selective, Integrated, Contextualised, with Knowledge of purpose
Three ways to integrate a quotation	A dialogue tag and comma, a colon after a full clause, or flowing it into your own syntax
How long should a quotation be?	Four or five words: keep only what would make it meaningless if removed
The two moves of a conclusion	Summation, then contemporary connect
Contemporary connect	How the text, and the broad idea it yields, matters to the reader in a moral or philosophical way
Three rules for conclusions	No new evidence, no sentence repeated verbatim, and write it even if a paragraph is unfinished
Whose context should you use?	Context contemporary to the writer; anachronistic links to current events are a named fault
Which tense for the text, and which for history?	Present tense for the text, past tense for historical events
What to write instead of this quote shows	Name the device and its mechanism, or use a precise verb: conveys, connotes, exposes, intimates
What is a comma splice?	Two independent clauses joined by only a comma, the commonest expression error
Register faults to proofread for	Contractions, colloquial you, slang, and abbreviations such as etc. and e.g.
Who makes a play, a film and a novel?	A playwright, a director and a novelist; a play or film is never a book
The minutes of a one-hour essay	0–5 choose and annotate; 5–12 plan; 12–52 write; 52–57 conclude; 57–60 proofread
How many body paragraphs?	Four as the target, three as the minimum
Why can excellent expression still fail?	Expression cannot reach 5/5 if the material is not relevant to the question
