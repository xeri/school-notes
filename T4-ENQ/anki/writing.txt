#deck:English::4 Writing and argument
Which papers are sat in Term 4?	Papers 1, 3 and 4; Paper 2 is excluded
Time and marks for each Term 4 paper	One hour and 25 marks each
Total time and marks for the three-paper set in 2025	Three hours and 75 marks
The three parts of every question	The command word, the focus noun, and the stated limits
What does analyse demand?	Break the method into parts and explain how each produces its effect
What does discuss demand?	A reasoned case with more than one side acknowledged, then a position reached
What does evaluate demand?	A judgement made against stated criteria for success or failure
What does explore demand?	Following an idea through the text, including where it complicates itself
What does examine the ways demand?	Identify the methods and analyse the effect of each; ways is plural, so at least three
What does to what extent demand?	A judgement of degree: largely, because of X, but not in respect of Y
What does how does the writer demand?	Method only, with the writer as the subject of your sentences
What does comment on how, and with what effects demand?	Two things: the method, and its effect on the reader or viewer
What does compare demand?	Sustained two-way comparison inside each paragraph, not one text and then the other
What does with this comment in mind demand?	That you test the quoted position and say how far it holds
What must you do with an and/or question?	Choose one, and say in the thesis which you have chosen
What is a thesis?	A single arguable sentence stating an informed position in answer to the question
Why is naming a device not a thesis?	It makes no claim anyone could disagree with
Where does the thesis go?	As the final sentence of the introduction
Three steps to building a thesis	Write the focus noun, ask what the text finally claims about it, phrase it with an intention verb
Examples of author-intention verbs	Exposes, undercuts, qualifies, interrogates, satirises, legitimises, refuses, complicates
The four moves of an introduction	Hook, transition, rationale, thesis
What is the hook?	A broad, universally applicable claim about human experience, which the next sentence narrows to the text
What must the transition contain?	The title and the writer, playwright or director, named accurately
What is the rationale?	A brief indication of the sequence of points to come, in the order they will come
Extra move for a passage-based introduction	One or two sentences placing the extract in the plot, without retelling the story
How much evidence belongs in the introduction?	None
The five moves of a body paragraph	Point, evidence, analysis, explanation, link
How is the weight distributed in a paragraph?	Point and evidence carry about a quarter; the exploration carries the rest
How many language features per paragraph?	At least two, from different categories
How many short quotations per paragraph?	Three to five
Three levels every paragraph must pass through	Language, then literary or filmic element such as character or structure, then theme
The how failure	Wiring a device straight to a universal theme without passing through character or narrative
The so what failure	Stopping the analysis at the character and never reaching the argument
Three legal ways to embed a quotation	Dialogue tag with a comma, a colon after an independent clause, or flowing it into your own syntax
What do ellipses and square brackets do in a quotation?	Ellipses mark what you cut; square brackets mark a word, pronoun or tense you altered
How do you contextualise a quotation?	By speaker, moment and circumstance, not by page number
How long should a quotation be?	Four or five words: keep only what would be meaningless if removed
Why are long quotations costly?	They consume time, fill the page without earning credit, and show you have not decided which word matters
Test for plot summary	Ask whether the sentence could appear in a plot summary; if it could, rewrite or delete it
What counts as a choice even with no obvious device?	Selection, placement, omission, repetition, contrast and sequence
Three ways to handle counter-argument	Concede and qualify, name a competing reading and adjudicate, or argue against the premise
Useful concession markers	Although, While it is true that, Even though, It may well be that, Nonetheless
The two moves of a conclusion	Summation of the thesis as demonstrated, then a widening into moral or philosophical terms
Three rules for conclusions	No new evidence, no sentence repeated verbatim, and write it even if a paragraph is unfinished
Whose present should the widening belong to?	The writer's own moment; anachronistic links to the reader's present are a named fault
What is a comma splice?	Two independent clauses joined by only a comma, the commonest expression error in timed essays
Which tense for the text, and which for history?	Present tense for the text, past tense for historical events
What is banned instead of this quote shows?	Name the device and describe its mechanism, or use a precise verb such as conveys, connotes or undercuts
Which words count as vague praise?	Strong imagery, effective scene, powerfully written, interesting, important
Which words are assertion adverbs?	Clearly, obviously, undoubtedly, used in place of argument
Who makes a play, a film and a novel?	A playwright, a director and a novelist; never call a film's maker an author
Minutes 0 to 5 of the hour	Read all the questions, choose, and annotate the command word, focus noun and limits
Minutes 5 to 12 of the hour	Plan four or five point sentences with two or three pieces of evidence each, and draft the thesis
Minutes 12 to 52 of the hour	Write, at roughly eight minutes per paragraph
Minutes 52 to 57 of the hour	Write the conclusion: summation and widening
Minutes 57 to 60 of the hour	Proofread for comma splices, contractions, tense slippage and misspelt names or titles
How many body paragraphs should you aim for?	Four as the target, three as the floor
What do you do if time runs out mid-paragraph?	Stop and write the conclusion; a complete argument outscores an incomplete one
Why can excellent expression still fail?	An essay with excellent expression cannot score full marks if the material is not relevant to the question
What must a passage-based essay show beyond the extract?	Knowledge of the wider text, which is credited under three of the five criteria
