#deck:English::1 Paper 1 unfamiliar texts
Paper 1 format	One hour, 25 marks, one commentary on either an unfamiliar prose extract or an unfamiliar poem
How many of the two Paper 1 texts do you write about?	One only
Usual wording of the Paper 1 focus question	Comment on how, and with what effects, the author presents X
What is guaranteed to appear in both Paper 1 texts?	At least one biblical or mythical allusion
The five Paper 1 criteria	Understanding and interpretation, appreciation of literary conventions, identification of allusion, organisation and development, expression
Marks carried by the allusion criterion	Five of the twenty-five
Decisive tiebreaker when choosing between the prose and the poem	Which text's allusion you can actually name and use
Deadline for choosing your text	The first three minutes, and never revisit the decision
Definition of enjambment	A sentence or phrase running over the end of a line without a pause
Definition of caesura	A distinct pause inside a line of verse
Definition of end-stopping	A line that closes on its own punctuation, creating finality or flatness
Definition of a volta	The turn in a poem's argument, where its direction changes
Definition of a refrain	A line or phrase repeated across stanzas, meaning something new on each return
Definition of metre	The repeating pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in a line
What is an iamb?	An unstressed syllable followed by a stressed one
What is iambic pentameter?	Five iambs per line, the metre closest to English speech
Definition of blank verse	Unrhymed iambic pentameter
Effect of half rhyme	It raises the expectation of a chime and then denies it, producing unease or incompleteness
Definition of assonance	Repetition of vowel sounds in nearby words
Definition of consonance	Repetition of consonant sounds anywhere within nearby words, not only at the start
Assonance or consonance: which repeats vowels?	Assonance repeats vowels; consonance repeats consonants
Definition of sibilance	Repetition of s, sh and z sounds
Effect of sibilance	Hush, whisper, menace, or the noise of air or water in motion
Which consonants are plosives?	p, b, t, d, k and g
Effect of plosives	Abrupt, percussive interruption
Which consonants are fricatives?	f, v and th
Effect of fricatives	Breathy, continuous, sustained sound
Definition of onomatopoeia	A word whose sound imitates the thing it names
Simile or metaphor: which asserts an identity?	The metaphor; a simile admits the comparison is a comparison by using like or as
What can be personified?	Only non-living things and abstractions; a human character cannot be personified
Definition of pathetic fallacy	Landscape, weather or surroundings presented as reflecting human emotion
Definition of a semantic field	A cluster of words drawn from one domain, such as commerce, disease or warfare
Definition of connotation	The associations a word carries beyond its literal sense
Tone or mood: which belongs to the voice?	Tone is the speaker's attitude to the subject; mood is the feeling of the world of the text
Definition of anaphora	Repetition of the same words at the beginning of successive clauses or lines
Definition of epistrophe	Repetition of the same words at the end of successive clauses or lines
Definition of epizeuxis	Immediate repetition of a word for pressure
Definition of asyndeton	Omission of conjunctions from a list, which accelerates it
Definition of polysyndeton	Piling up of conjunctions, which slows a list and makes it feel endless
The four moves of the allusion paragraph	Identify and name it, explain the source, map the parallel, analyse the divergence
Test before claiming an allusion	Two independent signals in the text, not one
What to do when an allusion is possible but thin	Say so in a clause and analyse the image on its own terms
Signals of a garden, a wall, forbidden fruit, nakedness	Eden and the Fall: innocence lost, knowledge as transgression, exile, the origin of shame
Signals of a jar or box not to be opened, or a forbidden door	Pandora: curiosity, blame, released suffering, and hope left at the bottom
Signals of wings, wax, flying too high, the sea	Icarus and Daedalus: hubris, the cost of ambition, a father's failed protection
Signals of a maze, a monster, a thread	Theseus and the Minotaur: being lost, guidance, the monster at the centre
Signal of a backward glance that loses her	Orpheus and Eurydice: grief, the fatal look back, art set against death
Signal of a rock rolled uphill for ever	Sisyphus: futile, repeated labour
Signal of fruit or water always out of reach	Tantalus: desire structurally frustrated
Signal of prophecy nobody believes	Cassandra: truth without credibility
Signals of a kiss, silver, an intimate betrayal	Judas: treachery bought, betrayal from inside
Signals of rain, a flood, an ark, a dove	Noah: cleansing destruction, covenant, beginning again
Signals of a tower, hubris, confused speech	Babel: overreach and the fragmentation of understanding
Signals of a son returning and a father forgiving	The Prodigal Son: waste, return, unearned grace
Signal of suffering without cause	Job: undeserved affliction, faith tested to breaking
The shape of an analytical sentence	Name the device, describe its mechanism, state the effect, state the implied meaning
Marks for naming a device without analysing it	One or two out of five on the conventions criterion
The most praised observation in examiner comments	Tracking how and where the tone shifts
How long should a quotation be?	Four or five words: keep only what would make it meaningless if removed
Three levels every paragraph must pass through	Language, then literary element such as character or structure, then theme
