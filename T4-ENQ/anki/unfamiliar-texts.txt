#deck:English::1 Paper 1 unfamiliar texts
Paper 1 format	One hour, 25 marks, one commentary on either an unfamiliar prose-fiction extract or an unfamiliar poem
Usual wording of the Paper 1 focus question	Comment on how, and with what effects, the author presents X
What is guaranteed to appear in both Paper 1 texts?	At least one biblical or mythical allusion, to be identified and analysed
The five Paper 1 criteria	Understanding and interpretation, literary conventions, allusions, organisation and development, expression: 5 marks each
What earns 5/5 for Paper 1 understanding and interpretation?	Detailed textual knowledge and perceptive understanding of the text's implied meanings
What earns 5/5 on the Paper 1 allusion criterion?	The allusion perceptively identified and analysed in detail, with sensitive appreciation of its effect
Tiebreaker when choosing between the prose and the poem	The text whose allusion you can actually name and use
Three margin questions before any analysis	Who speaks? What is the situation? Where does it change?
What do the examiner comments on every school model commentary praise?	Recognising a shift in tone or attitude
Five things to mark on the second read	The allusion, the turn, repeated words and sounds, anything anomalous, the first and last lines
Signals of a tonal shift	A change in sentence length, tense or person, or register; the first question or imperative; concrete becoming abstract
Where does a Paper 1 thesis usually come from?	The structural turn: what the text finally claims about the focus noun
A reliable paragraph order for a commentary	Structure, imagery, diction and sound, allusion, then the close
How the school's model commentaries open	A broad, arguable claim about the subject, narrowed to the text; the thesis last, after Ultimately or Therefore
What gains nothing at the start of a commentary?	Defining the audience and purpose of the text
The four moves of the allusion paragraph	Identify and name it, explain the source, map the parallel, analyse the divergence
Test before claiming an allusion	Two independent signals in the text, not one
What to do when an allusion is possible but thin	Hedge it in a clause, then analyse the image on its own terms
Allusion: a garden, a wall, forbidden ground, fruit	Eden and the Fall: innocence lost, knowledge as transgression, exile
Allusion: a jar not to be opened, troubles flying out	Pandora: curiosity, blame, released suffering, hope left behind
Allusion: one forbidden door or room	Bluebeard: curiosity punished, violence hidden behind a husband's wealth
Allusion: wings, wax, flying too high	Icarus and Daedalus: hubris, the cost of ambition, a father's failed protection
Allusion: a backward glance that loses her	Orpheus and Eurydice: grief, the fatal look back
Allusion: stolen fire, endless punishment	Prometheus: defiant gift, suffering for knowledge
Allusion: two brothers, jealousy, a killing	Cain and Abel: the first violence, the mark of the outcast
Allusion: a kiss, silver, an intimate betrayal	Judas: treachery bought, betrayal from inside
Allusion: a rock rolled uphill for ever	Sisyphus: futile, repeated labour
Allusion: fruit or water always out of reach	Tantalus: desire structurally frustrated
The allusion in the Destructors exemplar	Light appears as the boys work with the seriousness of creators: Genesis, casting the boys as God
2024 Paper 1 texts and their allusions	<i>eden</i> by Jim Crace (Eden); <i>Ajar</i> by A. E. Stallings (Pandora, named outright)
2025 Paper 1 texts and their allusions	<i>Crime and Punishment</i> (Christian faith and suffering); Atwood's <i>Ice Palace</i> (Eden, Bluebeard, Beauty and the Beast)
The shape of an analytical sentence	Name the device, describe its mechanism, state the effect, state the implied meaning
Score for naming conventions without analysing them	The 1 to 2 band out of 5 on the conventions criterion
Effect of enjambment	Momentum, hesitation, or a double reading of the last word before the break
Effect of a caesura	It interrupts the line, or splits it into two opposed halves
Where the volta falls in a sonnet	At line nine in a Petrarchan sonnet; at the closing couplet in a Shakespearean one
Effect of half rhyme	It raises the expectation of a chime and denies it: unease or incompleteness
Plosives and their effect	p, b, t, d, k, g: abrupt, percussive interruption
Fricatives and their effect	f, v, th: breathy, continuous, sustained sound
Effect of sibilance	Hush, whisper, menace, or the noise of air, sand or water in motion
Fastest route to the writer's attitude	Quote three words from one semantic field and name the domain
