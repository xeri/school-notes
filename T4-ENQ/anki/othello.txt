Paper 3 format	One hour, 25 marks, one essay on Othello: either a general question or a passage-based question
May you take the play into the Paper 3 exam?	No; the studied text may not be taken in, so quotations must be memorised
Restriction stated on the 2025 paper for the general question	You may not use any evidence from the printed passage
Usual wording of the Othello general question	Examine the ways in which Shakespeare presents a named abstract noun in the play
Usual wording of the Othello passage question	Comment on how, and with what effects, Shakespeare presents or creates X at this point in the play
First recorded performance of Othello	At court on 1 November 1604, in the reign of James I
What does the move from Venice to Cyprus do structurally?	It ends the external war and begins the internal one
The pivot scene of the play	Act III scene iii, the temptation scene
Othello's language across the play	Blank verse, then shared lines with Iago, then fragmentary prose, then recovered verse in the final speech
Which register does Iago use with Roderigo, and which alone on stage?	Prose with Roderigo, verse in soliloquy
How many motives does Iago give for his hatred?	At least four, and he commits to none
Coleridge's phrase for Iago's shifting justifications	The motive-hunting of motiveless Malignity
Iago's central technique	Insinuation rather than assertion: he refuses to speak and lets Othello supply the content
The echo technique in III.iii	Iago repeats Othello's words back as questions, so the implication seems to originate in Othello
Whose warning does Iago recycle against Desdemona?	Brabantio's, that a daughter who deceived her father may deceive her husband
What does Iago offer when Othello demands proof?	A fabricated story of Cassio talking in his sleep
Why is the demand for ocular proof the fatal error?	Othello asks for visible evidence and accepts a narrative instead
What makes the audience complicit?	Iago announces his plan in soliloquy, so the audience watches a known catastrophe arrive on schedule
How often does honest and its cognates appear?	Around fifty times, almost always ironically
Who introduces animal imagery, and who has adopted it by Act IV?	Iago introduces it in I.i; Othello is using it by IV.i
The argument to make about poison imagery	Iago's imagery is medical because his method is infection, and the ear is the point of entry
What does the handkerchief stand for?	White cloth spotted with red: the wedding sheets, and so Desdemona's sexual honour made portable
Why do Othello's two accounts of the handkerchief matter?	They contradict each other, proving its meaning is invested rather than inherent
Significance of the willow in IV.iii	The tree of forsaken lovers, in a song inherited from a woman who died singing it
How does the play treat conventional colour symbolism?	It sets it up and then detonates it: the white Venetian is the devil and the woman named White is a courtesan
Emilia's arc	From complicity over the handkerchief to open defiance, and death for telling the truth
The three women and their social positions	Desdemona idealised, Emilia pragmatic, Bianca commodified, all destroyed or degraded by one system
Definition of a shared line, and its use in III.iii	Two speakers completing one verse line; Othello begins finishing Iago's, dramatising the takeover of his thought
Structure for the general question	Chronological by act: establishment, escalation, collapse, consequence
Structure for the passage question	Structure and pacing, one speaker's language, the other's in contrast, then dramatic irony and links out
Appearance and reality, in five words	I am not what I am, says Iago in I.i
Iago naming his own technique of seeming	Divinity of hell, when devils will the blackest sins put on, they do suggest at first with heavenly shows, says Iago in II.iii
Poison imagery named by the poisoner	I'll pour this pestilence into his ear, says Iago in II.iii
Iago's nearest thing to a genuine motive	He hath a daily beauty in his life that makes me ugly, says Iago in V.i
Iago's final refusal to speak	Demand me nothing: what you know, you know, says Iago in V.ii
Jealousy as self-consuming	The green-eyed monster which doth mock the meat it feeds on, says Iago in III.iii
Flimsy evidence treated as scripture	Trifles light as air are to the jealous confirmations strong as proofs of holy writ, says Iago in III.iii
Reputation called worthless, and to whom	Reputation is an idle and most false imposition, says Iago to Cassio in II.iii
Reputation called priceless, and to whom	Good name in man and woman is the immediate jewel of their souls, says Iago to Othello in III.iii
Reputation as identity	I have lost the immortal part of myself, and what remains is bestial, says Cassio in II.iii
The seed of the whole tragedy	She has deceived her father, and may thee, says Brabantio in I.iii
Authority exercised without violence	Keep up your bright swords, for the dew will rust them, says Othello in I.ii
Love built on narrative	She loved me for the dangers I had pass'd, and I loved her that she did pity them, says Othello in I.iii
Love as the only thing holding a self together	When I love thee not, chaos is come again, says Othello in III.iii
Public identity mourned before the private	Othello's occupation's gone, says Othello in III.iii
Irreversibility imagined as geography	Like to the Pontic sea, whose icy current and compulsive course ne'er feels retiring ebb, says Othello in III.iii
The collapse of Othello's language into prose	Lie with her? lie on her? says Othello in IV.i
Animal imagery transferred from Iago to Othello	Goats and monkeys, says Othello in IV.i
Desdemona as a text others inscribe	Was this fair paper, this most goodly book, made to write whore upon? asks Othello in IV.ii
The murder staged as ritual justice	It is the cause, it is the cause, my soul, says Othello in V.ii
Light imagery at its terminus	Put out the light, and then put out the light, says Othello in V.ii
Othello's last request about his reputation	Speak of me as I am, says Othello in V.ii

A woman asking whether she has become a word	Am I that name, Iago? asks Desdemona in IV.ii
Desdemona's last line	Nobody; I myself, says Desdemona in V.ii

Dramatic irony at maximum pressure	I will be hanged if some eternal villain have not devised this slander, says Emilia in IV.ii
