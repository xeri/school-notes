#deck:English::2 Paper 3 Othello
Paper 3 format	One hour, 25 marks: a general question on the whole play or a passage-based question. Closed book
Rule for the Paper 3 general question	Do not use any evidence from the printed passage
First recorded performance of Othello	At court in Whitehall, 1 November 1604, by the King's Men, under James I
Coleridge's phrase for Iago, set as the 2024 question	<i>the motive-hunting of motiveless malignity</i>
Iago's stated motives	Passed over for promotion; rumours about Emilia with Othello and Cassio; desire for Desdemona; envy of Cassio's daily beauty
Venice against Cyprus	Venice is law and order; Cyprus is passion and confusion (Garber). The storm ends the outer war and begins the inner one
Turning point of Act I	Brabantio's warning that a daughter who deceived her father may deceive her husband
Turning point of Act II	Cassio's drunken brawl and dismissal, which sends him to Desdemona to plead
The pivot of the play	III.iii, the temptation scene: Othello transfers his trust from his wife to his ensign
Turning point of Act IV	Bianca appears with the handkerchief, which Othello takes as proof
Turning point of Act V	Emilia refuses to obey Iago and exposes him
Iago's central method	Insinuation: he withholds and hesitates, and Othello supplies the content
Iago's echo technique (III.iii)	He repeats Othello's words back as questions, so the suspicion seems to start in Othello's head
Iago's borrowed authority (III.iii)	<i>She did deceive her father, marrying you</i>: Brabantio's warning reused
Iago's fabricated evidence (III.iii)	Asked for ocular proof, he invents Cassio talking in his sleep about Desdemona
Iago's staged scene (IV.i)	He hides Othello to overhear Cassio laughing about Bianca, and Othello takes it as about Desdemona
Othello's language across the play	Assured blank verse, then lines shared with Iago, then fragmented prose in IV.i, then recovered verse at the end
Iago on himself (I.i)	<i>I am not what I am</i>
Iago on the birth of his plot (I.iii)	<i>Hell and night / Must bring this monstrous birth to the world's light</i>
Iago on his own method (II.iii)	<i>When devils will the blackest sins put on, / They do suggest at first with heavenly shows</i>
Iago on poison and the ear (II.iii)	<i>I'll pour this pestilence into his ear</i>
Iago's final refusal (V.ii)	<i>Demand me nothing. What you know, you know</i>
Iago's animal slander to Brabantio (I.i)	<i>an old black ram / Is tupping your white ewe</i>
Brabantio's warning to Othello (I.iii)	<i>Look to her, Moor, if thou hast eyes to see: / She has deceived her father, and may thee</i>
Cassio on reputation (II.iii)	<i>Reputation, reputation, reputation! O, I have lost my reputation!</i>
Iago to Cassio on reputation (II.iii)	<i>Reputation is an idle and most false imposition</i>
Iago to Othello on reputation (III.iii)	<i>Good name in man and woman, dear my lord, / Is the immediate jewel of their souls</i>
Iago on jealousy (III.iii)	<i>the green-eyed monster which doth mock / The meat it feeds on</i>
Iago on flimsy evidence (III.iii)	<i>Trifles light as air / Are to the jealous confirmations strong / As proofs of holy writ</i>
Othello demands proof (III.iii)	<i>Give me the ocular proof</i>
Iago's vow as he kneels with Othello (III.iii)	<i>I am your own for ever</i>
Othello on love and chaos (III.iii)	<i>when I love thee not, / Chaos is come again</i>
Othello on race (III.iii)	<i>Haply, for I am black</i>
Othello on his lost career (III.iii)	<i>Othello's occupation's gone!</i>
Othello's language collapsing (IV.i)	<i>Lie with her? Lie on her?</i> and <i>Goats and monkeys!</i>
Othello on Desdemona as a book (IV.ii)	<i>Was this fair paper, this most goodly book, / Made to write "whore" upon?</i>
Othello before the murder (V.ii)	<i>Put out the light, and then put out the light</i>
Othello's mythical allusion (V.ii)	<i>I know not where is that Promethean heat / That can thy light relume</i>
Othello's epitaph for himself (V.ii)	<i>one that loved not wisely but too well</i>
Othello on how he won Desdemona (I.iii)	<i>She loved me for the dangers I had passed, / And I loved her that she did pity them</i>
Desdemona on the slander (IV.ii)	<i>Am I that name, Iago?</i>
Desdemona's last words (V.ii)	<i>Nobody. I myself.</i>
Emilia on men (III.iv)	<i>They are all but stomachs, and we all but food</i>
Emilia on wives (IV.iii)	<i>Let husbands know / Their wives have sense like them</i>
Emilia defying Iago (V.ii)	<i>'Tis proper I obey him, but not now</i>
