Marks: **[k]** knowledge, **[app]** application, **[an]** analysis, **[ev]** evaluation.

## Missing model answers

### Q6. Identify one way CCC was expanding in size [1]

**Number of employees** — Mr Crooks took on a part-time worker. Also accepted: **sales revenue** — business was booming. Not profit: profit is not a measure of size.

### Q7. Identify and explain one reason why it would be beneficial for CCC to have a business plan [3]

A business plan would help Mr Crooks **get a bank loan** [k]. If CCC needed extra finance to upgrade its coffee machines [app], the bank would want to see the plan to judge whether CCC could repay the loan before agreeing to lend [an].

Other accepted reasons: it helps him plan and organise, and it sets targets to measure CCC against.

### Q8. Identify two stages of the recruitment process for CCC [2]

Any two of: **job description**, **person specification**, **advertising** the job, **long list / shortlist** of applicants, **interview**. One mark each; no explanation needed, and only the first two count.

### Q15. The break-even answer, in words

The answer slide shows the completed table (losses in red, break-even in blue, profits in green). Fixed costs are \$300 in every row; variable cost is \$1.50 × output; total cost = \$300 + variable cost; revenue = \$4 × output.

| Coffees a month | 40 | 80 | 120 | 160 | 200 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Variable costs | 60 | 120 | 180 | 240 | 300 |
| Total costs | 360 | 420 | 480 | 540 | 600 |
| Total revenue | 160 | 320 | 480 | 640 | 800 |
| Profit / loss | (200) | (100) | **0 — break-even** | 100 | 200 |

**Drawing it as a chart.** Horizontal axis "Output (coffees per month)", 0 to 200; vertical axis "Costs and revenue (\$)", 0 to 800. Fixed costs: a horizontal line at \$300. Total costs: a straight line from \$300 at 0 coffees to \$600 at 200. Total revenue: a straight line from \$0 at 0 coffees to \$800 at 200. The revenue line crosses the total cost line at **120 coffees and \$480** — the break-even point. Left of it the gap between the lines is the loss; right of it, the profit. At 200 coffees the margin of safety is 200 − 120 = **80 coffees** (40% of output). Method: [operations](operations.md).

### Q18. Identify and explain one reason why Mr Crooks should change his stall location [3]

A more convenient location could **increase sales** [k]. Moving the stall to a site with more car parks [app] would let more people reach the stall easily, so more coffee would be sold and revenue would rise [an]. It could also improve CCC's brand image and bring new customers through word of mouth.

Other accepted reasons: a better brand image, showing customers he responds to feedback, moving towards the optimal location.

### Q20 (the second Q20). In order to expand, Mr Crooks would need another source of finance. Identify and explain one source he could obtain [3]

The slides number two questions "20"; this one has no model answer, so these follow the pattern of the other 3-mark answers.

- **Bank loan** [k]. Mr Crooks has written a business plan and his cash-flow forecast shows a positive net cash flow every month, with the closing balance rising from \$500 to \$1320 [app], so a bank is more likely to lend, and he can repay in fixed instalments out of coffee sales [an].
- **Retained profit** [k]. CCC made \$732 profit after tax in one quarter [app]; keeping it in the business could pay for another stall or coffee machine with no interest to pay and no loss of control [an].
- **Hire purchase or leasing** [k] for a new coffee machine [app], which spreads the cost over time so CCC's cash balance is not drained at once [an].

Not acceptable: a **share issue** — a sole trader cannot sell shares.

### Q29. Explain what is meant by the "recession" stage [3]

Any three of: **GDP falls** (usually for two quarters in a row); most businesses face **falling demand and sales**; most businesses' **profits fall**; workers **lose their jobs**. One mark each.

## Predicted long-answer questions

The 2018 sample's café case asked for: marketing roles, a cash-flow calculation and "can it afford to expand", which of three chefs to recruit, which finance source, expansion option A or B, and used a news item on interest rates, inflation and a multinational. These ten questions follow that pattern for CCC. Each frame lists the options with an applied advantage and drawback, then a defensible recommendation. Any recommendation scores if it is justified with case facts and the other options are rejected.

### P1. Second stall, café premises or coffee van [12]

*Consider these ways for CCC to expand: a second stall, renting small café premises, or a coffee van. Recommend which Mr Crooks should choose. Justify your answer.*

**Facts:** one gifted stall, Sundays only; side business; one part-time worker; OPM 56.8%; profit after tax \$732 a quarter; closing balance \$1320; car-park complaints; teacher's model answer to Q24: expand gradually.

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Second stall | cheap; could trade on another day or at another market; doubles capacity at low risk | needs a second trained barista — quality is the USP; still weather-dependent and fixed to a site |
| Café premises | open every day, all weather, seating, much higher revenue potential | rent is a large new fixed cost, so break-even rises far above 120 coffees a month; fit-out needs a loan; a side business cannot staff a café all week |
| Coffee van | mobile — can go where customers are (sports grounds, markets, events) and solves the car-park problem | cost of the van and fit-out; fuel and running costs; council permits needed to trade |

**Recommendation:** a second stall (or a second-hand van if the car-park problem is the main issue). It matches CCC's size, keeps fixed costs low, and can be paid for from retained profit; café premises are too big a jump for a one-stall sole trader making \$732 a quarter, with a risk of diseconomies of scale and cash-flow problems. Depends on finding a reliable barista and on Mr Crooks's time.

### P2. Which source of finance [12]

*Consider a bank loan, retained profit and taking on a partner. Recommend which CCC should use to expand. Justify your answer.*

**Facts:** sole trader (no shares); business plan written; positive net cash flow every month; \$732 profit after tax a quarter (about \$2900 a year if every quarter is similar — state the assumption); interest-rate news if given.

- **Bank loan:** full sum at once; Mr Crooks keeps control and all the profit; the business plan and cash-flow forecast support the application. But interest and repayments, and unlimited liability — his own assets are at risk. A \$20 000 van at 8% costs \$1600 a year in interest, over half of a year's profit.
- **Retained profit:** no interest, nothing to repay, no loss of control. But it is small — enough for a stall or a machine, not premises — and he may rely on it as income.
- **Partner** (for example, his bean-supplier friend): new capital that is not repaid, and skills and contacts. But profits and decisions are shared, disagreements are possible, and liability stays unlimited.

**Recommendation:** match the source to the plan. For a second stall or machine, retained profit — cheap and risk-free. For a van or premises, a bank loan supported by the business plan, or hire purchase for the van. Reject a partner unless the expansion is too large to borrow for, because sharing profit costs more than interest over time.

### P3. Motivating and recruiting staff [8 + 12]

**(a) Explain two ways Mr Crooks could motivate his part-time worker. [8]**

**Facts:** one part-time worker, once a month; works alone while Mr Crooks plays golf; exceptional quality is the USP. Each point needs two case facts.

- **Training** as a barista [k] — the worker runs the stall alone once a month [app], so better skills build confidence and keep coffee at CCC's exceptional standard [app]; it meets esteem needs (Maslow) [an].
- **Job enrichment / responsibility** [k] — trust them to run the Sunday stall and suggest a new drink [app]; responsibility is a Herzberg motivator, so they take pride in the quality [an].
- **Financial:** a bonus for a high-sales Sunday, or free coffee as a fringe benefit [app]; cheap for a small firm, but money alone may not raise quality [an].

**(b) Mr Crooks needs a second barista for a new stall. Recommend how he should recruit. Justify your answer. [12]**

Stages to show: identify the vacancy → job description (make coffee to CCC's standard, serve customers, handle cash, Sunday mornings) → person specification (barista experience, reliability, friendly) → advertise → shortlist → interview **plus a practical test making a flat white** → appoint → induction and training.

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Internal: offer the existing part-timer more shifts | already trained to CCC's standard; cheap and quick; motivating | still leaves a gap if the second stall needs two people at once; one worker cannot cover both stalls |
| External: advertise on social media or a notice at the stall | wider choice of skilled baristas; nearly free; reaches people who already like CCC | takes time to shortlist and interview; a stranger's quality is unknown |
| Recommendation through the bean-supplier friend's café network | experienced, tested baristas; trusted reference | small pool; may poach staff from the friend's customers and strain the relationship |

**Recommendation:** promote the existing worker to run the second stall, and recruit externally through social media for the Sunday shift, using a practical coffee test because quality is CCC's USP. Depends on how good the current worker is.

### P4. Becoming a private limited company [12]

*Consider staying a sole trader, forming a partnership, or becoming a private limited company. Recommend which is best for CCC.*

**Facts:** sole trader; side business; small (\$1610 revenue a quarter); wants to expand; may need to borrow.

- **Stay a sole trader:** keeps all the profit and full control; easy and cheap. But unlimited liability: if he borrows for premises and fails, his house could be taken.
- **Partnership:** more capital and skills (perhaps the supplier friend). But profit shared, disagreements, and liability still unlimited.
- **Private limited company (Ltd):** limited liability and a separate legal identity, continuity, and can sell shares privately to friends and family. But registration costs and paperwork, accounts must be filed, and some control and profit go to shareholders.

**Recommendation:** stay a sole trader while CCC remains a small side business with little borrowing; become a private limited company once he borrows heavily (premises), because then unlimited liability puts his own assets at serious risk.

### P5. Raising prices or premium pricing [12]

*Mr Crooks is thinking of raising his prices. Consider keeping his current prices, raising them slightly, or raising them to the level of the top Auckland cafés. Recommend which he should do.*

**Facts:** \$3.50 / \$4 / \$4.50, below most Auckland cafés; exceptional quality; strong brand image; the teacher's SWOT lists "increase prices to match the quality" as an opportunity; a regular coffee's contribution is \$2.50 and break-even 120 a month.

- **Use PED:** loyal customers, a strong brand and few nearby substitutes make demand more **inelastic**, so a rise increases revenue; many cafés nearby make it **elastic**.
- **Use the numbers:** a regular at \$4.50 raises contribution to \$3.00 and cuts break-even to 300 ÷ 3.00 = **100 coffees**; to match the revenue of 200 regulars at \$4 (\$800) he needs only 800 ÷ 4.50 = 177.8, so **178** coffees ([formulas](../CALCULATIONS.md)).
- **Keep prices:** penetration pricing still attracts new customers and is a selling point in a recession; but leaves profit unclaimed.
- **Premium prices:** match the quality image and raise margins; but risk losing price-sensitive customers who came for the low price.

**Recommendation:** a moderate rise (for example the regular to \$4.50), still below most cafés, then watch sales; test customers' reaction first with the online survey. Not a full premium jump while a recession is feared.

### P6. Economic news: interest rates and a recession [8 + 12]

**(a) Explain two ways a rise in interest rates could affect CCC. [8]**

- **Dearer borrowing** [k]: any loan for a van or premises costs more [app] (\$20 000 at 8% instead of 6% is \$400 a year more), cutting CCC's small profit [an].
- **Customers spend less** [k]: Auckland homeowners' mortgage payments rise [app], so they cut small luxuries like a \$4 Sunday coffee [app], and CCC's sales fall [an].
- A possible upside: a higher interest rate tends to raise the NZ dollar, making imported goods cheaper — imported beans may cost less [an] (assumption: beans are imported).

**(b) A recession is forecast. Consider cutting prices, cutting costs, or carrying on and delaying expansion. Recommend how CCC should respond. [12]**

**Facts:** Mr Crooks fears a recession after the pandemic; prices already below most cafés; one part-time worker; positive cash flow and \$1320 in the bank; high margins.

- **Cut prices or run promotions** (a loyalty card): keeps customers, but CCC is already cheaper than most cafés, so cuts mostly lose margin.
- **Cut costs** (fewer worker shifts, less stock): protects cash, but quality — the USP — must not fall.
- **Carry on, delay expansion:** keep the cash reserve, hold prices. CCC's low prices may even **win** customers who trade down from expensive cafés.

**Recommendation:** delay the expansion, keep prices and quality, and promote value (loyalty card, social media). Its \$1320 cash reserve and positive net cash flow let it ride out a short recession; depends on how deep and long it is.

### P7. Rising bean costs: inflation or a falling NZ dollar [12]

*The price of CCC's coffee beans is rising. Consider raising prices, absorbing the cost, or changing supplier. Recommend what Mr Crooks should do.*

**Facts:** beans cost \$55–\$100 a month, roughly a fifth of monthly receipts (Jan: 80 ÷ 420 × 100 = 19%); the friend's **50% discount**; GPM 70.2%; prices below most cafés. Inflation raises all costs; a falling NZ dollar raises the cost of imported goods (state the assumption that the beans are imported).

- **Raise prices:** protects the margin; but if demand is elastic customers may switch to cafés — though cafés face the same rise.
- **Absorb the rise:** with a GPM of 70.2% there is room; a 20% rise on \$80 of beans is only \$16 a month. But a lasting rise slowly erodes profit.
- **Change supplier:** might find cheaper beans, but would lose the 50% discount and the quality that customers praise.

**Recommendation:** absorb the rise for now and keep the friend's discount, which still gives CCC a cost advantage over every café paying full price; raise prices slightly if the rise lasts. Depends on how big and how long-lasting the rise is.

### P8. A multinational chain opens nearby [12]

*A multinational coffee chain plans to open in Mr Crooks's area. Consider cutting prices, differentiating on quality and local service, or moving the stall. Recommend how CCC should respond.*

**Facts:** the multinational has economies of scale (bulk buying, national advertising, cheaper finance) and a famous brand; CCC: one stall, no marketing, exceptional quality, low prices, loyal customers, strong brand image.

- **Cut prices:** CCC cannot win a price war against a firm with lower average costs, and it is already cheaper than most cafés.
- **Differentiate (non-price competition):** exceptional quality, a local owner, personal service; start marketing at last — a social media page, a loyalty card. Low cost, builds on strengths; the 2018 mark scheme's market researchers thought customers might prefer their local café.
- **Move the stall:** avoids head-on competition; but loses existing customers and costs effort.

**Recommendation:** differentiate. CCC's USP is quality and personality, which a chain's standardised product cannot copy; low-cost promotion now fixes its "no marketing" weakness. Depends on how close the chain is and how loyal CCC's Sunday customers are.

### P9. Sustainability versus cost [12]

*Consider these sustainable changes: compostable cups, a discount for customers who bring a reusable cup, or new energy-efficient equipment. Recommend which CCC should introduce.*

**Facts:** Mr Crooks likes buying from sustainable businesses; the teacher's answers: sustainability attracts customers, but the obvious drawback is higher costs (cups, bins, machines); CSR = people, planet, profit; recession fears.

- **Compostable cups:** visible to customers, improves brand image and may attract more of them; but each cup costs more, lowering the margin on every coffee.
- **Reusable-cup discount:** cuts cup costs and waste and builds loyalty; but loses a little revenue per coffee and only some customers will take part.
- **New equipment:** lowers power use over time; but a large capital cost when a recession is feared.

**Recommendation:** start with the reusable-cup discount (it saves cup costs, so it can pay for itself) and promote it; add compostable cups once margins allow; delay new equipment. Depends on whether customers will pay a little more for sustainability.

### P10. Keeping quality as CCC grows [12]

*Consider quality control, quality assurance and total quality management. Recommend which CCC should use as it expands.*

**Facts:** exceptional quality is the USP; job production; a part-time worker whose quality "cannot be guaranteed"; expansion means Mr Crooks cannot make or check every coffee.

- **Quality control:** Mr Crooks checks the finished coffee; needs little training; but he cannot check a second stall, and a bad coffee is already made and wasted.
- **Quality assurance:** set standards at each stage — grind, dose, milk temperature — with a written recipe and training; faults are caught as the coffee is made; but costs training time and relies on staff following the rules.
- **TQM:** every worker owns the quality, aiming for zero defects; motivating; but needs commitment that is hard to get from occasional part-time staff.

**Recommendation:** quality assurance — a written standard and training for every barista protects the USP at more than one stall without Mr Crooks being there; build a TQM culture once he has a stable team.
