# Practice case: applying answers to a business

> **Revision practice only — not examined.** This case is a class revision exercise for practising application. The exam has its own unseen business and data, so learn the *method* here, not the facts of this business.

The case follows a small coffee business, the Crooks Coffee Company (CCC), through three years. Each section below gives the facts you would use, the question types the case asks, and how a model answer earns its marks. Mark labels: **[k]** knowledge, **[app]** application, **[an]** analysis or development, **[ev]** evaluation.

## The business in brief

| Stage | What happens | Facts worth quoting |
|---|---|---|
| Start-up | Mr Crooks starts a side business selling coffee he makes, from a stall he was given, on Sunday mornings | one owner; one stall; Sunday mornings only; a friend who supplies cafés offers a **50% discount on coffee beans** |
| Year 1 | Business booms; customers praise the quality (flat white, latte, Americano, long black). He hires **one part-time worker** once a month so he can play golf and write a business plan | high quality; small range; one part-time employee |
| Year 2 | Steady success. Prices are below most Auckland cafés: **\$3.50 small, \$4 regular, \$4.50 large**. He does **no marketing** and wants to expand | low prices; no promotion; break-even forecast for a regular coffee |
| Year 3 | Strong brand image. He has a five-month cash-flow forecast and a quarterly income statement, and wants to expand further | positive cash flow; high margins |
| Later | He wants to operate sustainably but fears a recession after the pandemic | sustainability costs; recession risk |

## Before you write: pull the hooks

Underline every fact you could quote **before** answering. For this case: sole trader, one stall, Sunday mornings, 50% bean discount, high-quality coffee, the named drinks, one part-time worker, prices below cafés, no marketing, the cost and cash figures. Each answer should use at least one hook per point — two per point in an 8-mark answer.

## Question types and model structures

### Identify (1 mark per point)

*Three characteristics of an entrepreneur [3]; three benefits of high quality [3]; three impacts of a recession [3].* One mark per correct point, no explanation needed. Only the first ones you write are marked, so do not list extras.

Watch the rejected answers in the mark scheme: "increase profit" was **not** accepted as a benefit of high quality — it is too general; name the route instead (brand image, customer loyalty, more sales, market share).

### "What is meant by" (2 marks)

*What is meant by CSR? [2]* — the full term [1] + its meaning: a firm operating with the triple bottom line of people, planet and profit in mind [1].

*What is meant by "output per month"? [2]* — the number of coffees [1] that the business makes and sells in a month [1]. The marks come from tying the general term to *this* product.

### Identify and explain one (3 marks)

The most common pattern: **point [k] → case fact [app] → consequence [an]**.

*Business structure [3].* "A sole trader [k], because Mr Crooks is opening the coffee business [app] on his own with no partners or shareholders [an]."

*Objective [3].* "Survival [k], as the business has only just started selling from one stall on Sunday mornings [app], so he will first be focused on staying afloat [an]." Profit **maximisation** was not accepted for a brand-new micro-business; growth, market share, quality or profit satisficing were.

*One drawback of a part-time worker [3].* "Consistent quality and productivity cannot be guaranteed [k]. The business sells high-quality coffee [app], so a worker on one shift a month may not make it to the owner's standard or as quickly [an] — unless he hires an experienced barista or trains them." The last clause is a small evaluation that strengthens the answer.

*One form of advertising [3].* "Posters [k] in the area around the stall, showing where and when it sells, its prices and its quality [app], which raise awareness cheaply [an]." TV advertising was **not** accepted — it is unrealistic for a one-stall business. Application includes judging what is *realistic* for the size of the business.

*Production method [3].* "Job production [k], because each coffee is made one at a time to order [an], such as a small flat white or a large long black [app]."

*Why move the stall [3]; one advantage and the main drawback of sustainability [3 each].* Same pattern: "Higher costs [k], as he may need compostable cups, recycling bins or different machines [app], all adding to expenses [an]."

### One advantage and one disadvantage (4 marks)

Two separate point + development pairs. "Sole traders keep all the profit [1], so Mr Crooks keeps all the profit from every coffee sold [1]. But they have unlimited liability [1], so he is personally liable for the business's debts and his own assets could be taken [1]."

### Four items, each applied (8 marks)

*Factors of production with an example of each [8]* and *the marketing mix [8]*: 4 × (name the item [1] + a case example [1]).

| Item [1] | Applied example [1] |
|---|---|
| Land | the coffee beans; the stall's site |
| Labour | Mr Crooks making and selling the coffee |
| Capital | the coffee machine; the stall |
| Enterprise | Mr Crooks bringing the others together and taking the risk |
| Product | the high-quality flat whites, lattes, Americanos and long blacks |
| Price | \$3.50 / \$4 / \$4.50 — below most cafés, so arguably **penetration** pricing for a new entrant |
| Promotion | none at present — no advertising, sales promotion or sponsorship |
| Place | a direct channel: customers buy at his stall, with no intermediary |

Note that "no promotion" still earns the mark: describing the current position accurately *is* application.

### Explain a term with reference to the business (4 marks)

*Fixed and variable costs [4].* Definition [1] + a case example [1], twice: fixed costs do not change with the number of coffees made and sold (insurance, the stall's site fee); variable costs do (beans, milk, cups).

### SWOT with reference to the business (6 marks)

Definition [2]: SWOT analyses a business's internal strengths and weaknesses and its external opportunities and threats. Then one applied example of each:

- **S** — high-quality coffee; 50% discount on beans; survived a full year
- **W** — no marketing; a small range; one stall, one day a week
- **O** — raise prices to match the quality; open on more days or more sites
- **T** — strong, well-known competitors; a recession cutting spending

The common error is to mix them up: strengths and weaknesses are **inside** the business, opportunities and threats are **outside** it.

### Choose between two options (6 marks)

*Should he tell his worker about a cancelled shift by email or by phone? [6]* Structure: advantages and drawbacks of option A, of option B, then a decision with a reason that compares them.

- Email: quick and easy to write — but no guarantee it is read in time.
- Phone: almost guarantees the message is received, and is more personal — but the worker may not answer.
- **Decision:** phone, because the risk of the worker not seeing an email and turning up for nothing outweighs the small extra effort, and a personal call makes a part-time worker feel valued.

### Recommend one of three (12 marks)

*Which market research should he use: an online survey, government review statistics on the top cafés, or focus groups? [12]*

1. **Discuss every option**, with a benefit and a drawback each, applied:
    - online survey: cheap and easy for a small firm and its customers; but needs a large sample, and no follow-up questions
    - government statistics: free and quick; but say almost nothing about **his** customers' opinions, and cover established cafés
    - focus groups: rich, detailed opinions and follow-up questions; but expensive and time-consuming for a one-stall business
2. **Recommend** one: the online survey.
3. **Justify against the others**: the statistics will not answer his question; focus groups cost too much for a new, small firm; a survey is cheap and can reach enough customers.
4. **Add a condition or time frame**: use focus groups later, once the business has grown.

Steps 1–2 alone reach the middle level; steps 3–4 lift it to the top.

### Interpret a cash-flow forecast (6 marks)

Use the numbers: the closing balance rose from 500 to 1320 over five months (more than doubled); net cash flow was positive every month; so there is working capital to absorb a rise in bean prices or wages. Then a caution: total payments in May were up on April and net cash flow dipped from 170 to 150 in April — watch the trend. Conclude: he should be pleased.

### Use ratios to recommend (12 marks)

*Using the income statement and profitability ratios, should he expand in the short term? [12]*

| Level in the school scheme | What the answer contains |
|---|---|
| 1 | quotes the figures: revenue 1610, gross profit 1130, operating profit 915, profit after tax 732 |
| 2 | calculates **both** ratios — GPM 70.2%, OPM 56.8% — and explains what each shows (low cost of goods sold; expenses under control) |
| 3 | both ratios **plus** a strong applied conclusion |

The model conclusion: the margins show he *can* expand, but the business is tiny (one stall, one worker), so expanding too fast risks diseconomies of scale and cash-flow problems; expand gradually — another barista, another trading day, one more stall or machine — and grow further later if that works.

### Draw a diagram (6 marks)

*Draw the business cycle [6]:* axes labelled [1], curve drawn correctly [1], four stages labelled [1 each] — growth, boom, recession, slump. See the diagram in [External influences](../notes/external.md).

## The calculations, re-derived

**Break-even table** for a regular coffee: fixed costs \$300 a month, variable cost \$1.50 a coffee, price \$4.

| Coffees per month | Fixed | Variable | Total cost | Revenue | Profit / loss |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 | 300 | 60 | 360 | 160 | (200) |
| 80 | 300 | 120 | 420 | 320 | (100) |
| 120 | 300 | 180 | 480 | 480 | **0 — break-even** |
| 160 | 300 | 240 | 540 | 640 | 100 |
| 200 | 300 | 300 | 600 | 800 | 200 |

Check: contribution $= 4 - 1.50 = 2.50$ dollars a coffee, so break-even $= 300/2.50 = 120$ coffees a month. At 200 coffees the margin of safety is $200 - 120 = 80$ coffees. Each extra coffee beyond break-even adds \$2.50 of profit: 40 more coffees, \$100 more profit, as the table shows.

**Cash-flow forecast** — the missing values, January to May:

| | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Total receipts | 420 | 450 | **400** (= sales) | 340 | 430 |
| Coffee beans | 80 | 90 | 70 | **55** (= 190 − 100 − 35) | 100 |
| Wages | 120 | 120 | 120 | 100 | 125 |
| Milk | 50 | 60 | 40 | 35 | 45 |
| Total payments | 250 | 270 | **230** (= 70 + 120 + 40) | 190 | **270** (= 100 + 125 + 45) |
| Net cash flow | 170 | 180 | 170 | **150** (= 340 − 190) | 160 |
| Opening balance | 500 | 670 | **850** (= Feb closing) | 1020 | 1160 |
| Closing balance | 670 | 850 | 1020 | 1160 | **1320** (= 1160 + 160) |

The question sheet printed May's closing balance as 1290, which does not follow from its own figures; working down the column gives 1320, which is also the figure in the model answer. If a printed total disagrees with your working, trust a method you can show.

| | \$ |
|---|---:|

## Checklist: applying any answer to a case

Use this on every unseen business.

1. **Read the question first**, then the case, so you know which facts to hunt for.
2. **Underline the hooks**: what it sells, to whom, its legal structure and size, its sector, its market, named people, every number, its stated aims and problems.
3. **Count the marks** and plan the points: 2 marks = one developed point; 4 = two; 6 = two developed and applied points or two sides plus a decision; 8 = two points with two case facts each; 12 = every option, then a justified choice.
4. **One point per paragraph**: point → case fact → *so / which means* → consequence for this business.
5. **Use the business's name and its people's names**, never "the business" or "the workers" alone.
6. **Quote and use the numbers** — calculate a percentage change or a ratio if it strengthens the point.
7. **Check it is realistic for this business** — a one-stall firm does not run TV adverts or float on the stock market.
8. **Run the paste test**: if the sentence would fit any business unchanged, add a case fact.
9. **For evaluation, decide**: choose one option, give the deciding case fact, say why the others lose, and state what it depends on.
10. **Show every calculation**: formula, substitution, answer with units.
