# Business study guide (exam Fri 30 Oct)

What to revise first, why, and how. The exam rewards **Term 3** content (finance, quality, location, external influences) and **application to an unseen case** far more than Term 1 and 2 detail, so the order below follows the marks.

## The paper

3 hours, **120 marks**, no choice ([full layout](../SYLLABUS.md)).

| Section | Questions | Parts | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short answer | 3 × 20 | 2-, 4- and 6-mark parts (the 2018 sample used 2 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 6) | 60 |
| Long answer | 3 × 20 | an 8-mark part and a 12-mark part, on **unseen case data** (the 2018 sample used one café case with appendices for every long question) | 60 |

About **30 marks are application** — roughly 5 per short-answer question and 6 per long-answer question.

### Timing

180 minutes for 120 marks is **1.5 minutes per mark**.

| Part | Marks | Time at 1.5 min a mark |
|---|---:|---:|
| Identify / define | 2 | 3 min |
| Two points, or an advantage and a disadvantage | 4 | 6 min |
| Explain two / do you think | 6 | 9 min |
| One short-answer question | 20 | 30 min |
| Long answer: identify and explain two | 8 | 12 min |
| Long answer: consider, recommend, justify | 12 | 18 min |
| One long-answer question | 20 | 30 min |

The case has to be read inside the 3 hours. The short-answer questions are mostly recall, so aim to finish them a little under time and spend the saving on reading and underlining the case before the long answers. Never leave a 12-mark question without a recommendation: a rushed decision still lifts the answer into the top level, while a blank one cannot get there.

## The four skills

Every mark is one of four skills ([how marks are awarded](../SYLLABUS.md)):

| Skill | Mark | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | [k] | the correct term, definition, advantage or point |
| Application | [app] | the point tied to **this** business with a case fact or number |
| Analysis | [an] | the chain: *so… which means… leading to…* |
| Evaluation | [ev] | a decision, the deciding case fact, why the other options lose, what it depends on |

How each question type is built:

- **2 marks** — a precise definition, or point + development.
- **4 marks** — two points, each developed (applied if the question names the business).
- **6 marks, "explain two"** — two paragraphs, each point → case fact → consequence.
- **6 marks, "do you think"** — one side, the other side, a justified judgement.
- **8 marks** — two points; each needs [k] + [an] + **two** case facts.
- **12 marks** — discuss **every** option with an advantage and a drawback each, applied; then recommend one, say why the others lose, and say what the choice depends on. Level 3 (9–12, or 7–10 plus 2 [app] on the school scheme) needs at least two developed explanations **and** a justified decision.

Work down this list. Each item says why it sits where it does and how to study it.

### Methods by kind of content

| Content | Method |
|---|---|
| Definitions and lists | Anki; say the definition aloud before turning the card |
| Calculations | by hand on paper, formula → substitution → answer with unit; check a second way |
| Diagrams (break-even chart, business cycle) | draw from memory with labelled axes, then check against the notes |
| Decisions (8- and 12-mark) | write the plan: options, one advantage and drawback each, the deciding case fact, the decision, what it depends on |
| Application | underline the case hooks first (size, legal form, product, market, numbers, named people, problems); every paragraph uses one |

## Last read before the exam

The formula list in [CALCULATIONS.md](../CALCULATIONS.md), and the 12-mark frame:

1. Every option: an advantage and a drawback each, each with a case fact and a *so… which means…* chain.
2. "Overall, [business] should choose X" — the deciding case fact.
3. Why the other options lose **for this business**.
4. What it depends on, or the short run vs the long run.
