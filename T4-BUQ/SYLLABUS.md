# Business Studies — Pre-Q Syllabus

**Final exam (Term 4):** 3 hours, **120 marks** total.

- **3 compulsory short-answer questions**, 20 marks each — 50% of the paper
- **3 compulsory long-answer questions**, 20 marks each — 50% of the paper, requiring you to interpret and analyse **previously unseen data** in a business context

There is no choice of question. Earlier terms: a 1-hour exam at the end of Term 1 and a 2-hour exam at the end of Term 2. Class and common tests each term.

**Course aims.** To develop understanding of the environments in which businesses operate and how they operate; how businesses are structured and their role in sectors of the economy; business objectives and the effect of major groups on them; how businesses are financed and how their relations with other organisations, consumers, employees, owners and society are regulated; and how inputs are transformed into outputs.

## 1. Understanding business activity

- The purpose of business: needs, wants, scarcity, opportunity cost, adding value.
- Classification of business: primary, secondary and tertiary sectors; the public and private sectors.
- Business plans and the causes of business failure.
- Measuring business size: number of employees, revenue, capital employed, market share — and why the measures can rank firms differently.
- Economies and diseconomies of scale: purchasing, technical, financial, marketing and managerial.
- Sole traders and partnerships: formation, control, liability, finance.
- Limited companies: private and public; limited liability; separate legal identity; shares.
- Franchises: the franchisor–franchisee relationship, fees, benefits and restrictions.
- Joint ventures and co-operatives.
- Social enterprise and the public sector.
- Business aims and objectives: survival, profit, growth, market share, social objectives; how objectives change over time.
- Corporate social responsibility and business ethics.
- Stakeholder groups: owners, employees, customers, suppliers, lenders, government, the local community — their aims and how those aims conflict.
- Technology and business: its effect on operations, marketing, communication and employment.

## 2. People in business

- Why people work; the meaning and importance of motivation.
- Motivation theory: Maslow's hierarchy of needs, Herzberg's two-factor theory, Taylor's scientific management.
- Financial motivators: wages, salaries, piece rate, commission, bonus, profit sharing, fringe benefits.
- Non-financial motivators: job rotation, job enrichment, job enlargement, teamworking, training, opportunities for promotion.
- Organisational structure: hierarchy, chain of command, span of control, delegation, accountability, de-layering, tall and flat structures.
- Management functions and leadership styles: autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire — and when each suits.
- Communication: the process, formal and informal channels, one-way and two-way, barriers to effective communication and how to remove them.
- Trade unions: their role, collective bargaining, industrial action, the effects on workers and on the business.
- Human resource management: workforce planning, recruitment and selection, job description and person specification, internal vs external recruitment, induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training, reducing the workforce by dismissal or redundancy, legal controls on employment.

## 3. Marketing

- The role of marketing: identifying customer needs, satisfying them, maintaining and increasing market share, building customer loyalty.
- Market segmentation, niche and mass markets, market share, market growth.
- Market research: primary and secondary; the methods and their costs; sampling; the reliability and bias of results; presenting and interpreting data.
- The marketing mix — **Product**: features, brand, packaging, differentiation, the product life cycle and extension strategies, product development.
- The marketing mix — **Price**: cost-plus, penetration, skimming, competitive, promotional and psychological pricing; price elasticity of demand in a business context.
- The marketing mix — **Promotion**: advertising, sales promotion, branding, the aims of promotion, and judging whether a campaign is cost-effective.
- The marketing mix — **Place**: distribution channels, retailers, wholesalers, direct sales, e-commerce.
- Marketing strategy: how the four elements must support each other and the target segment.
- Entering new markets, including other countries: the opportunities, the risks, and the need to adapt the mix.

## 4. Operations management

- Production, productivity and capital/labour intensity; calculating and raising labour productivity.
- Methods of production: job, batch and flow — and the circumstances suiting each.
- Lean production and just-in-time stock control: the benefits and the risks.
- Operations management: inputs, process, outputs; managing capacity.
- Break-even analysis: fixed and variable costs, contribution, break-even output, margin of safety, break-even charts, and the limitations of the model.
- Quality: quality control vs quality assurance; total quality management; the costs of poor quality and of improving it.
- Location decisions: the factors affecting the location of a factory, a retail outlet and a service business; locating in another country.

## 5. Financial information and decisions

- The need for business finance: start-up capital, working capital, expansion.
- Sources of finance — internal: retained profit, sale of assets, owner's savings. External: bank loan, overdraft, share capital, debentures, trade credit, hire purchase, leasing, grants, microfinance, crowdfunding.
- Choosing a source of finance: short vs long term, cost, control, risk, size and legal form of the business.
- Cash flow forecasting: receipts, payments, net cash flow, opening and closing balance; why cash is not profit; the causes of and solutions to cash-flow problems.
- Working capital.
- Income statements: revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, expenses, operating profit, profit for the year; the use of profit.
- Statements of financial position: non-current and current assets, current and non-current liabilities, equity.
- Ratio analysis — profitability: gross profit margin, operating profit margin, return on capital employed.
- Ratio analysis — liquidity: current ratio, acid-test ratio.
- Who uses accounts and why; the limitations of ratio analysis.

## 6. External influences on business activity

- Government economic objectives: growth, low unemployment, low inflation, a favourable balance of payments.
- Government policies: fiscal policy (spending and taxation) and monetary policy (interest rates); how each affects a business's costs, demand and decisions.
- The business cycle: boom, recession, slump, recovery; how firms are affected differently by sector.
- Economic influences: inflation, interest rates, unemployment, economic growth, exchange rates.
- Legal controls: on employment, consumer protection, health and safety, and the environment; the costs of compliance and of non-compliance.
- Environmental and ethical influences: externalities, sustainability, pressure groups; whether ethical decisions help or harm profit.
- Business and the international economy: globalisation, multinational companies, importing and exporting, exchange-rate changes, free trade, tariffs and quotas, and protectionism.
