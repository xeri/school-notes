# Business glossary

Definitions are worth marks on their own, and precision matters. Where two terms are easily confused, both appear together.

## Business activity

**Need** — something essential for survival: food, water, shelter, clothing.
**Want** — something desired but not essential.
**Scarcity** — limited resources against unlimited wants.
**Opportunity cost** — the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
**Added value** — selling price minus the cost of bought-in materials. *Not profit.*
**Factors of production** — land, labour, capital, enterprise.

**Primary sector** — extracting natural resources.
**Secondary sector** — manufacturing and construction.
**Tertiary sector** — providing services.
**Private sector** — owned by individuals, usually profit-seeking.
**Public sector** — owned by government, usually service-providing.

**Business plan** — a document stating the idea, market, operations and financial forecasts, used to focus the owner's thinking and persuade lenders.

## Size and growth

**Economies of scale** — the fall in **average cost per unit** as the scale of operations expands.
**Diseconomies of scale** — the rise in average cost when a firm becomes too large to coordinate.
**Capital employed** — the total long-term finance invested in the business.
**Internal growth (organic)** — growth by expanding the firm's own operations.
**External growth** — growth by merger or takeover.

## Ownership

**Unlimited liability** — the owner is personally responsible for all business debts.
**Limited liability** — a shareholder can lose no more than the amount invested.
**Separate legal identity** — the company is a legal person distinct from its owners; this is what makes limited liability possible.

**Sole trader** — a business owned and controlled by one person with unlimited liability.
**Partnership** — a business owned by two or more partners who share profits and, usually, unlimited liability.
**Private limited company (Ltd)** — shares sold privately and not transferable without agreement.
**Public limited company (plc)** — shares may be offered to the public. **A plc is a private-sector business** — "public" refers to share sale, not government ownership.
**Franchise** — an agreement in which the franchisee pays the franchisor to trade using its brand, products and systems.
**Joint venture** — two businesses creating a separate shared enterprise, sharing cost, risk and profit.
**Co-operative** — a business owned and democratically run by its members, who share the profits.
**Social enterprise** — a business trading in order to pursue a social or environmental purpose, reinvesting its surplus.

## Objectives and stakeholders

**Objective** — a target the business aims to achieve; ideally **SMART** (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound).
**Stakeholder** — any individual or group with an interest in the business's activities.
**Shareholder** — a part-owner of a company. *A shareholder is one kind of stakeholder, not a synonym.*
**Corporate social responsibility** — accepting responsibility for the business's effects on society and the environment beyond legal requirements.
**Ethics** — principles of right and wrong conduct, a different boundary from legal compliance.

## People

**Motivation** — the willingness to work towards the business's objectives.
**Labour turnover** — the proportion of the workforce leaving in a period.
**Piece rate** — payment per unit produced.
**Commission** — payment as a percentage of sales value.
**Fringe benefits** — non-cash rewards such as a company car or health cover.
**Job rotation** — moving between tasks of similar difficulty.
**Job enlargement** — adding more tasks at the same level.
**Job enrichment** — adding more challenging work and genuine responsibility.

**Hierarchy** — the levels of authority in an organisation.
**Chain of command** — the route down which instructions pass.
**Span of control** — the number of subordinates directly supervised by one manager.
**Delegation** — passing authority for a task downwards; **accountability remains with the manager**.
**De-layering** — removing a level of management.

**Recruitment** — identifying and attracting candidates.
**Selection** — choosing between candidates against the job's requirements.
**Job description** — the duties and responsibilities of the post.
**Person specification** — the skills, qualifications and qualities the post-holder needs.
**Induction** — introducing a new employee to the premises, people and procedures.
**Dismissal** — ending employment because of the employee's conduct or performance.
**Redundancy** — ending employment because the job itself is no longer needed.
**Trade union** — an organisation representing employees collectively.
**Collective bargaining** — negotiation between a union and an employer on behalf of all members.

## Marketing

**Market segmentation** — dividing a market into groups with shared characteristics.
**Niche market** — a small, specific part of a larger market.
**Mass market** — a large market with broad appeal.
**Market share** — one firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales.
**Market growth** — the percentage increase in the size of the whole market.

**Primary research** — collecting new data for the present purpose.
**Secondary research** — using data that already exists.
**Qualitative data** — opinions and reasons; explains *why*.
**Quantitative data** — numerical data that can be measured and compared.
**Sample** — a group selected to represent the whole population.
**Bias** — a flaw in method that makes results systematically unrepresentative.

**Marketing mix** — product, price, promotion and place, which must support one another.
**Product differentiation** — making a product distinguishable from rivals'.
**Brand** — a name or image that identifies a product and distinguishes it.
**Product life cycle** — development, introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
**Extension strategy** — an action taken to prolong the maturity stage.
**Penetration pricing** — a low launch price to build volume and share.
**Price skimming** — a high launch price, lowered later.
**Cost-plus pricing** — adding a mark-up to unit cost.
**Price elasticity of demand** — how responsive quantity demanded is to a price change.
**Distribution channel** — the route a product takes from producer to consumer.

## Operations

**Productivity** — output per unit of input, usually per worker.
**Capital-intensive** — using a high proportion of machinery relative to labour.
**Labour-intensive** — using a high proportion of labour relative to machinery.
**Job production** — one distinct product made at a time to order.
**Batch production** — groups of identical items made before changing task.
**Flow production** — continuous mass production of a standardised product.
**Lean production** — reducing every form of waste in the process.
**Kaizen** — continuous small improvements.
**Just-in-time** — holding almost no stock; materials arrive as needed.
**Quality control** — inspecting finished output and rejecting defects.
**Quality assurance** — designing the process to prevent defects arising.
**Total quality management** — organisation-wide commitment to zero defects.

**Fixed cost** — a cost that does not change with output in the short run.
**Variable cost** — a cost that changes directly with output.
**Contribution** — selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit.
**Break-even output** — the output at which total revenue equals total cost.
**Margin of safety** — how far actual output exceeds break-even output.

## Finance

**Start-up capital** — finance needed to begin trading.
**Working capital** — current assets minus current liabilities; finance for day-to-day operations. *Not the bank balance.*
**Retained profit** — profit kept in the business rather than distributed.
**Overdraft** — a short-term facility to go into deficit on a bank account; **repayable on demand**.
**Debenture** — a long-term loan to a company, usually at a fixed rate of interest.
**Trade credit** — buying from a supplier now and paying later.
**Leasing** — paying to use an asset without ever owning it.
**Hire purchase** — paying in instalments and owning the asset at the end.
**Gearing** — the proportion of a firm's finance that is borrowed.

**Revenue** — income from sales: price × quantity.
**Cost of sales** — the direct cost of the goods actually sold.
**Gross profit** — revenue minus cost of sales.
**Operating profit** — gross profit minus operating expenses.
**Dividend** — a share of profit paid to shareholders.
**Non-current asset** — an asset kept for more than a year (premises, equipment).
**Current asset** — an asset expected to turn into cash within a year (inventory, receivables, cash).
**Current liability** — a debt due within a year (payables, overdraft).
**Inventory** — stock of raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
**Liquidity** — the ability to meet short-term debts as they fall due.
**Insolvency** — being unable to pay debts when they are due.
**Overtrading** — expanding faster than the available working capital allows.

**Cash flow is not profit.** Profit is revenue minus expenses for a period; cash flow is money actually moving in and out.

## External influences

**Inflation** — a sustained rise in the general price level.
**Recession** — a sustained fall in a country's output.
**Business cycle** — the pattern of boom, recession, slump and recovery.
**Fiscal policy** — government use of spending and taxation.
**Monetary policy** — influencing monetary conditions, usually through interest rates.
**Exchange rate** — the price of one currency in terms of another.
**Depreciation (of a currency)** — a fall in its value against other currencies. *Distinct from depreciation of an asset, which is the fall in its book value over time.*
**Externality** — a cost or benefit falling on third parties rather than the business.
**Sustainability** — meeting present needs without depriving future generations.
**Pressure group** — an organised group campaigning to change business or government behaviour.
**Globalisation** — the growing integration of national economies through trade and investment.
**Multinational company** — a business with production operations in more than one country.
**Tariff** — a tax on imports.
**Quota** — a physical limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported.
**Protectionism** — restricting imports to shield domestic producers.
**Free trade** — trade without barriers such as tariffs or quotas.
