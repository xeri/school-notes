# Business glossary

Definitions are worth marks on their own, and precision matters. Where two terms are easily confused, both appear together.

## Business activity

**Need** — something essential for survival: food, water, shelter, clothing.
**Want** — something desired but not essential.
**Scarcity** — limited resources against unlimited wants.
**Opportunity cost** — the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
**Added value** — the selling price of a finished item minus the cost of the bought-in materials and components. *Not profit.*
**Factors of production** — land, labour, capital, enterprise.
**Entrepreneur** — a person who takes the risk of bringing the factors of production together to produce a good or service.
**Specialisation** — people, machines or firms concentrating on the task they do best.

**Primary sector** — extracting natural resources.
**Secondary sector** — manufacturing and construction.
**Tertiary sector** — providing services.
**Private sector** — the part of the economy owned and run by private individuals and firms, usually profit-seeking.
**Public sector** — the part of the economy owned and controlled by government, usually service-providing.
**De-industrialisation** — the decline in the importance of the secondary (manufacturing) sector.
**Free-market economy** — resources privately owned, no government control, prices set by supply and demand.
**Command (planned) economy** — all resources owned and planned by government.
**Mixed economy** — has both a private and a public sector; nearly every country.
**Privatisation** — selling government-owned businesses to the private sector. **Nationalisation** is the reverse.

**Business plan** — a formal statement of business goals, why they are attainable and the plans for reaching them, from a marketing, financial and operational viewpoint; used to focus the owner's thinking and persuade lenders and investors.

## Size and growth

**Economies of scale** — the fall in **average cost per unit** as the scale of operations expands.
**Diseconomies of scale** — the rise in average cost when growth makes a firm less efficient.
**Capital employed** — the total long-term finance invested in the business.
**Market capitalisation** — share price × number of shares issued; a size measure for a plc.
**Internal growth (organic)** — growth by expanding the firm's own operations, usually from retained profit.
**External growth** — growth by merger or takeover.
**Merger** — two or more businesses mutually agree to join into one.
**Takeover** — one business buys another, which comes under its name and control.
**Horizontal integration** — joining a firm in the same industry at the same stage of production.
**Vertical integration** — joining a firm in the same industry at a different stage: **backward** towards raw materials, **forward** towards the consumer.
**Conglomerate integration** — joining a firm in an unrelated industry.

## Ownership

**Unlimited liability** — the owner is personally responsible for all business debts.
**Limited liability** — a shareholder can lose no more than the amount invested.
**Separate legal identity** — the company is a legal person distinct from its owners; this is what makes limited liability possible.

**Sole trader** — a business owned and controlled by one person with unlimited liability.
**Partnership** — a business owned by two or more partners who share profits and unlimited liability.
**Deed of partnership** — an agreement setting out partners' voting rights, responsibilities and profit split; not a legal requirement.
**Private limited company (Ltd)** — shares sold privately and not transferable without every shareholder's agreement.
**Public limited company (plc)** — shares bought and sold openly on the stock market. **A plc is a private-sector business** — "public" refers to share sale, not government ownership.
**Directors** — elected by shareholders at the AGM to run the company on their behalf.
**Franchise** — the franchisor sells the franchisee the right to use its name, logo, products and methods in another location, for a fee and a % of sales revenue.
**Joint venture** — two or more parties pooling their resources to accomplish a specific task, sharing its profits, losses and costs.
**Co-operative** — a business owned and democratically controlled by its members, run for their mutual benefit rather than for investors' profit.
**Social enterprise** — a private-sector business with mainly social objectives whose surpluses are reinvested rather than used to maximise owners' profit.
**Public corporation** — a business wholly owned by the government, run by a board it appoints.

## Objectives and stakeholders

**Aim** — where the business wants to go; a statement of purpose.
**Objective** — a stated, measurable target for achieving an aim; ideally **SMART** (specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time specific).
**Mission statement** — a short, motivating statement of the business's core aims.
**Profit satisficing** — aiming for enough profit, without profit being the only focus.
**Stakeholder** — anyone who has a direct interest in the performance of a business.
**Shareholder** — a part-owner of a company. *A shareholder is one kind of stakeholder, not a synonym.*
**Corporate social responsibility** — operating ethically towards people, planet and profit (the **triple bottom line**), beyond legal requirements.
**Ethics** — considering the interests of society beyond legal obligations; deciding what is right, not just what is legal.
**Kaitiakitanga** — guardianship of natural resources. **Manaakitanga** — the ethic of caring and hospitality. **Tikanga** — Māori values followed in the business. **Tino rangatiratanga** — self-determination and ownership. **Pūtake** — the origin or reason for being.

## People

**Motivation** — the driving force behind behaviour; at work, the desire to work hard and do a good job.
**Theory X / Theory Y** — McGregor: an X manager believes workers dislike work and need close supervision; a Y manager believes they are self-motivated and want responsibility.
**Labour turnover** — the number of employees leaving in a year as a percentage of the number employed.
**Piece rate** — payment per unit produced.
**Commission** — payment as a percentage of sales value.
**Performance-related pay** — pay linked to an appraisal of how effectively the employee works.
**Fringe benefits** — non-cash rewards such as a company car or health cover.
**Job rotation** — moving between tasks of similar difficulty.
**Job enlargement** — adding more tasks at the same level.
**Job enrichment** — adding more challenging work and genuine responsibility.

**Hierarchy** — the levels of authority in an organisation.
**Chain of command** — the route down which instructions pass.
**Span of control** — the number of subordinates directly supervised by one manager.
**Delegation** — passing authority for a task downwards; **accountability remains with the manager**.
**De-layering** — removing a level of management.
**Centralised organisation** — decision-making concentrated at the top. **Decentralised** — most decisions delegated down the chain of command.

**Recruitment** — identifying and attracting candidates.
**Selection** — choosing between candidates against the job's requirements.
**Job description** — the duties and responsibilities of the post.
**Person specification** — the skills, qualifications and qualities the post-holder needs.
**Induction** — introducing a new employee to the premises, people and procedures.
**Dismissal** — ending employment because of the employee's conduct or performance.
**Redundancy** — ending employment because the job itself is no longer needed; **voluntary** if the employee chooses to go, **compulsory** if forced, both with compensation.
**Trade union** — an organisation that aims to improve its members' conditions and provide support and legal services.
**Collective bargaining** — negotiation between a union and an employer on behalf of all members.

## Marketing

**Marketing** — the management process of anticipating, understanding and satisfying customer needs at a profit.
**Market orientation** — basing products on market research into what customers want. **Product orientation** — focusing on products and processes without market research.
**Market segmentation** — grouping consumers with similar characteristics, preferences and buying habits.
**Target market** — the group of consumers a business designs its products and marketing to appeal to.
**Niche market** — a small, specific part of a larger market.
**Mass market** — a large market with broad appeal.
**Market share** — one firm's sales as a percentage of total market sales.
**Market growth** — the percentage increase in the size of the whole market.

**Primary research** — collecting new data for the present purpose.
**Secondary research** — using data that already exists.
**Qualitative data** — opinions and reasons; explains *why*.
**Quantitative data** — numerical data that can be measured and compared.
**Sample** — a group selected to represent the whole population.
**Bias** — a flaw in method that makes results systematically unrepresentative: sampling, questionnaire or response bias.

**Marketing mix** — product, price, promotion and place, which must support one another.
**Product differentiation** — making a product distinguishable from rivals'.
**Unique selling point (USP)** — a feature or benefit that differentiates a product from its competitors.
**Brand** — a name or image that identifies a product and distinguishes it. **Branding** creates distinctive, durable perceptions in consumers' minds.
**Product life cycle** — the stages of a product's sales over time: introduction, growth, maturity, decline (a development stage is sometimes shown first).
**Extension strategy** — an action taken at maturity or decline to prolong sales.
**Penetration pricing** — a low launch price to build volume and share.
**Price skimming** — a high launch price, lowered later.
**Cost-plus pricing** — adding a mark-up to unit cost.
**Price elasticity of demand** — how responsive quantity demanded is to a price change: % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price.
**Above-the-line promotion** — promotion through mass media. **Below-the-line** — promotion without mass media, within the firm's control.
**Distribution channel** — the route a product takes from producer to consumer, including every intermediary.
**Intermediary** — a middleman between producer and consumer, such as a wholesaler, retailer or agent.
**SWOT analysis** — internal strengths and weaknesses, external opportunities and threats.

## Operations

**Production** — the amount of goods and services produced in a period.
**Productivity** — the ratio of outputs to inputs; how efficiently they are produced, usually per worker.
**Capital-intensive** — using a high proportion of machinery relative to labour.
**Labour-intensive** — using a high proportion of labour relative to machinery.
**Job production** — one distinct product made at a time to order.
**Batch production** — groups of identical items made before changing task.
**Flow production** — continuous mass production of a standardised product.
**Delivery lead time** — the time between placing an order and receiving it.
**Lean production** — producing with the minimum of wasted resources while maintaining high quality.
**Kaizen** — continuous improvement through small changes suggested by employees.
**Just-in-time** — keeping stock to a minimum; materials arrive just as they are needed.
**Quality** — meeting or exceeding customer expectations; being fit for purpose.
**Quality control** — inspectors checking output at the end of production for faults.
**Quality assurance** — checking for faults at each stage of production to prevent defects.
**Total quality management** — a philosophy that quality is every employee's responsibility, aiming at zero defects.
**Quality circle** — a small group of workers meeting regularly to solve production problems.
**Optimal location** — the site giving the best combination of quantitative and qualitative factors.
**Infrastructure** — facilities supporting everyday economic activity: roads, rail, power, water, telecommunications.
**Offshoring** — relocating a business process from one country to another.

**Fixed cost** — a cost that does not change with output in the short run.
**Average cost** — total cost ÷ output.
**Variable cost** — a cost that changes directly with output.
**Contribution** — selling price per unit minus variable cost per unit.
**Break-even output** — the output at which total revenue equals total cost.
**Margin of safety** — how far actual output exceeds break-even output.

## Finance

**Start-up capital** — finance needed to begin trading.
**Capital expenditure** — spending on non-current assets. **Revenue expenditure** — spending on running costs.
**Working capital** — current assets minus current liabilities; finance for day-to-day operations. *Not the bank balance.* (The school slides also express it as a ratio, current assets : current liabilities.)
**Retained profit** — profit kept in the business rather than distributed.
**Overdraft** — a short-term facility to go into deficit on a bank account; **repayable on demand**.
**Debenture** — a long-term loan to a company, usually at a fixed rate of interest.
**Trade credit** — buying from a supplier now and paying later.
**Leasing** — paying to use an asset without buying it; at the end it can be returned or sometimes bought at a discount.
**Hire purchase** — hiring an asset for monthly payments plus interest, owning it once the full price is paid.
**Sale and leaseback** — selling a non-current asset and renting it back, raising cash while still using it.
**Venture capital** — finance from a venture capital firm or business angel in return for a share of ownership and management.
**Gearing** — the proportion of a firm's finance that is borrowed.

**Revenue** — income from sales: price × quantity.
**Cost of sales** — the direct cost of the goods actually sold: opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory.
**Gross profit** — revenue minus cost of sales.
**Operating profit** — gross profit minus operating expenses.
**Profit for the year** — profit after interest and tax.
**Dividend** — a share of profit paid to shareholders, after tax; not compulsory.
**Non-current asset** — an asset kept for more than a year (premises, equipment).
**Current asset** — an asset expected to turn into cash within a year (inventory, receivables, cash).
**Current liability** — a debt due within a year (payables, overdraft).
**Inventory** — stock of raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
**Liquidity** — the ability to meet short-term debts as they fall due.
**Insolvency** — being unable to pay debts when they are due.
**Equity** — share capital plus retained earnings; total assets minus total liabilities.
**Overtrading** — expanding faster than the available working capital allows.

**Cash flow is not profit.** Profit is revenue minus expenses for a period; cash flow is money actually moving in and out.

## External influences

**Inflation** — a sustained rise in the general price level.
**Unemployment** — people willing and able to work who cannot find paid employment.
**Economic growth** — a rise in a country's total output (GDP).
**Balance of trade** — the difference between a country's exports and imports.
**Recession** — a sustained fall in a country's output, usually two quarters in a row of falling GDP.
**Business cycle** — the recurring pattern of growth, boom, recession, slump and recovery.
**Fiscal policy** — government use of spending and taxation.
**Monetary policy** — influencing monetary conditions, usually through interest rates.
**Supply-side policy** — improving the efficiency of supply: privatisation, training and education, more competition.
**Exchange rate** — the price of one currency in terms of another.
**Depreciation (of a currency)** — a fall in its value against other currencies. *Distinct from depreciation of an asset, which is the fall in its book value over time.*
**Externality** — a cost or benefit falling on third parties rather than the business.
**Social cost** — private cost plus external cost. **Social benefit** — private benefit plus external benefit.
**Cost-benefit analysis** — weighing all private and external costs and benefits before a decision.
**External constraint** — a limit on business activity that the firm cannot directly control.
**Sustainability** — meeting our needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
**Pressure group** — an organisation aiming to change the behaviour of a business that fails to act in a socially or environmentally responsible way.
**Globalisation** — the world becoming increasingly interconnected through increased trade and cultural exchange.
**Multinational company** — a business with its headquarters in one country and operations in another; exporting alone does not count.
**Tariff** — a tax on imports.
**Quota** — a physical limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported.
**Embargo** — a ban on trading certain goods or with a certain country.
**Protectionism** — restricting imports to shield domestic producers.
**Free trade** — trade without barriers such as tariffs or quotas.
