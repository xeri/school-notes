#deck:Business::6 External influences
Four government economic objectives	Low and stable inflation, high and stable employment, economic growth, a favourable balance of international trade and payments
Give an example of conflicting government objectives	Stimulating growth can pull in imports and push up inflation
Definition of fiscal policy	Government use of spending and taxation
Definition of monetary policy	Influencing monetary conditions, usually through interest rates
Effect of lower income tax on a business	Consumers have more disposable income, so demand rises
Effect of higher indirect tax (VAT/GST)	Higher prices, so demand may fall — especially for elastic goods
Effect of higher interest rates on borrowing	Repayments on variable-rate loans and overdrafts rise, cutting profit
Effect of higher interest rates on investment	Expansion becomes less attractive and may be postponed
Effect of higher interest rates on consumer demand	Credit-financed purchases such as cars and houses fall sharply
Effect of higher interest rates on the exchange rate	Foreign savers deposit money there, so the currency appreciates — exports dearer, imports cheaper
Three supply-side policies	Privatisation, training and education, promoting competition (less government control, action against monopolies)
Five stages of the business cycle	Growth, boom, recession, slump, recovery
Typical business response in a recession	Cut costs, reduce stock, discount, delay investment
Typical business response in a boom	Raise prices, expand capacity, recruit — while watching for overtrading
Which sectors are most sensitive to the business cycle?	Luxury goods, construction and travel
Which sectors are least sensitive?	Basic food, utilities and discount retail
Definition of inflation	A sustained increase in the average level of prices in an economy
Four effects of inflation on a business	Higher input costs, lower real customer purchasing power, wage demands, harder planning and pricing
Who benefits from inflation?	Borrowers — the real value of existing debt falls
Two effects of unemployment on a business	Lower total demand, but a larger pool of available workers and weaker wage pressure
What does SPICED stand for?	Strong Pound (any strong currency), Imports Cheap, Exports Dear
Effect of a weaker currency on exports	Cheaper to foreign buyers, so export volume tends to rise
Effect of a weaker currency on imported materials	More expensive in domestic currency, so costs rise
What determines the net effect of a currency fall?	The invoicing currency, demand elasticity, and the share of imported costs
Four areas of legal control on business	Employment, consumer protection, health and safety, the environment
Two consumer protection requirements	Goods must be fit for purpose and as described; advertising must be honest
Cost of non-compliance with health and safety	Accidents, prosecution, compensation and lost output
Definition of an externality	A cost or benefit falling on third parties rather than the business
Definition of sustainability	Meeting our needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs
Why can sustainability measures cut costs?	Less waste and less energy used — though investment is needed first
How do pressure groups influence a business?	Boycotts, protests and publicity campaigns
What determines a pressure group's influence?	Its size, media attention, and how exposed the brand is
Does an ethical decision always increase profit?	No — judge the specific case and state what it depends on
Definition of globalisation	The world becoming increasingly interconnected through increased trade and cultural exchange
Definition of a multinational company	A business with its headquarters in one country and production or operations in another — exporting alone is not enough
Three benefits of a multinational to a host country	Jobs and training, tax revenue, technology and skills transfer
Three drawbacks of a multinational to a host country	Mainly low-skilled jobs, profits taken home (little tax), local firms suffer (also resources depleted)
Definition of a tariff	A tax on imports
Definition of a quota	A physical limit on the quantity of a good that may be imported
Who is harmed by a tariff besides foreign producers?	Domestic firms that use imported materials, and consumers paying higher prices
Three benefits of free trade	Access to larger markets, economies of scale, cheaper inputs and more consumer choice
Main risk of free trade for domestic firms	Exposure to stronger foreign competitors
Four options for a firm facing higher imported input costs	Change suppliers, reduce waste, absorb a lower margin, raise prices
When is raising prices the worst option?	When customers have many substitutes — demand is elastic
What inflation rate do most governments aim for?	Low and stable — about 1% to 3% a year
Definition of unemployment	People willing and able to work who cannot find paid employment
What is a favourable balance of trade?	Exporting more than importing (or reducing a trade deficit)
Usual definition of a recession	Two quarters in a row of falling GDP
Effect of higher corporate tax on a business	Lower profit after tax, less to reinvest, expansion harder
Social cost formula	Private cost + external cost
Definition of an external benefit	A positive effect of an activity on an unrelated third party — e.g. bees pollinating nearby crops
Definition of a pressure group	An organisation aiming to change the behaviour of a business that fails to act responsibly, through publicity and protest
Four tools of protectionism	Tariffs, quotas, subsidies to domestic producers, embargoes
Definition of an embargo	A ban on trading certain goods or with a certain country
Home vs host country	Home: where a multinational has its headquarters; host: where it sets up operations
