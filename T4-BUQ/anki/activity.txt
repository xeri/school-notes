#deck:Business::1 Understanding business activity
Definition of opportunity cost	The next best alternative given up when a choice is made
Definition of added value	Selling price minus the cost of bought-in materials
Is added value the same as profit?	No — wages, rent and overheads still come out of it
Four ways to add value	Branding, better quality, convenience, after-sales service
Four factors of production	Land, labour, capital, enterprise
Primary sector	Extracting natural resources — farming, fishing, mining
Secondary sector	Manufacturing and construction
Tertiary sector	Providing services — retail, banking, transport
What is de-industrialisation?	The falling share of employment in the secondary sector as an economy develops
Difference between private and public sector	Private is owned by individuals and usually profit-seeking; public is government-owned and service-providing
Two purposes of a business plan	To force the owner to think the venture through, and to persuade a lender or investor
Five causes of business failure	Poor cash-flow management, no market research, poor management of growth, over-borrowing, external shocks
Four ways to measure business size	Number of employees, revenue, capital employed, market share
Why can size measures disagree?	A capital-intensive firm can have huge revenue with very few employees
Definition of economies of scale	The fall in average cost per unit as the scale of operations expands
Purchasing economy of scale	Bulk buying earns discounts per unit
Technical economy of scale	Specialist machinery spreads its cost over more units
Financial economy of scale	Larger firms are seen as lower risk so borrow more cheaply
Marketing economy of scale	One advertising campaign supports a larger volume
Three diseconomies of scale	Poor communication, poor coordination, low morale
A $6000 campaign over 2000 vs 6000 units	$3 per unit vs $1 per unit
Liability of a sole trader	Unlimited — personally responsible for all business debts
Two advantages of being a sole trader	Total control and keeps all the profit (also easy to set up)
Two drawbacks of being a sole trader	Hard to raise capital, unlimited liability (also no continuity)
Two advantages of a partnership	Combines finance and expertise, shares the workload
Two drawbacks of a partnership	Disagreements, and each partner is bound by the others' decisions
Definition of limited liability	A shareholder can lose no more than the amount invested
What makes limited liability possible?	The company has separate legal identity from its owners
Is a public limited company in the public sector?	No — "public" means shares are offered to the public; it is a private-sector business
Difference between Ltd and plc	Ltd sells shares privately; plc may offer shares to the public
Two drawbacks of becoming a plc	Expensive to set up and must publish accounts; risk of takeover and loss of control
What does a franchisee pay the franchisor?	An initial fee plus ongoing royalties
Two benefits to the franchisee	Established brand and lower failure risk; training and support
Two drawbacks to the franchisee	Fees reduce profit; little freedom over product or pricing
Two benefits to the franchisor	Rapid expansion using someone else's capital; royalty income
Definition of a joint venture	Two businesses creating a separate shared enterprise, sharing cost, risk and profit
Definition of a co-operative	A business owned and democratically run by its members, who share the profits
Definition of a social enterprise	A business trading to pursue a social or environmental purpose, reinvesting its surplus
What does SMART stand for?	Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound
Why do objectives change over time?	A start-up aims to survive, then to grow, then perhaps to maximise profit
Give an example of conflicting objectives	Growth may require cutting prices, reducing short-run profit
Definition of corporate social responsibility	Accepting responsibility for the business's effects on society and the environment beyond legal requirements
Three arguments for CSR	Better reputation and sales, easier recruitment, avoids fines and boycotts
Three arguments against CSR	Higher costs, shareholders may prefer dividends, non-complying competitors undercut you
Difference between ethics and legal compliance	Ethics is about what is right; legality is a separate, usually lower, boundary
Definition of a stakeholder	Any individual or group with an interest in the business's activities
Is a shareholder the same as a stakeholder?	No — a shareholder is one kind of stakeholder
Main aim of employees as stakeholders	Fair pay, job security and good working conditions
Main aim of suppliers as stakeholders	Regular orders and prompt payment
Main aim of the local community as a stakeholder	Jobs with little pollution and congestion
Give an example of stakeholder conflict	Raising wages pleases employees but reduces shareholders' profit
Effect of technology on operations	Automation cuts unit costs and raises consistency, but needs capital and may cut jobs
Effect of technology on employment	Demand shifts from unskilled to skilled work, creating retraining and redundancy costs
Three continuing costs of technology	Maintenance, training, and the risk of obsolescence or system failure
