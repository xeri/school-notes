#deck:Business::3 Marketing
Five roles of marketing	Identify customer needs, satisfy them, maintain loyalty, gain information about customers, anticipate changes in their needs
Definition of market segmentation	Grouping consumers with similar characteristics, preferences and buying habits
Six bases for segmentation	Income, age, lifestyle, gender, location, socio-economic group
Two advantages of segmentation	More effective marketing to each segment, and gaps in the market are easier to spot
Three problems with segmentation	Lack of data, consumer behaviour is hard to predict, segments can be hard to reach
Niche market: two advantages	Fewer direct rivals and the ability to charge a premium price
Niche market: main risk	Limited sales and growth, and a firm relying on one product fails if demand for it falls
Mass market: two advantages	High sales and profits, and marketing economies of scale
Market share formula	(firm's sales ÷ total market sales) × 100
Definition of market growth	The percentage increase in the size of the whole market
Definition of primary research	Collecting new data for the present purpose
Definition of secondary research	Using data that already exists
Four primary research methods	Questionnaires and surveys, interviews, focus groups (consumer panels), observation (also test marketing)
Four secondary research sources	Internal sales records, government statistics, trade journals, competitors' accounts
Primary research: advantage and drawback	Specific and up to date; slow and expensive
Secondary research: advantage and drawback	Cheap and quick; may be out of date or collected for a different purpose
Qualitative vs quantitative data	Qualitative explains why (opinions, motives); quantitative gives measurable numbers
Why is surveying only current customers biased?	It misses everyone who considered the product and rejected it
Three types of research bias	Sampling bias, questionnaire bias (badly worded questions), response bias (untruthful answers)
Core, tangible and augmented product	Core: what it does; tangible: what it is made of and looks like; augmented: extras that add perceived value, such as the brand or after-sales service
Definition of product differentiation	Making a product distinguishable from rivals'
Two benefits of branding	Repeat purchase through brand loyalty, and a higher price
Risk of a strong brand	One scandal damages the whole range
Four functions of packaging	Protects, eases transport and storage, carries legal information, promotes at point of sale
Four main stages of the product life cycle	Introduction, growth, maturity, decline (a development stage is sometimes shown first)
What happens to sales in the growth stage?	They rise fast, and the first profits appear
Typical marketing in the maturity stage	Defend market share and profits — competitive pricing, reminder promotion, extension strategies
Five extension strategies	More advertising, a price reduction, adding new features, exploring new markets, new packaging
Why hold a product portfolio?	A product in decline is balanced by one in growth, spreading risk
Cost-plus pricing	Add a fixed % mark-up to the unit cost — always covers costs, but ignores market conditions
Penetration pricing	A low price to enter the market — wins rivals' customers, but they may leave when the price rises
Price skimming	A high launch price, lowered later; suits a genuinely new or advanced product
Psychological pricing	Pricing at $199.99 rather than $200 to look like a good deal
Elastic demand: effect of a price rise on revenue	Total revenue falls
Inelastic demand: effect of a price rise on revenue	Total revenue rises
Four features of inelastic demand	Few substitutes, essential product, strong brand, small share of income
400 bottles at $20 — how many at $18 to match revenue?	8000 ÷ 18 = 444.4, so 445 whole bottles
Does matching revenue mean matching profit?	No — extra units carry extra variable cost and the promotion itself costs money
Five sales promotion methods	BOGOF, coupons, free gifts, competitions, loyalty cards
Purpose of sales promotion vs advertising	Promotion gives a short-term volume boost; advertising informs or persuades over the longer term
How do you judge whether a campaign was cost-effective?	Compare the extra contribution generated with the cost of the campaign
Four distribution channels	Direct, via a retailer, via a wholesaler, via an agent
Advantages of e-commerce as a channel	National or global reach, low overheads, 24-hour selling
Drawbacks of e-commerce as a channel	Delivery costs, returns, and no personal contact
Four criteria for choosing a channel	The product, the market, the cost, the degree of control wanted
What makes a marketing mix coherent?	All four elements support each other and the target segment
Five reasons to enter a foreign market	Saturated home market, larger customer base, spreading risk, lower costs, extending the product life cycle
Six risks of entering a foreign market	Different tastes, language and culture, legal differences, distribution, exchange rates, tariffs and quotas
Five ways to overcome problems entering a foreign market	Local contacts, overseas assembly or sales units, licensing or franchising, a joint venture, merging with or taking over a local firm
Should a successful domestic mix be copied abroad?	No — it usually needs adapting to the new market
Definition of marketing	The management process of anticipating, understanding and satisfying customer needs at a profit
Market-oriented vs product-oriented business	Market-oriented: designs products from market research into customer wants; product-oriented: focuses on products and processes without research
Definition of a target market	The group of consumers a business designs its products and marketing to appeal to
Definition of a USP	A feature or benefit that differentiates a product from its competitors
Difference between invention and innovation	Invention discovers something new; innovation turns it into a product customers will buy
PED formula	% change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price
PED between 0 and 1 vs above 1	0–1: inelastic (essentials such as petrol); above 1: elastic (luxuries)
Loss leader pricing	Selling below cost to draw customers in to buy other, profitable items
Above-the-line vs below-the-line promotion	Above: mass media (TV, radio, newspapers); below: no mass media, within the firm's control (sales promotion, sponsorship)
Informative vs persuasive advertising	Informative gives facts (timetables, menus); persuasive aims to make consumers buy this product rather than a rival's
What does SWOT stand for?	Strengths and weaknesses (internal), opportunities and threats (external)
What is a wholesaler?	An intermediary that buys and stores in bulk from producers and sells small quantities to retailers
