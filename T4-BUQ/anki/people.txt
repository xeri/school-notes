#deck:Business::2 People in business
Definition of motivation	The willingness to work towards the business's objectives
Five benefits of a motivated workforce	Higher productivity, better quality, lower absenteeism, lower labour turnover, more willingness to accept change
Taylor's view of motivation	People are motivated primarily by money — use piece rate and simplified tasks
Criticism of Taylor	Ignores job satisfaction and teamwork, and encourages speed over quality
Maslow's five levels, bottom to top	Physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation
How does a business meet safety needs?	Job security, safe conditions, a contract
How does a business meet esteem needs?	Recognition, status, job titles, praise
Criticism of Maslow	People do not all follow the same order, and the levels are hard to measure
Herzberg's hygiene factors	Pay, conditions, supervision, job security — their absence causes dissatisfaction but they do not motivate
Herzberg's motivators	Achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself, advancement
Herzberg's practical conclusion	Improving pay removes a grievance; enriching the job creates motivation
How does piece rate work, and what is the risk?	Paid per unit produced — encourages speed but quality may fall without monitoring
How does commission work?	Payment as a percentage of sales value
Risk of profit sharing	Individual effort is diluted across the whole workforce
Four examples of fringe benefits	Company car, staff discounts, health cover, pension
Definition of job rotation	Moving between tasks of similar difficulty to reduce boredom
Definition of job enlargement	Adding more tasks at the same level
Definition of job enrichment	Adding more challenging tasks and genuine responsibility, with training
Which non-financial motivator does Herzberg recommend?	Job enrichment
Two costs of training	Time away from production and the expense; the trained worker may also leave
Definition of chain of command	The route down which instructions pass
Definition of span of control	The number of subordinates a manager directly supervises
Definition of delegation	Passing authority for a task downwards
Who keeps accountability after delegation?	The manager — accountability cannot be delegated
Definition of de-layering	Removing a level of management
Advantages of a tall structure	Close supervision and clear promotion steps
Disadvantages of a tall structure	Slow communication and high management cost
Advantages of a flat structure	Fast communication and cheaper management
Disadvantages of a flat structure	Thinner supervision and fewer promotion opportunities
When does a wide span of control work?	With experienced capable staff doing similar low-risk work
Five functions of management	Planning, organising, coordinating, commanding, controlling
When does autocratic leadership suit?	Emergencies, inexperienced staff, safety-critical work
When does democratic leadership suit?	Skilled experienced teams, and decisions needing buy-in
When does laissez-faire leadership suit?	Creative, highly expert professionals
Three factors determining a suitable leadership style	Urgency, the experience of the staff, and the level of risk
Five elements of the communication process	Sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback
Main weakness of one-way communication	No feedback, so no confirmation of understanding
What is the grapevine?	Informal communication — fast but unreliable and prone to rumour
Remedy for a message distorted by too long a chain of command	De-layer, or communicate directly
Remedy for no feedback	Ask the receiver to confirm or repeat back the instruction
Which channel suits detailed technical information?	Written, so it can be referred back to
Which channel suits sensitive news?	Face-to-face, allowing questions and reassurance
Definition of a trade union	An organisation of employees that represents members collectively
Definition of collective bargaining	Negotiation between a union and an employer on behalf of all members
Four forms of industrial action	Overtime ban, work to rule, go-slow, strike
Who loses from a strike?	Both — the business loses output and revenue, the employees lose pay
Difference between a job description and a person specification	The description states the duties; the specification states the skills and qualities needed
Two advantages of internal recruitment	Cheaper and quicker, and the candidate is already known
Two disadvantages of internal recruitment	A narrower pool, no new ideas, and it leaves another vacancy
Two advantages of external recruitment	A wider pool and new skills and fresh ideas
Purpose of induction training	To introduce a new employee to the premises, procedures, safety rules and colleagues
On-the-job training: advantage and drawback	Cheap and directly relevant; bad habits can pass on and the trainer's output falls
Off-the-job training: advantage and drawback	Broader higher-quality skills; expensive and time away from work
Difference between dismissal and redundancy	Dismissal is for the employee's conduct or performance; redundancy is because the job no longer exists
What makes a dismissal unfair?	No adequate reason, or the correct procedure was not followed
Four areas of employment law	Minimum wage, maximum hours, health and safety, protection from discrimination
