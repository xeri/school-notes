# Business calculations

Every formula examinable in Pre-Q Business Studies. Show the formula, the substitution and the answer with its unit or percentage sign.

## Added value and market share

$$\text{added value} = \text{selling price} - \text{cost of bought-in materials}$$
$$\text{market share} = \frac{\text{firm's sales}}{\text{total market sales}}\times100$$
$$\text{market growth} = \frac{\text{change in market size}}{\text{original market size}}\times100$$

Added value is **not** profit — wages, rent and overheads still have to come out of it.

## Business size

$$\text{market capitalisation} = \text{current share price}\times\text{number of shares issued}$$

Market capitalisation applies only to a public limited company. Profit is **not** a measure of size.

## People

$$\text{labour turnover} = \frac{\text{number of employees leaving in a year}}{\text{number of people employed}}\times100$$

## Productivity

$$\text{labour productivity} = \frac{\text{total output in a period}}{\text{number of employees}}$$
$$\text{revenue productivity of labour} = \frac{\text{total revenue}}{\text{number of employees}}$$
$$\text{unit labour cost} = \frac{\text{total labour cost}}{\text{units produced}}$$

## Costs

$$\text{total cost} = \text{fixed costs} + (\text{variable cost per unit}\times\text{output})$$
$$\text{average (unit) cost} = \frac{\text{total cost}}{\text{output}}$$
$$\text{total revenue} = \text{price}\times\text{quantity sold}$$
$$\text{profit} = \text{total revenue} - \text{total cost} \qquad \text{profit per unit} = \text{price} - \text{average cost}$$

## Break-even

$$\text{contribution per unit} = \text{price} - \text{variable cost per unit}$$
$$\text{break-even output} = \frac{\text{fixed costs}}{\text{contribution per unit}}$$
$$\text{profit} = (\text{output}\times\text{contribution per unit}) - \text{fixed costs}$$
$$\text{margin of safety} = \text{actual output} - \text{break-even output}$$
$$\text{output needed for a target profit} = \frac{\text{fixed costs} + \text{target profit}}{\text{contribution per unit}}$$

*Example.* $F = \$4800$, $p = \$20$, $v = \$12$ → contribution $\$8$, break-even 600 units. At 750 units, margin of safety 150 units and profit $\$1200$.

*Example.* $F = \$5000$, $p = \$8$, $v = \$3$ → contribution $\$5$, break-even $5000/5 = 1000$ units. At 2000 units, total cost $= 5000 + 2000(3) = \$11\,000$, so average cost $= 11\,000/2000 = \$5.50$.

## Cash flow

$$\text{net cash flow} = \text{total receipts} - \text{total payments}$$
$$\text{closing balance} = \text{opening balance} + \text{net cash flow}$$

This month's closing balance becomes next month's opening balance.

## Income statement

$$\text{cost of sales} = \text{opening inventory} + \text{purchases} - \text{closing inventory}$$
$$\text{gross profit} = \text{revenue} - \text{cost of sales}$$
$$\text{operating profit} = \text{gross profit} - \text{operating expenses}$$
$$\text{profit before tax} = \text{operating profit} - \text{interest}$$
$$\text{profit for the year} = \text{profit before tax} - \text{tax}$$
$$\text{retained profit} = \text{profit for the year} - \text{dividends}$$

## Statement of financial position

$$\text{assets} = \text{liabilities} + \text{equity}$$
$$\text{working capital} = \text{current assets} - \text{current liabilities}$$
$$\text{net assets} = \text{total assets} - \text{total liabilities}$$
$$\text{capital employed} = \text{equity} + \text{non-current liabilities}$$

The school slides also show working capital as current assets compared with current liabilities, written as a ratio such as 2:1 — the same calculation as the current ratio below. Read which form the question wants.

## Profitability ratios

$$\text{gross profit margin} = \frac{\text{gross profit}}{\text{revenue}}\times100$$
$$\text{operating profit margin} = \frac{\text{operating profit}}{\text{revenue}}\times100$$
$$\text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{operating profit}}{\text{capital employed}}\times100$$

**This course uses operating profit as the ROCE numerator.** Do not substitute a different profit figure.

*Example.* Revenue $\$12$m, gross profit $\$9$m, operating profit $\$2$m, capital employed $\$10$m → GPM $= 75\%$, OPM $= 16.7\%$, ROCE $= 20\%$.

## Liquidity ratios

$$\text{current ratio} = \frac{\text{current assets}}{\text{current liabilities}}$$
$$\text{acid-test ratio} = \frac{\text{current assets} - \text{inventory}}{\text{current liabilities}}$$

Both are quoted as a ratio or a multiple (e.g. 1.5 or 1.5 : 1), **not** as a percentage. The school slides treat 1:1 to 1.5:1 as the "ideal" range.

*Example.* Current assets 60,000 (including inventory 30,000), current liabilities 40,000 → current ratio $= 60/40 = 1.5{:}1$; acid test $= (60-30)/40 = 0.75{:}1$.

## Percentage change

$$\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}}\times100$$
$$\text{percentage of a total} = \frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}\times100$$

## Pricing

$$\text{cost-plus price} = \text{unit cost}\times(1+\text{mark-up})$$
$$\text{mark-up on cost} = \frac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost}}\times100 \qquad \text{margin on price} = \frac{\text{profit}}{\text{selling price}}\times100$$

Mark-up and margin are different — check which the question asks for.

$$\text{price elasticity of demand} = \frac{\%\ \text{change in quantity demanded}}{\%\ \text{change in price}}$$

Ignoring the minus sign: between 0 and 1 is **inelastic** (a price rise increases revenue); greater than 1 is **elastic** (a price rise reduces revenue).

$$\text{sales needed to match old revenue after a price cut} = \frac{\text{old revenue}}{\text{new price}}$$

Round **up** to a whole unit, and remember that matching revenue is not matching profit.

## Interest and social cost

$$\text{interest for a year} = \text{amount borrowed}\times\text{interest rate}$$
$$\text{social cost} = \text{private cost} + \text{external cost} \qquad \text{social benefit} = \text{private benefit} + \text{external benefit}$$

## Exchange rates

To convert **into** a foreign currency, multiply by the rate quoted as "1 domestic = $x$ foreign". To convert **back**, divide.

$$\text{revenue in domestic currency} = \frac{\text{foreign price}}{\text{units of foreign currency per domestic unit}}$$

## Checks before you write the answer

1. Are all figures for the **same time period**?
2. Have you distinguished **totals** from **per-unit** values?
3. Is the unit correct — dollars, units, a percentage, or a ratio?
4. Does the sign make sense — is a loss shown in brackets or as a negative?
5. Can you verify the answer a second way? (e.g. profit from revenue minus total cost, as well as from contribution minus fixed cost)
