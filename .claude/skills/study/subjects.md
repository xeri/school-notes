# Subjects: order, what counts as right, what works

**Order when no subject is named:** Geography, Business, Economics, English, Maths, then Physics. Weigh it against how close each exam is. English's memory part (quotes, themes, the film's sequences) gives fast wins like Business, so it can come early; its writing part is slow. Maths often deserves a higher place than its spot here: the methods below suit how the student works. Offer Physics only when its exam is within about 3 days or is the next one. The student can always override.

Grade what they meant. Ask for exact wording only where the exam marks it: quotes, key terms, units, significant figures. Each subject's own traps are in the session brief; `topic(<subject>)` gives the exam format.

A sweep fix is the word or two that makes a false statement true: the right curve or direction (Economics), the right place or figure (Geography), the right word, speaker or act (English), the right number, sign or unit (Maths, Physics).

## Geography (GEQ)

Breadth pays most here. Marks need a concept and a case study together, so the core skill is pairing them.
- **Right means:** a process in the right order with the right terms; a named place with a specific fact; a labelled diagram.
- **Works well:** "which case shows X?" and "what does case Y show?"; sweeps that swap places and figures in case details (`GEQ/CASE-STUDIES`); put a process in order; sketch it; link two (`GEQ/overlaps`).
- **Errors to plant:** the wrong process name (hydraulic action vs abrasion); the wrong place or figure; steps out of order; HIC/LIC mixed up; "all LICs…".
- **Plan it:** concept, case, judgement.
- **Exam:** 3 hours, sections A–D, 10-mark essays in D.

## Business (BUQ)

The fastest wins, with a trap: definitions sweep quickly and feel like progress, but the marks come from applying ideas to the business in the question.
- **Right means:** a point applied to this business (a sentence that fits any business doesn't count), developed into a consequence, with a judgement that gives a reason.
- **Works well:** once a definition is warm, go straight to "apply it to this business" in a line or two (`BUQ/case-study`); which calculation; judge two answers. Never let it become a definitions drill.
- **Errors to plant:** a generic point; gross/net or profit/cash mixed up; a wrong break-even or ratio formula; an advantage given as a disadvantage; an undeveloped point.
- **Exam:** 3 hours, 120 marks, no choice.

## Economics (ECQ)

Hard ideas are where these methods work best. It overlaps heavily with Business: an idea learned in one is half-learned in the other, so link them.
- **Right means:** a full chain ("because … which leads to … so …"); the right curve shifting the right way; movement and shift kept apart; a judgement that says what it depends on.
- **Works well:** chains, what-ifs and the gap loop; sweeps and quick multiple choice (direct practice for the 30-question MCQ paper; `ECQ/mcq-practice` is a timed one and lists the traps); "which way does it shift?" sweeps and quick sketch-and-label (check the "Marks are lost by" lines in `ECQ/DIAGRAMS`).
- **Errors to plant:** the wrong curve shifting; movement vs shift; elastic vs inelastic; cause and effect swapped; a missing link; a one-sided judgement.
- **Exam:** two papers, 110 marks: Paper 1 is 30 MCQ in 45 minutes, then Paper 2 (2h15) is a compulsory 20-mark data response and 3 of 6 structured questions (20 each).

## English (ENQ)

Two subjects in one: the memory part (themes, quotes, the point each makes) gives fast wins; the writing part is slow.
- **Right means:** the exact quote words; the speaker and the act and scene (or the film moment); the technique and its effect; a point that answers the question. Close-but-wrong quote wording is a miss.
- **Works well:** chunks, not whole essays (whole essays break when the question comes at a new angle): a theme, a few quotes for it, the point each makes. Then "which chunks, in what order?" for a question. Quote sweeps with one word changed keep the wording exact.
- **Errors to plant:** one word changed in a quote; the wrong speaker; the wrong act; the wrong technique; a wrong context claim.
- **Exam:** Papers 1, 3 and 4, 25 marks each (unseen, *Othello*, *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy*).

## Maths (MAQ)

The student makes their own methods. That's a strength: it means understanding, not recall.
- **Right means:** a valid method (check it's valid, not that it's the textbook one) and the right answer; 3 s.f. unless told otherwise, angles to 1 d.p., correct signs. A right answer at the wrong accuracy is shaky.
- **Works well:** breadth is recognising problem types: "here are 5 questions, what's your first step for each?" Solve fully only the ones where they don't know where to start. Derive formulas rather than memorise; memorise only what they can't derive and isn't given in the exam. `MAQ/exam-errors` is the trap list.
- **Errors to plant:** sign slips; wrong rounding; a dropped ±; a wrong rearrangement; completing the square with a ≠ 1; a missing "+C".
- **Exam:** one 3-hour paper, 180 marks.

## Physics (PHQ)

Keep it light. Recognition and formulas (level 3 at most) unless they push for more.
- **Right means:** the equation from memory, substitution with units, the answer with a unit and sensible significant figures. No formula sheet.
- **Works well:** sweeps (40 MCQ in the exam); "which formula / which principle?" (`PHQ/formula-chooser`), like Maths. Units rebuild a half-remembered formula and catch a wrong one: use them. Space science: one quick sweep to confirm, then leave it.
- **Errors to plant:** the wrong unit or prefix; a wrong rearrangement; scalar/vector mixed up; series/parallel rules swapped; mass/weight mixed up.
- **Exam:** 40 MCQ, then 10 sections of 15 marks.

## Error types (all subjects)

- `slip`: careless, but they know it.
- `fact`: a missing or wrong fact.
- `mix-up`: two ideas confused.
- `link`: a step missing from the reasoning.
