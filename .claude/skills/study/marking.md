# Marking rules by subject

Use these when setting or marking a quiz, paper or blurt. The full versions live in each subject's notes: call `topic(<subject>)` for the exam format and mark rules, and `read` the pages named below. Mark like the examiner: credit what is written, not what was meant.

## Mathematics (MAQ)
- **Format:** one 3-hour paper, 180 marks. Questions set with marks; about 1 minute per mark.
- **Marks:** M (method), A (accurate answer, depends on M), S (statement), C (intermediate calculation). Show where each mark is won or lost.
- **Accuracy:** 3 significant figures unless told otherwise, angles to 1 decimal place, calculator π. A correct answer given to the wrong accuracy loses the A mark.
- **Before marking**, `read("MAQ/exam-errors")` for the rounding, sign, unit and statement traps; name the trap when a mark is lost.
- Error types: `method`, `accuracy`, `sign`, `units`, `statement`, `misread`.

## Physics (PHQ)
- **Format:** Paper 1 is 40 MCQ (1 hour). Paper 2 is 10 sections × 15 marks (2 hours). No formula sheet.
- **Every calculation:** equation in symbols → substitution with units → answer with unit and sensible significant figures. Each step can carry a mark; a missing unit loses the final mark.
- Ask for the equation from memory before the numbers (see `PHQ/formula-chooser`).
- Error types: `equation`, `rearranging`, `units`, `sig figs`, `concept`.

## Economics (ECQ)
- **Format:** 3 hours, 100 marks: 20 MCQ, one data-response question, three of six 20-mark structured questions with parts (a) 2, (b) 4, (c) 6, (d) 8.
- **(a)/(b):** precise definitions and points; a definition needs the key words.
- **(c) analyse:** a cause-and-effect chain ("because… which leads to… so…"), usually with a labelled diagram. Diagram marks: axes, curves, equilibria, shift arrows (see the "Marks are lost by" lines in `ECQ/DIAGRAMS`).
- **(d) discuss:** both sides, then a conclusion that says what it **depends on**. One-sided answers cap at about half.
- **Data response:** quote figures with years; trend → highest/lowest → exception.
- Error types: `definition`, `chain`, `diagram`, `evaluation`, `data`, `calculation`.

## Business (BUQ)
- **Format:** 3 hours, 120 marks, no choice: three short-answer and three long-answer 20-mark questions (2, 4, 6, 8 and 12-mark parts).
- **Skills annotated:** [k] knowledge, [app] application to *this* business, [an] analysis chain, [ev] justified judgement. About a quarter of all marks are application: a sentence that could be pasted into an answer about another business earns no [app].
- **By marks:** 2 = point + development; 3 = [k] + [app] + [an]; 6 explain = 2 × (point, case fact, consequence); 6 judge = both sides + decision; 8 = two developed points each using **two** case facts; 12 = discuss every option, recommend one, say why the others lose (levels).
- **Calculations:** formula, substitution, answer with unit; own-figure rule applies.
- Error types: `no application`, `undeveloped`, `no judgement`, `definition`, `calculation`.

## Geography (GEQ)
- **Format:** 3 hours: A Theme 1 (50), B Theme 2 (25), C Theme 3 (25), D three 10-mark essays (30).
- **Short answers:** one mark per valid point; "explain" needs the process, not just the outcome. Processes in sequence (three steps) with the correct terms.
- **Named examples** earn marks: name the place and give a specific fact.
- **Diagrams:** labelled and annotated diagrams can earn marks; check every label.
- **Essays:** levels out of 10; Levels 3–4 need developed place-specific examples and visible evaluation (see `GEQ/exam-technique`).
- Error types: `process`, `example`, `terminology`, `diagram`, `evaluation`.

## English (ENQ)
- **Format:** Papers 1, 3 and 4, 25 marks each, about an hour each. Paper 1 unseen commentary (prose or poem, with a biblical or mythical allusion); Paper 3 *Othello*, closed book (general or passage question); Paper 4 *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy* (film).
- **Quote recall:** exact words, then act.scene (or film moment), speaker, technique and effect. Close-but-wrong wording is a miss.
- **Plans (10 minutes):** a thesis that answers the exact question, three or four points each with evidence and analysis, a link to context or the director's choices.
- **Every essay:** author or director named, present tense, short embedded quotations, more analysis than description.
- Error types: `quote`, `placement`, `technique`, `thesis`, `context`.

## Logging lost marks

One error per lost mark: `what` (the mistake, in the student's terms), `fix` (the correct version, short enough to recall), `type` (from the lists above), `source` (the section id that teaches it). The fix is what the drill will ask for later, so make it specific.
