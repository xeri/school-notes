# How to run each task

Keep every task short and plain, and follow the Wording rules in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md). Build it from the brief's gist and statements, or `read(id)` for level 3+. Log one review per idea it touched, at the task's level.

## Making subtle changes

Used in sweeps, fix-it passages and the prep bank. A good change is believable: the mistake a real student makes, not a silly one.
- **Direction:** flip it (rises ↔ falls, left ↔ right, increase ↔ decrease).
- **Cause and effect:** swap them.
- **Close terms:** swap them (demand ↔ quantity demanded, elastic ↔ inelastic, weathering ↔ erosion, speed ↔ velocity, gross ↔ net).
- **Numbers, units and signs:** the wrong number, unit, sign or rounding.
- **Conditions:** drop one ("ceteris paribus", "if PED > 1", "at constant temperature").
- **"Always":** overgeneralise it ("always", "only", "never").
- **People and places:** the wrong speaker, act or scene (English), or the wrong place or figure in a case study (Geography, Business).
- **Missing link:** remove one step from a chain.

Use the brief's **traps** and **their past mistakes** first. One change per statement in a sweep; 3–5 changes in a level-2 passage. The changed statement keeps the length and tone of a true one, so only the content gives it away.

## Level 1: the sweep

[SKILL.md](SKILL.md) has the rules (5 per message, a fix after each F, the grading table). Variants, now and then, to stop it going stale:
- **Two truths and a lie:** three statements on one idea; they name the lie and fix it.
- **Multiple choice:** four options, the distractors from close mix-ups. More in Economics and Physics, which have MCQ papers.

## Level 2: Fix it

- **Spot the errors:** a 60–120 word passage in the notes' style with 3–5 planted errors, one from their past mistakes if there are any.
  - Say how many errors at first; later say "some".
  - They quote each wrong bit and write the fix.
  - Grade per error, as a review on that error's idea: found and fixed ok, found but not fixed shaky, missed miss.
- **Put it in order:** 5–7 jumbled steps, events or chain links. One step may be wrong or missing; they fix it too.
- **Sort it:** 6–10 items into 2–3 groups (types of cost, push and pull factors, quotes by theme). One item may belong to no group.
- **Wrong step (Maths, Physics):** a worked solution with one wrong step. They find it, say why, and redo it.

## Level 3: Build it

- **Fill the gap:** a chain with 1–2 missing links, or a half-done solution to finish.
- **Which tool:** a short situation. They name the formula, diagram, case study or quote that fits, and give the first step only.
- **Why, in one line:** after a right answer, ask why it is true. They give the mechanism ("because…"), not a restatement.
- **Link two:** two ideas, often from different topics or a paired subject. They write the chain between them in 2–4 steps.
- **Sketch it:** they draw the diagram on paper, then type the labels and arrows (or paste a photo). Check every label against the notes.
- **Judge two:** two short answers to the same prompt, one with subtle flaws. They say which is better and fix the weaker one.
- **Reshape:** turn an idea into a cause → effect chain or a table, or explain it to a 12-year-old in 3 sentences, then put the exam words back in.

## Level 4: Explain it

- **What if (default):** change one thing in a known example. They predict what happens and why, in a line.
- **Teach me (rare):** you play a confused student with 1–2 misconceptions from traps or their past mistakes. Ask naive follow-ups; only "get it" when their explanation clears the misconception. 3–5 messages.
- **Argue (rare):** you hold a confident wrong or one-sided view. They take it apart; push back once; concede when they give the chain and what it depends on.

## Level 5: From memory

- **Compress (default):** the topic in 2 sentences, then 1, then 3 words. At each step, say what important thing fell out.
- **Blurt (rare):** everything they know about a topic. Compare with the ideas: ✓ what they got, then what's missing (most important first), then what's wrong. Log every idea it covered, then 1–2 items on the biggest gap.

## Plan it (exam questions)

Only when the brief allows it. Give a real exam-style question; they write a 3–5 line skeleton. Mark it against [subjects.md](subjects.md), then one line on the biggest gap.
- **Economics:** the chain (because → which leads to → so), the diagram, and a judgement that says what it depends on.
- **Geography:** the concept, the named case with one specific fact, and a judgement.
- **English:** which chunks (theme, quote, the point it makes), in what order, answering the question asked.
- **Business:** the point, applied to this business, developed into a consequence, and a judgement with a reason.
- **Maths, Physics:** the first step and the method for each part, no working.

A full written answer only if they ask. Log one review per idea it used, at level 4, with `mode="plan it"`.

## Hints

The smallest nudge comes first: the first word, the category ("it's a demand-side factor"), or a contrast ("not supply, the other curve"). An item they needed a nudge on is graded **shaky**.
